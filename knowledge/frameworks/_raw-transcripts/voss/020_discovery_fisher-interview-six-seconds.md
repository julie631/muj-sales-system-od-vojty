# Voss x Jefferson Fisher — Earnest Questions + Genuine Storytelling

## Context
Long-form interview (~16 min) between Voss and trial lawyer Jefferson Fisher. Topic: roots of Fisher's communication style + how lawyers read jurors. Less tactical than other videos; more about craft and mindset.

## Earnest Questions vs Accusatory Questions [01:01]
- Voss notes Fisher asks "earnest questions" — questions where the answer reveals the truth.
- Example from his book: instead of telling sales leaders "your boss doesn't want everyone to quit," ASK them "Do you think your boss wants everyone to leave the company?"
- "They have to answer the question. The answer reveals the truth to them."
- This = no-oriented question pattern + Socratic method.

## Fisher's Background [02:08]
- 5th-generation attorney. Father, cousins, uncles. Great-grandfather was a federal judge.
- Father would pick him up from school, finish depositions with Jefferson sitting in the corner.
- Watching closing arguments in junior high.
- "No dog to fight, no stake in the outcome, no ego involved. Just paying attention to what works."
- "You get to see real quickly what persuades jurors, judges, anybody — what works, what grabs their attention, what turns them off."

## Father as Communication Teacher [04:01]
- Never said "this is what I want for you."
- Father was a great LISTENER. Not just listening — also anticipating where Jefferson was going.
- Taught through QUESTIONS (Socratic method).
- "Questions are designed to induce thinking first and get an answer second."

## Phrasing Words to Change Impact [06:09]
- Same message, different phrasing → huge impact difference.
- "If you're using game to manipulate — bad. But if 'game' is to be interested and actually fascinating — that's not bad."

## The 6-Second Rule [07:10]
- "Every six seconds counts."
- Juror's eyes shift every 6 seconds — they watch what OPPOSING counsel is doing while listening to whoever is talking.
- Example: if you're at counsel table and someone says something you disagree with, jurors WATCH your non-verbal reaction. They feed on it.
- "If our nonverbals show we don't believe in their message... they pick up on that."

## Reading Jurors During Closing [09:10]
- After a week-long trial, you can tell who genuinely listens, who's out, who's already decided.
- "Just with people, they have a 6th sense of telling who's being genuine."
- Witness on stand acting for sympathy → jury hates it.
- Witness being cold/callous/defensive → jury hates it.
- Expert witness who bucks back at a question → "they look invested" → hurts the OTHER side's case.
- "Ego gets involved. But the jury picks up on all of it. And judges do too."

## Court Reporters Are Best Data Source [11:13]
- Court reporters/bailiffs/judges talk between breaks.
- They've watched thousands of trials. They know BS from truth.
- "Treat your court reporters: they're up here, I'm down here. They rule the land. They pick on everything you don't."

## Genuine Wins Over Performative [10:11]
- "They just have a 6th sense of telling who's being genuine."
- Over-dramatic = bad. Over-defensive = bad.
- "Human perception is incredibly powerful, especially in the courtroom."

## The Storytelling Connection [07:37]
- All the men + women in Fisher's family are great storytellers.
- He watched big dramatic closing arguments with jury packed in.
- "You can see really the difference on what would capture somebody's attention and what would turn it off."

## Coldplay Closing Touch [15:16]
- Fisher mentions taking his girlfriend to Coldplay.
- Voss: "Coldplay continues to fill stadiums while other artists are canceling tours. They are great. They make you feel they're genuinely happy you're there. That's a great community experience."
- (Genuineness > performance, again.)

## CZ adaptation notes
- "Questions induce thinking first" → USE more questions instead of statements in sales calls. NE "máte málo zakázek" → ALE "Co byste s tím udělali, kdyby vám měsíčně chyběly 3 zakázky?"
- 6-second rule → on Zoom/Meet calls, watch prospect's micro-expressions every 6 sec. They DO judge based on your reactions.
- Genuine > performative → kritické pro hlasovky. Your voice messages must sound natural, not "rep doing outreach." Drop the performative tone.
- Earnest questions = Socratic method = no-oriented questions in another wrapper.
