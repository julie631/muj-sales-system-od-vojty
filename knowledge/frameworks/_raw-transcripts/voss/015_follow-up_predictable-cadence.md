# #1 Trust-Building Trick — Predictable Cadence

## Context
Voss's most-viewed video among the top 30 (70k views). Topic: how to build trust with ANY client. The trick: predictable cadence of communication, even when there's nothing to report.

## The Core Problem [00:00]
- Most people call clients only when they have good news or bad news.
- Clients then never know when they'll hear from you.
- They get anxious → Google → call you at odd hours / weekends → ask "why aren't you doing X?"
- "In their anxiety to get to the transaction, if they feel they're being held in the dark, they're going to go out and start searching stuff."

## The Hostage Negotiation Example [01:01]
- Voss working overseas kidnappings. Families hated FBI/Voss team.
- State Dept rep Ted was loved by families — but Ted wasn't actually doing anything to save anyone.
- Voss: "How? Ted gets the love. We do the work."
- Ted explains: "They never wonder when they're going to hear from me. I have scheduled calls. I always tell them in advance: 'You'll hear from me in exactly a day and a half.'"
- "If I got nothing to tell them, I call them on the phone and say 'I got nothing to tell you.' And they love it."

## The Australian Real Estate Agent [02:04]
- Female agent in Australia — top of conference because of her referral pipeline.
- Her secret: clients always knew WHEN they'd hear from her (not just good/bad news).
- Same principle.

## Why Clients Love Predictability [03:04]
- "If they feel they're being held in the dark, they're going to go out and they're going to start searching stuff... talking to their brother-in-law... 'your agent's not doing X.'"
- If they know they'll hear from you Monday at 10am, they're LESS likely to call you 10pm Sunday.
- Voss's full practice: "Every call I'd say 'You're going to hear from me in an hour.' An hour later I got back on the phone, even if I had nothing new, just to confirm: 'There's nothing new.'"

## The Mary Jones Story [04:05]
- Hostage case in Philippines. Family rep Mary Jones — small, pixie haircut.
- Everyone scared of her. Voss meets her — "How is everyone scared of this girl?"
- His SWAT colleague avoided calling her because "I'm tired of asking her how she's doing and her biting my head off."
- Voss: "First, that's not what I told you to do. Second — how the hell do you THINK she's doing?"
- **"Don't ask somebody how they are when you know how they are. Demonstrate an understanding. You make yourself look stupid."**
- From Mary's perspective: "How do you think I am? My sister's being held by Islamic terrorists. They're known for horrible things."

## The Two Rules [05:05]
1. **Don't ask how they are when you know how they are.** Demonstrate understanding.
2. **Don't let them wonder when they'll hear from you.** Even if "nothing new," call at scheduled time.

## The Principle [06:07]
- "You put predictability back in a process. You build predictability in an unpredictable process."
- "You don't know when there's going to be good news. You don't know when there's going to be bad news. But you can be eminently predictable in the way that you interact with your clients, and they will love it."

## CZ adaptation notes
- Your clients: implement weekly/biweekly scheduled "nothing new" check-ins. Even short Slack/email: "Tento týden žádné větší změny. Příští čtvrtek 14:00 vám pošlu standardní reporting."
- "Demonstrate understanding instead of asking" CZ: NOT "Jak se ti vede?" → instead "Vím, že tohle období bývá u účetních hektické, snad ten reporting přijde ve správnou chvíli."
- For active sales pipeline: schedule next-touch-time at end of every conversation. Even FU at "nic nového" cadence reduces anxiety + builds trust → higher close rate.
- This is critical for the gap between sales close → onboarding → first results. Predictable cadence kills client buyer's-remorse.
