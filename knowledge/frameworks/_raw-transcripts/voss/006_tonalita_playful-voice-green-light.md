# Playful Voice + Green-Light Time — How to Get an 80% Discount

## Context
Voss tells a first-person story about a $3,300 medical bill. He goes in hoping for 10% discount, walks out paying $700 total — an 80% discount — using tactical empathy + tone + timing.

## The Story [00:00 – 02:01]
- 10:30am, sitting at desk to pay $3,300 medical bill.
- "I'm feeling kind of happy, in a playful mood."
- Hopes for 10% discount (~$330 = $660/hr value for the call).
- Phone tree. Reaches debt collections. Young lady picks up: "Hello."
- Voss (playful tone, self-effacing): **"I owe you money."**
- She: "You got an identification number?" Looks it up: "Oh yes, you do." Starting to play along.
- Voss: "Look, I need to pay you guys before you come to my house and drag me out of my house." She laughs: "Yeah, we will do that."
- "If I pay the whole thing now, what kind of a discount can I get?"
- She: "Let me check." Confers in background. He hears "$700."
- Voss: confirms — "$700 off?" She: **"No, we'll settle the whole thing for $700."**
- 80% discount on the spot.

## Why It Worked

### 1. Playful Mood [03:01]
- "You're 31% smarter if you're in a positive frame of mind."
- Playful tone = depth, quickness, right-word-finding.
- Empathy comes from asking: "What is the voice in their head saying about me?"
- Collections people get constant complaints, attacks, excuses.
- Voss hits her with **exactly what's in her head**: "You owe us money" → reframed playfully as "I owe you money."
- Self-effacing humor: tone of voice signals NOT whining, NOT complaining, NOT attacking.

### 2. Green-Light Time [05:04]
- Law enforcement term: when it's okay for the sniper to pull the trigger.
- For YOU: when it's okay to make your ask.
- Optimal time: **mid-morning, ~10am**.
- "You're at your sharpest. Still got mental gas in the tank. Fully caffeinated. Haven't been worn out."
- The OTHER side is also more likely in their green-light window if in the same time zone.

### 3. Going All The Way There [06:55]
- He brings up the worst-case ("drag me out of my house") and laughs with her about it.
- "I'm not making any excuses, and I've got her in a playful tone of voice also."
- Critical: her tone will carry forward when she pitches the discount to her supervisor. Is she pitching it as "this guy's a jerk" or "this guy's positive"?

### 4. The Underlying Principle [05:04]
- "Never be mean to someone who can hurt you by doing nothing."
- Flip side: "If they like you, they could probably do a lot for you."

## The Framework
1. **Audit your mood** — am I playful? If not, don't make the ask yet.
2. **Check timing** — am I in green-light? Is the other side likely in green-light too?
3. **Imagine empathy** — what's the voice in their head saying about me / my ask?
4. **Open with what they're already thinking** — beat them to the negative.
5. **Use self-effacing humor** to remove yourself as a threat.
6. **Make your ask. Shut up. Let them surprise you.**

## CZ adaptation notes
- Self-effacing humor CZ: "Volám vám, abych se přiznala k vině" (when calling about payment issue) — playful confession tone.
- Green-light time CZ: schedule discovery calls 9:30-11:00, not after lunch. Pre-load mood (music, energy).
- Asking for discount on supplier (e.g. software you pay monthly): use playful confession + go all the way there: "Dřív nebo později vám stejně přestanu platit za pomalý systém, tak co kdybyste mi dali zlevu, ať vás můžu doporučovat?"
- Critical mindset for cold outreach (calls, voice messages): TONE = playful, NOT desperate. The 31% sharpness applies on your side AND landing on prospect.
