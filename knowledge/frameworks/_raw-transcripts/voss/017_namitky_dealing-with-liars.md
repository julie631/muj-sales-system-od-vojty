# How to Deal with Liars (Without Burning the Deal)

## Context
Tight 5-min YouTube tactical video. Voss's framework for negotiating with someone who's clearly lying — without exploding the relationship.

## The Counterintuitive Insight [00:55]
- "Most people don't lie because they're evil. They lie because they're scared."
- Scared of losing leverage, looking weak, you saying no if they told the truth.
- "Fear of loss is the single biggest driver of human decision making."
- **"The lie isn't about you. It's about the story they're telling themselves about what they stand to lose."**

## What Most Negotiators Do Wrong [01:25]
- Jump to confrontation: "You're lying."
- Play detective: yes/no interrogation.
- Give ultimatums: "If I find out you lied, the deal's dead."
- **Every one of those drives the liar deeper into bunker mentality.**
- "When you trigger defensiveness, you don't get honesty. You get resistance."

## The Right Approach: Tactical Empathy
"You don't call out the lie directly. You call out the fear underneath the lie."

### Tool 1: Label the Fear
- "It seems like there's something you're worried about losing here."

### Tool 2: Mirror with Curiosity
- "Not in the budget?" (repeat last 1-3 words, curious tone)

### Tool 3: Accusations Audit
- "You're probably afraid I'll walk if you tell me the truth."

## Three Techniques When You Sense Deception

### A. Calibrated Questions [02:02]
- NOT: "Are you lying to me?"
- INSTEAD: "How am I supposed to make that work if that number isn't accurate?"
- "What happens if that turns out not to be true?"
- These force the liar to think through consequences. Often reveals cracks in the story.

### B. No-Oriented Questions [02:43]
- Liars fear commitment. Give them a safe way out.
- "Would it be ridiculous to say there's more to the story?"
- "Is it a bad idea to walk me through what's really behind that number?"
- "When they can say no, they relax. That's when the truth comes out."

### C. "That's Right" Summary [03:03]
- Summarize what you DO know about their situation.
- "So far, it sounds like you're under pressure from your boss to get this done fast, and the last thing you want is for this to fall apart."
- When they respond "That's right" → you've got buy-in.
- **Critical: Pause AT LEAST 6 SECONDS after your summary. Don't ask "Have I got that right?" or "What did I leave out?" → that's yes-seeking. Just SILENCE.**
- Then pause ANOTHER 6 seconds after the "That's right" — liars don't agree to a summary unless it feels accurate. They may add more.

## The Closing Principle [03:54]
- "You don't fight the lie. You drain the fear that fuels it."
- "The person across the table is never the problem. The unsolved issue is. Let's solve it together."
