# Breaking Bad News — Don't Lose the Client

## Context
Voss + Steve Shull (real estate coach, ex-NFL) on the #1 mistake agents make: delivering bad news poorly. Long-form (16 min) — one of Voss's most actionable videos for client retention.

## Become a Trusted Advisor [00:00]
- "How do you become a trusted advisor? Tell the truth."
- Steve: "Well, of course I tell the truth." Voss: "No, you don't."
- Most agents promise the moon. Real trusted advisor tells the truth about Pitfalls.
- "A lot of houses out there. Some you won't like. I don't want you to buy. There are houses we should look at — but not all of them."

## More Is Not the Answer [01:00]
- "More equals more stress and more unhappiness."
- One coachee went from $50M to $175M production in 4 years. Still panicked.
- "Becoming the favorite is a byproduct of doing all these other things."
- "When you convince someone to do business with you, you're getting them to do something they weren't planning on doing. Now their expectations go through the roof. First time something goes wrong, they're all over you."

## The Trusted Advisor Mindset [03:03]
- Don't try to "make people feel good" — that puts THEIR stress on YOU.
- Make them feel **UNDERSTOOD** instead.
- "There's an actual chemical release — oxytocin — that creates bonding... promotes truth-telling, makes people feel satisfied and less demanding."

## Get In Front of Problems [07:08]
- "Every agent — they don't want to bring up a problem because they think they have to have the solution in hand. So they stick it in the drawer and hope and pray."
- "By the time it comes out, it biases the whole experience."

## The Magic Preface: "I've Got Some Really Bad News" [08:10]
- Voss's bombshell phrase: **"I've got some really bad news."**
- People are like: "I could never say that."
- But: "When you say 'I've got some really bad news,' they go to death. They imagine the WORST possible thing."
- When you then say what it actually is → it's almost a relief.
- They can process.

## Labels That Get In Front [09:11]
- "You're probably thinking..."
- "You're probably feeling..."
- "You're not going to want to hear this."
- "This is going to seem incredibly disappointing."
- **"This is going to break your heart."**
- "From there you can say anything you want."

## Doctor Analogy [10:11]
- Doctor A: "This won't hurt at all." Then jabs you. You're angry — caught off guard, lied to.
- Doctor B: "This is going to hurt." Same shot. You think "that wasn't that bad."
- **Emotional pain centers in brain = same location as physical pain.**
- People are incredibly resilient if you let them brace.

## Last Impression > First Impression [11:13]
- "People get this whole first impression / last impression thing mixed up."
- "They get so caught up that the first impression's got to be really nice. They don't understand how devastating last impressions are."
- "Creep someone so the last impression is actually not as unpleasant as it could have been."
- Person who received bad news but had braced + got light at the end = **relief + love for you because you're a straight shooter**.

## The Email Sandwich Rule [12:14]
- Bad news email structure:
  - **Good news at the beginning** (maybe).
  - **Bad news in the middle.**
  - **Good news at the END.**
- "That's the last thing they remember."
- "Mentally the human brain is so prone to negative feelings. You give them positive at the end → that's really their lasting impression."

## Understood, Not Good [14:15]
- "Real estate agents are always trying to make the other person feel good. Problem with that = you take their stress on yourself."
- "Make them feel UNDERSTOOD. That changes the whole dynamic."
- Then use calibrated questions to guide them: how/what questions to help them think it through.
- "With them thinking it's their idea. That's real skill."

## Put Responsibility Where It Belongs [15:16]
- "Agents are always making the mistake of taking responsibility for things when they can't make a decision."
- The buyer/seller decides. Keep responsibility on them.
- "Be able to deliver in a way they can hear it, process it. Then use other tools."

## CZ adaptation notes
- "I've got some really bad news" CZ: **"Mám pro vás dost špatnou zprávu"** — kritické framing.
- Labels in front of problems CZ:
  - "Pravděpodobně to nebudete chtít slyšet..."
  - "Tohle bude znít hodně překvapivě..."
  - "Asi vám to zlomí srdce..."
- Email sandwich CZ for client comms: good news → bad news → good news (last sentence = positive). Nasaď to jako rule pro každý "něco se rozbilo" email.
- "Understood, not good" CZ → kritické: NEslibovat "vyřešíme to za den" když nevíte. INSTEAD label: "Chápu, že tě to štve. Tohle bys nikdy nečekal."
- Oxytocin reference = scientific anchor for empathy framework. Worth citing.
