# Latent Labels — Label What They're NOT Saying

## Context
Fireside coaching call (Black Swan members-only Q&A). Voss + Don Felman + Nick Paluso debrief a role-play they did earlier with experienced Black Swans who stayed too surface-level despite knowing better.

## Core thesis
Skills used as box-ticking exercises = useless. The whole point of labels, mirrors, calibrated questions is **guided discovery** of black swans — small pieces of information that change the outcome. Pro-level negotiators label **latent dynamics** (what's not said), not just presenting ones (what's said).

## Key Distinctions

### Presenting vs Latent Dynamic [04:31]
- **Presenting dynamic** = actual words coming out of counterpart's mouth.
- **Latent dynamic** = what they DIDN'T say with those words.

### Voss's "Optics" example [05:35]
- Voss used the word "Optics" in a role-play several times: "I'm concerned about the optics if XYZ occurs."
- What he WAS actually conveying: "I'm worried about saving face. I'm worried about being embarrassed."
- "Optics is just the way that I camouflaged it."
- Anchor question when stuck on latent dynamic: **think identity issues.**
  - "How is the direction this conversation is going going to impact their identity?"
  - "How is my ask going to impact their identity?"
  - "How is the bad news I'm going to give them impacting their identity?"

## Surface vs Latent Labels [06:36]
- "Surface labels are, as the kids say these days, mid."
- Latent labels = "identifying things they're not saying" → carries far more weight.
- "The only way that I know you truly get me is when you start to label things that I am not saying myself. That's when you start to look like a mind reader. That's when you spark collaboration."

## Why people stay surface-level [07:37]
- They're afraid to take a shot.
- Afraid to embarrass themselves, afraid of getting it wrong.
- "When you're afraid to take the shot, you've made the conversation about you and not about the other side."
- Amygdala = predisposed to negatives, threats. Fear outweighs gut.

## Intuition is data [03:28]
- When Voss stops a role-play participant and asks "what did you hear?" they can almost always pick up information that wasn't presented verbally.
- Intuition is intact. The problem: they deliberately CHOOSE to ignore it in the hot seat.

## How it ties together
- Skills (labels, mirrors, calibrated questions) ≠ checkbox exercise.
- They are **deliberate tools for guided discovery** of black swans.
- "Negotiations is a guided discovery process where you are deliberately trying to uncover information that's going to help you influence the person."

## CZ adaptation notes
- "It seems like you're worried about how this looks to your boss" → CZ: "Vypadá to, že vás trápí, jak to bude působit směrem k vedení."
- Identity-issue framing for small-business prospects = owners who care about reputation with their team/community (e.g. jazyková škola: "co řeknou lektoři, když to nezavedeme správně").
- Pro level: don't say "máte obavy z ceny" (surface). Say "vypadá to, že chcete před manželkou/parťákem nevypadat, že jste vyhodili peníze" (latent identity).
