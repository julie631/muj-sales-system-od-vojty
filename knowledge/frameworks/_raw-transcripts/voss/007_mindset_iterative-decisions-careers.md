# Iterative Decisions + Cover-and-Move Leadership

## Context
Live audience Q&A with Voss + Jocko Willink (former Navy SEAL). Two main themes covered.

## Thread 1: When to Jump from Day Job to Side Hustle [00:00]

### Jocko's military analogy
- Out on operation, taking fire from the north. Don't immediately say "everyone attack!"
- Instead: "Chris, go to the roof and tell me what you see."
- One AK-47, one guy. Move one building north. Reassess. Still taking fire? Same threat? Move again.
- Eventually: "30 enemy with technical vehicle." Now decision is informed.
- **Iterative steps. Feedback loop. Don't commit blind.**

### Application to side-hustle [01:30]
- Don't quit day job on hope.
- Continue building side hustle while keeping financial security.
- Set milestones: "I can see the trajectory."
- Be honest with yourself about market signal: "I invented this widget — everyone's going to want it." Sometimes the market says no.
- "I'm not making a move based on hope. I'm not making a move based on 'I really love this.' I love surfing and jiu-jitsu — I don't get paid to surf."
- Build in 18-24 months. Judge in 6-9 months whether side hustle has REAL trajectory.
- Family stability > personal happiness in the short term — but exit strategy required (don't stay miserable forever).

## Thread 2: "How Can I Help You Win?" Leadership [06:06]

### Don't think about getting promoted [06:23]
- "If someone is focused on getting promoted, intent has a smell."
- Team smells it: "Chris is just doing this because he's trying to kiss up to Jocko."
- Creates bad, antagonistic relationships.

### Instead — Cover and Move [07:07]
- First law of combat leadership: how can I best support the team?
- To subordinates, peers, superiors: **"How can I help you win? How can I support you? What do you need from me?"**
- Works up/down/across the chain.

### Strong teams are not strong because of skill [08:07]
- "What makes a SEAL platoon strong isn't the number of pull-ups. It's the relationships and the support they give each other."
- Same in any business team.

## CZ adaptation notes
- Main business + side project is exactly this situation. Use iterative milestones, not hope.
- "Don't lie to yourself" → every ambitious projection (X closed clients in 90 days) needs honest reality check. Are signals genuinely there or hopium?
- "Intent has a smell" → critical for cold outreach. If a voice message smells of "I need to close you," prospects feel it. Tone must be care-first.
- "How can I help you win?" → directly translatable to first sales call opening with a business owner: "Co byste potřeboval, abych vám mohla efektivně pomoct?" (instead of "Co pro vás můžu udělat?" which falls under Voss's "don't ask How can I help you" rule).
