# Body

## Current state → Gap → Objective state

[0:33] Every prospect has a **current state** (where they are now) and an **objective state** (where they could be once problems are solved). Between them = **the gap** = the problems you help them find.

**Two emotional drivers** that cause humans to change:
1. **Pain** (current or past)
2. **Fear of future pain**

Together → urgency → buying decision.

## Why "Problems" (plural)

[2:42] Three reasons we use problem-awareness questions:
1. **Real problems** (not surface-level)
2. **Root cause** of the problems
3. **Personal impact** — even Fortune 100 CEOs have emotions

> "If I only help them find one problem, the gap isn't that big. If I help them find 4–5 problems they didn't realize they had, the gap becomes really painful — they feel like they can't do this online, they have to hire someone."

## 6 Generic Problem-Awareness Frames

### Frame 1 — 100% love anchor

> "I mean, do you 100% love the results you've been getting?"

Nobody is 100% in love with anything. Forces honest doubt.

### Frame 2 — "Want to keep doing that if you didn't have to?" (concerned tone)

> "I mean, do you want to have to keep going through that if you… if you didn't have to?"

**Concerned tone is critical.** A curious tone makes it sound rhetorical ("of course not"). Concerned tone seeds: *I'm concerned for you* → raises trust.

### Frame 3 — "What's caused you to feel like that might not be enough?"

> "What's caused you to feel like that might not be enough though?"
> "What's caused you to feel like you might want to look at someone else?"

### Frame 4 — Tie to their tenure / vendor

> "I mean, you've been with XYZ company the last 5 years. I mean, they're fairly decent. I mean, what's caused you to feel like you might want something different?"

### Frame 5 — Playful "doom and gloom"

[8:21] After they've dumped problems:

> "Hold on, hold on. It can't be all doom and gloom over there. What do you like about what they've done?"

**Why playful tone:** dopamine release → calms nervous system → guard down → trust up.

Right after they answer:

> "What would you change if you could though?"

(Quick snap probe — they're already in honest mode.)

### Frame 6 — Reverse the predictable

Predictable questions that prospects see coming:
- "What don't you like about your current vendor?"
- "What are some problems / challenges?"
- "What would you change?"

[9:23] **Replace with pattern-interrupt structure** using their context:

> "So hold on. You had XYZ company put on the old roof 15 years ago. I mean, they're not horrible. I mean, what's caused you to feel like you might want to look at someone different?"

## Per-industry examples

### Roofing
[9:55]
> "So hold on. You had XYZ company put on the old roof 15 years ago. I mean, they're not horrible. I mean, what's caused you to feel like you might want to look at someone different?"

Key word swap: **"fairly decent"** / **"not horrible"** = neutral. Don't bash the competitor ("they're horrible") — loses trust. Don't praise them ("they're great") — reinforces their attachment. Stay neutral, let your tonality plant doubt.

### Real estate (seller)
[13:04]
> "I mean, you've got a very beautiful property here. I mean, what's caused you to feel like you might want to sell it?"

"Might want to" — neutral, doesn't assume. Triggers them to defend why they want to sell → emotional engagement.

### Marketing agency (B2B)
[14:08]
> "So you've been with XYZ agency the last two years. I mean, what's caused you to feel like they might not be able to scale you to $8 million a month?"

→ Plug in THEIR stated outcome ($8M/mo) — don't invent.

### Problem-stacking with "besides"
[15:12] If they've already mentioned a problem (e.g. unqualified leads):

> "Besides the leads not being so qualified — I mean, you've been with them for three years — what's caused you to feel like they might not be able to scale you to $8 million a month?"

→ This unlocks **additional** problems beyond the obvious one.

### Commercial insurance (cold call)
[15:43]
> "Besides the rate hikes they've been forcing you to pay, are you 100% happy with what they've been doing for you?"

Note **"forcing"** — emotional word. Nobody likes being forced. Positions you between the prospect and the "bad evil company."

### Cyber security
[17:47]
> "Besides X, are you 100% in love with the way they set up your fraud stack?"

[18:17] **Concerned tone** seeds doubt: *I might know something they don't.*

### Solar (D2D, in-home, or phone)
[18:49]
> "I mean, do you want to keep being forced to pay the rate hikes *(pause)* if you *(pause)* didn't have to?"

**Verbal pacing** is the secret — slow pauses make the brain internalize.

### Recruiting / staffing
> "I mean, you've been using these job boards like Indeed the last few years. I mean, what's made you feel like they might not be getting you the best people?"

### Auto
> "I mean, your Audi A6 — that's not a horrible car by any means. What's caused you to feel like you might want something newer?"

### Home improvement (shower for senior parent)
[20:54]
> "I mean, you've had the same tub here for 10 years. What's caused you to feel like it might not be safe enough for your mom?"

**Concerned tone** — if they don't act, mom could fall. Genuine concern is appropriate here.

### Financial services
> "So you've been investing with Ameriprise the last several years. They're fairly decent. What's caused you to feel like that plan might not be able to retire you by 63?"

Plug in **their stated retirement age** — don't invent.

### Health insurance
[22:59]
> "When you've gone to the doctor in the past and you've used that policy — did you 100% like how they covered you?"

If they say "yes":
> "I mean, wow, it sounds like the policy you have is 100% perfect. *(2-second pause)*"
> Prospect: "Well, I wouldn't say it's 100%."
> "Well, it's not perfect — hold on, what don't you like?"

## Key takeaways / CZ kontext

- **The "fairly decent" + "what's caused you to feel like…"** structure works perfectly in Czech: *"Ten fakturační systém, co teď máte — je v pohodě, ujde to. Ale co vás vede k pocitu, že to nemusí být úplně to nejlepší, co jde z toho dostat?"*
- **100% love anchor** in CZ: "Jste 100% v pohodě s tím, jak vám to funguje?" Or: "Jste 100% spokojený s tím, kolik vám to přitahuje klientů?"
- **"Forcing"** in CZ: "Nutí vás každý rok zvedat ceny?" / "Tlačí vás do…" — very emotional in Czech, even more than English.
- **"Besides X, what else…"** is the most under-used problem-stacking pattern in CZ sales. *"Krom toho, že vám klesly tržby v lednu — co dalšího vás na téhle situaci vlastně štve?"*
- **Playful "doom and gloom"** translates well: "Tak hele, ono to nemůže bejt všechno tak špatný — co se vám tam vlastně líbí?" → relaxes Czech reserve.
