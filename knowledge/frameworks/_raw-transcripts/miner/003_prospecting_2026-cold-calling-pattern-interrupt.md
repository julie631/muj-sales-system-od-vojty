# Body

[0:02] Most salespeople lose the sale before they even finish the first few sentences. How you came across, the tone, triggers fight-or-flight in the prospect's brain. The guard goes up. "Not interested. We already have somebody." Over at hello.

[0:33] Within 3–4 seconds of answering, the prospect's subconscious asks one question: **"Is this person someone I can trust? Am I safe?"** If yes → curiosity. If no → sale is dead.

> "Nobody tells a surgeon just to do more surgeries and eventually one will work out, right? They have to get the skill. The rep who talks to 10 prospects with the right framework outperforms the rep who talks to 50 prospects without it."

## Why the standard openers fail

[1:34] **Standard opener (broken):**
> "Hi, my name is X, I'm with XYZ company, the reason why I called was…"

→ Prospect's brain pattern-matches: "Salesperson. Guard up. Not interested." 99% rejection.

[2:06] **Slightly better but still broken:**
> "Hey John — full disclosure, this is a cold call. If you can give me 2 minutes I'll go over how we can do XYZ. Fair enough?"

→ A bit better but still triggers the salesperson script in their head. Most still bail at the end.

**Three tone failures that trigger guard:**
1. Monotone → sounds like a telemarketer.
2. Way too excited → sounds like a pitch.
3. Timid / nervous / anxious → triggers their Spidey sense.

## The NEPQ Problem Statement opener

[4:41] **Write this down. The full opener:**

> "Mary — hey, it's it's Jeremy. Jeremy Miner. I'm holding a copy of your blank, and I was wondering if you could possibly help me out for a moment."

Key elements:

### 1. Familiar tone

> "Have you ever gotten a call: 'Hey, it's Bill. Bill Smith. How are you?' What do you do? 'Uh, I'm good, how are you?' Your brain is going *who is Bill Smith? Did I meet him at a networking event?* You never say 'Who is this?' because their tone sounds like they already know you."

Sound like the prospect should already know you. Their brain spends the first 5–10 seconds trying to place you — pattern interrupt.

### 2. Verbal pause

> "Hey, it's *(pause)* Jeremy. *(pause)* Jeremy Miner."

Periods in writing = pauses in delivery.

### 3. "I'm holding a copy of your [X]" — with paper crinkle

Literally crinkle the papers. Triggers credibility — sounds real.

Examples by industry:
- "I'm holding a copy of the 2026 regulations for XYZ"
- "I'm holding a copy of your property tax records there on Willow Lane"
- "I'm on your website looking at [X]"
- "I'm looking at a copy of your online reviews from some of your previous doctors"
- "I'm holding a copy of one of your recent ads on Indeed about looking for reliable drivers"

It must connect to something you can solve for them.

### 4. Confused tone — "I was wondering if you could possibly help me out for a moment"

[7:49] Slight confusion in your voice triggers curiosity. They go: "Wait — what's this?"

> "Is that something you're doing to people or for people? You're doing it FOR them — because if you can't get their guard down in the first 10–15 seconds, they don't open up, they get rid of you, you can't solve any of their problems. You offer no value."

## "Sell the exit"

[9:21] After they ask "Sure, how can I help you?" or "Who is this again?":

> "Yeah, it's it's Jeremy. Um, and I'm not even sure if it makes sense for us to talk."

OR:

> "Well, I'm not even sure if you're the right person I should be talking to. I called to see who would be responsible in your company for looking at any possible hidden gaps in your [X] that could be causing the company to [Y]. Who should I be talking to about that?"

**Why this works:** First job is to lower the guard. If you sound like you might walk away, guard drops. You're controlling the frame.

### The "hidden gaps" structure

> "Hidden gaps in your [SYSTEM] that could be causing the company to [LOSE/MISS/UNDERPERFORM]."

Examples:
- Marketing agency: "hidden gaps in your lead generation that could be causing the company to lose sales they could be making"
- SaaS (recruiting): "hidden gaps in the recruiting that could be forcing you guys to keep several trucks vacant because some drivers end up quitting"
- AI dictation (hospitals): "hidden gaps that might be preventing them from seeing more patients, getting more billable hours"

The "negative consequence" piece must be something **most companies in that niche genuinely experience**. Stay neutral with "might be" / "could be causing" — don't assume yet.

### Why "Who should I be talking to about that?" not "Is that you?"

[13:31] Using "responsible" makes it hard to deflect: "Oh, yeah, I'm not responsible — that's Bill over in XYZ department."

If transferred to gatekeeper voicemail logic:
> "Should I have you transfer me over to their voicemail to leave a message so they can call back later if they'd like?"

(Not "Can you transfer me to Bill?" — too predictable.)

## The "opposed / against" frame

[17:37] **Distressed property example:**
> "I represent a group of buyers, and they're purchasing five or six different properties in that four-block area where your Willow Lane property's at. After they had me look at some of the specs of your property, they had me call you to see if you'd be against maybe having a brief conversation about getting an offer on the home. **Would you be opposed to having a brief conversation around that?**"

**Critical word swap:**
- ❌ "Would you be open to that?" → "No, we're good."
- ✅ "Would you be opposed to that?" / "Would you be against that?" / "Would it be completely unrealistic?" / "Would you be completely closed off to?"

> "The number one word a prospect wants to tell any salesperson is *no*. So I work with their brain rather than against it. It's really hard for them to say 'Yes, I'd be completely closed off.'"

## Full SaaS example (AI dictation for hospitals)

[11:58] Right when they answer:

> "Hey Mary, it's it's Jeremy. Jeremy Miner. I'm looking at a copy of your online reviews from some of your previous doctors, and I was wondering if you could possibly help me out for a moment?"

When they ask "What's this all about?":

> "Well — and I'm not even sure who I should be talking to. I called to see who would be the person responsible for like helping the doctors maybe reduce some of the time they're having to dictate their notes that might be preventing them from seeing more patients, getting more billable hours. Who should I be talking to about that?"

[13:00] Why "responsible" + the negative consequence (loss of billable hours, can't see more patients) → curiosity, hard to deflect, must engage.

## Full trucking SaaS example (with gatekeeper)

[19:10] Gatekeeper Wanda answers:

> "Wanda, hey, it's James. James Miller. I'm holding a copy of one of your recent ads on Indeed about looking for some reliable drivers, and I was wondering if you could help me out for a moment?"

Wanda thinks you might be a driver, not a salesperson. Guard down.

> "Well, I'm not even sure who I should be talking to. I'm trying to reach the person who's responsible for like overseeing any possible hidden gaps in your systems to recruit better drivers that might be causing you to hire several that end up quitting. Who should I be talking to about that?"

→ "Oh, that's Bill in XYZ."
> "Should I have you transfer me over to their voicemail to leave a message so they can call back later if they'd like?"

When Bill picks up:
> "Bill, hey, it's James. James Miller. I'm holding a copy of one of your recent ads on Indeed about you guys looking for reliable drivers. I was wondering if you could help me out for a moment? — Yeah, what's this about? — Well, Wanda in dispatch transferred me over. She mentioned you might be the person responsible for looking at any possible hidden gaps in the recruiting that could be **forcing** you guys to keep several trucks vacant because some drivers end up quitting. Are you the person I should be talking to about that?"

**Force** = emotional word. "Nobody likes to be forced or made to do" — taps into resistance.

## Key takeaways / CZ kontext

- The **"I'm holding a copy of"** opener can be adapted for local businesses: "Mám tady otevřenou vaši stránku/Google Maps profil — chtěla jsem se na něco rychle zeptat, jestli byste mi mohl/a pomoct na pár vteřin?"
- **"Familiar tone"** — sound like you already know the prospect. Czech: "Honzo, ahoj — tady {tvoje jméno}. {Tvoje jméno a příjmení}."
- **"Selling the exit"** — strongest single CZ adaptation. "Nejsem si vůbec jistá, jestli má smysl, abychom o tom mluvili..." Lowers Czech distrust dramatically.
- **"Opposed not open"** — Czech: "Bylo by úplně mimo, kdybychom si o tom 5 minut popovídali?" or "Byl byste úplně proti tomu si o tom krátce promluvit?"
- **Hidden-gaps statement** — adapt to the prospect's business, e.g. a language school: "skryté mezery v tom, jak udržujete studenty mezi semestry, které by mohly způsobovat, že se jich na další kurz zapíše míň, než by mohlo".
