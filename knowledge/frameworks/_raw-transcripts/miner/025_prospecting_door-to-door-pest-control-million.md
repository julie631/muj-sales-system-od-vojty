# Body

## Opener — reference-neighbor

[1:03]
> "Hey, how's it going? Sorry for getting the killer going *(alarm)*. We were swinging over — did Max mention we'd be stopping by? A couple streets over, got that big red door?"

**Mechanism:** Drops the name of a real neighbor down the street. Lower guard immediately.

[1:35] **Industry-specific pain seeding:**
> "We were getting called out with the weather starting to shift. Everyone's been getting little tiny black sugar ants popping up in the kitchen area. And with this stucco wall, when the cracks settle, that's how those scorpions have been pushing into the wall voids."

→ Specific. Concrete. Visual. Triggers "wait, do I have that?" response.

## "Already out here / route already paid for"

[5:42] When asked about price:
> "What we found out here — whether you guys rent or own — like literally no one likes bugs. Cool thing is, since we're doing most of the renters, we offer a massive renters discount."

[6:13]
> "If you were to call us up off the phone book, it would run $275 to send a separate truck down here. But all the homes I'm working for out here have collectively pitched in for this stuff together. So I figured if I can group you guys in while we're already out here…"

Anchor: $275 single price. Drop to discount because of route.

## Painting the bug pain visually

[9:00] Knock 2 — Coleman in Arizona, originally from Minnesota:
> "Let me show you this real quick. *(takes them outside)* All these little egg sacks popping up — when it starts to cool down, they'll get inside the house. So instead of just doing little sprays (which you could do yourself), I'll get all these down and put a product that repels them away. That way if you or the spouse are walking through the door, you're not getting webs in your face."

Tactic: physically walk them out, point at evidence, paint vivid scenario.

## Small compliance → big ask

[4:40]
> "I've noticed there's a lot of screen doors out here. So that's why I'm trying to get people in their backyard face to face. They don't have a door to close."
>
> "You want to bring them outside of the door for sure. What it is is you're getting them to do smaller things where they're complying to what you're asking — which leads to the bigger ask."

## Pain reframing — "You haven't been bit by the wolf spider yet?"

[4:09] Miner adds:
> "If you have a prospect that says 'I don't see anything' — *(skeptical concerned tone)* 'Oh, okay. So you haven't been bit by like the wolf spider yet?' See the doubt. He just creeps in."

## "Would you be completely opposed?" close

[14:39]
> Avery: "Hey, insides included — would you be completely opposed?"

[14:39] Why this wording wins:
> "With doors specifically — everyone's hardwired to say no. So in that situation when they say no, it actually means yes. Safer word for an unsafe word is to say YES. So it's hard for them to say yes when you say 'would you be open?' But easier to say no — which leads to the yes — when you say 'would you be completely opposed?'"

## Reading body language to know you've sold

[14:08]
> Avery: "I think the big thing is when you start picking up on the little body language — head nods and 'oh yeah, I wouldn't want that to happen.' At that point, the sale's done. So how I've always liked to go about it is just asking them something simple like 'hey, insides included, would you be completely opposed?'"

## The Steph Curry practice principle

[7:47]
> "Steph Curry shoots like 2,500 three-pointers in practice every other day. Everybody wonders why he's so good. Because he's winning the game in PRACTICE."

> "That's what door-to-door is. You're winning the game before you go on the doors. The more your skill is developed, the more confidence — because you're closing more deals."

## "Where would her jaw go if she saw the scorpion?"

[13:07] Avery uses spouse + scenario to paint emotion mid-close:
> "Your wife from Minnesota too? She hasn't seen the scorpions yet then?"
> "No, but she would freak out."
> "What would go like — where would her jaw go? Like if she saw the scorpion on the ground?"
> "She'd lose her mind. She'd probably throw me across."

→ Vivid mental movie of the worst-case = sells the prevention.

## Key takeaways / CZ kontext

- **Reference-neighbor opener** for in-person visits to local businesses in CZ: *"Honzo, ahoj — promiň, vyrušuju. Max ze salonu tři ulice odsud, ten s tou velkou cedulí — neříkal ti, že tě dnes budu zkoušet stavit?"* Even if Max doesn't exist by name, "kolega ze salonu pár ulic odsud" works.
- **"While we're already out here"** translates to: *"Dělám teď {tvoje služba} pro pár salónů ve Strašnicích — když už tu jsem, hodím to za zvýhodněnou cenu."*
- **Anchor-and-discount** structure: *"Normálně je to {tvoje cena}. Pro tebe — když jste v té skupině těch tří salónů, co teď děláme — to dáme za {zvýhodněná cena}."* (ceny ze sekce Ceník v `knowledge/moje-nabidka.md`)
- **"Would you be completely opposed?"** in CZ: *"Bylo by úplně proti tobě si o tom zkusit dát 15minutový hovor přes Zoom?"* — same psychology, Czech word.
- **Small compliance** for your offer: pošli odkaz na vaše Google Maps → pošli mi email kontakt → 15min hovor → demo → kontrakt. Každý krok malý, samostatně zvládnutelný.
- **Steph Curry principle** = you need to do 50+ cold calls/week in practice mode, not wait for "important" leads. Win the game in practice.
