# Body

## Two emotional drivers of change

[0:02] **Driver 1 — Pain.** Layown sales feel more pain from the problem than from losing the money to fix it. But only ~5% of prospects (often 1–2%) are layowns.

**Driver 2 — Fear of future pain.** They don't have much pain now but they fear it could happen or keep happening. Life insurance is the perfect example — no current pain until they see a relative die without coverage and the family fall into debt.

[1:36] **You need them feeling BOTH.** Only current pain → not enough urgency. Only future fear → too speculative. Together → unstoppable.

## Why prospects give surface-level answers

[1:36] Surface-level questions ("Can you tell me two challenges you're having?") get surface-level answers. The prospect's brain has been trained — they know where those questions lead.

> "If they tell you the problem, that's not really pain. You're only going to get the layowns this way."

## The 5 precision probes

### 1. Repeat the emotional word — concerned/confused tone

[4:40] Prospect: "I just feel stressed about [problem]." Or tension, frustrated, anxiety, annoyed, pressure.

You: *(concerned + slightly confused tone)* "Stress?"

Or: "Tension?"

Or: "Frustrated?"

The confused part: like you don't understand what *they* mean by stressed. Because stress means something different to every prospect — you genuinely don't know what they specifically mean.

[6:14] Result: "Your prospect will emotionally dump. They'll be like 'Oh gosh, you have no idea.' Then steer with more clarifying / probing — don't just let them dump for 7 minutes."

### 2. "How long has that been going on for?"

[6:44] Once they emotionally dump, ask:

> "How long has that been going on for?"

Purpose: gets them to **internalize how long they've had the problem without fixing it.** If they say 6 months or 2 years, the question's job is done — they hear themselves say it.

### 3. "Has that had an impact on you?" — verbal pacing

[7:18] Once they tell you the duration, slow way down:

> "So this has been going on for the past six months. *(pause)* Has that... *(pause)* had an impact on you?"

> "If you ask the question too fast — 'so this has been going on for six months has it had an impact on you?' — they give you a knee-jerk 'yeah it's not that bad.' The biggest reason prospects give vague answers is you ask the question so fast you give them no time to internalize."

**Industry hook example:** "You've been having to manually dictate your notes the last 6 years at that hospital. *(pause)* Has that had... an impact on you?"

### 4. "What's that doing to you?" — when already emotionally open

[8:21] If by tonality or body language they're already emotionally opening up — skip the "impact on you" question and lean in soft:

> *(soft, concerned tone)* "What's that doing to you?"

> "What's this doing to you?"

→ "Emotions fly."

### 5. "Can you give me a specific example?"

[8:51] When they list problems without pain ("We're having some issues with XYZ and ABC"):

> "Can you give me a specific example of when that actually happened?"

→ Takes them back to past history. Now they're reliving a specific painful event, not abstracting.

### Two-layer impact — Company first, then Personal

[9:23] After the specific example:

> "How does that affect your company / department?"

→ This is **logical**. Easier for them to answer, especially a C-level executive or analytical Type-A personality.

Then:

> "How does that affect you personally though?"

→ This is **emotional**. Much harder for execs to open up about. If asked first → guard goes up. If asked SECOND, after the logical answer warmed them up → they open up.

> "This is more logical. *(company)* This is more emotional. *(personal)* That's why I'm putting that second."

## Key takeaways / CZ kontext

- **Only 5% are layowns** → the other 95% need precision probing to feel pain. Most CZ small-business prospects are NOT layowns.
- **Both pain + fear of future pain.** For a physiotherapy practice: pain = empty Tuesdays now; fear of future pain = "co když se to ještě zhorší až přijde recese / až vám konkurence otevře vedle".
- **Company-impact → personal-impact** sequence is gold for small-business sales — an owner who also works in the business is both. Ask "co to dělá s tržbami" first, then "co to dělá s vámi osobně, doma?"
- **One-word echo + concerned/confused tone** stays the single most powerful move.
