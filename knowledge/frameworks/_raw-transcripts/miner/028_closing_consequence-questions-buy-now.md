# Body

## Three jobs Consequence Questions do

[0:34]
1. **Seed doubt** that they might be making a mistake — without them getting defensive
2. **Get the prospect to defend themselves** on why they need to change
3. **Get them to question** why they've allowed the problem to stay the same

> "When the prospect internally starts questioning why they've allowed the problem to stay the same, they start to see they need to change — and they change WITH YOU."

## The email-death scenario

[2:08] Prospect message:
> "We really liked you and what you went over, but right now it's not a good time. Keep in touch."

OR:
> "We really liked your presentation, but we decided to go a different direction."

**Wrong response:** rebuttals via email, defending why you're right.

> "Numbers game — you might get 1 or 2 out of 10. I want to show you a way to get 6, 7, 8 out of 10."

## Step 1 — Call them, don't email

[2:38]
> "Calling them is much better. You hear their tonality. They hear yours. You can actually overcome the objection rather than just emailing."

## Step 2 — Sell the exit / disarm

[3:09]
> "Hey John, yeah I got your message. And hey — don't don't worry about it. Can I — can I ask you something though? And you can always get back to me later."

[3:40]
> "I'm always exiting the stage. They feel like I'm not going to spend a lot of time with them. My good friend Dan Lok calls this 'selling the exit.' I call it 'disarming the prospect' — same thing. Get their guard down so they tell me the real concern."

> "Notice I said 'Can I — can I ask you something' not 'Let me ask you' (bulldoze). Concerned tone, empathetic."

## Step 3 — The deframe question

[4:43]
> "Yeah sure Jeremy, go ahead."
> "Well I guess — how can I communicate to you that you might be making a mistake without you getting upset with me?"

**Tone:** concerned, empathetic. Not aggressive.

[5:14]
> "Notice — concerned tone, tone that shows empathy. What if I did it like this: 'Well, how can I let you know that you're making a massive mistake here?' Now I'm making them defensive."

> "With the right tonality: 'How can I communicate to you that you might be making a mistake without you getting upset with me?' They'll say 'Oh no, you're not going to make me upset. What did you have in mind?'"

## Step 4 — The Consequence Question (challenge → concern pivot)

[5:45]
> "Because I'm concerned — what happens if you don't do anything about this?" *(then plug in the negative consequence they care about)*

### Marketing agency example (B2B, mid-funnel):
> "Yeah, because I'm concerned. *(challenge)* Because what happens if you keep getting all these unqualified leads to reps that don't even answer the phone when they call, and you keep losing your best reps? *(soften to concern)* I mean — what happens to your business at that point?"

### Chiropractor example (diabetic patient, neuropathy):
> "*(challenge)* So what happens if you don't do anything about this? The nerves get pinched off — they have to amputate your foot. *(concern)*"

[8:56]
> "I'm starting off with that challenging tone — and ending with concern. That gets them to internalize the foot getting cut off. They visually see it."

## Step 5 — Loop with another consequence + identity frame

[9:28]
> "Well, I don't know."
> "But I mean, do you want to have to go through all that if you — *(pause)* if you didn't have to?"

If prospect: "Well, no, I don't want that to happen."

> "I mean, you certainly sound motivated. *(getting them to qualify to you)* I mean for you — why is *(pause)* THIS so important to you now? Like, why not push it down the road *(pause)* like a lot of people do who end up having to be in a wheelchair the rest of their life?"

[10:28]
> "Now I'm pushing them. I'm getting them — this is called an Identity Frame. Why would I emphasize 'this' with verbal pauses? Because if I said 'why is this so important to you now' (monotone) — they don't hear the word 'this.' They don't focus."

## Repeat back the NEGATIVE consequence (not the positive)

[10:59]
> "Why not push it down the road like a lot of people do who end up having to be in a wheelchair the rest of their life? — repeat back the NEGATIVE consequence."

> "Don't say 'why not push it down the road like a lot of people do?' — because then they might just push it down the road."

## Key takeaways / CZ kontext

- **Call, don't email** — when you get the "moc se nám to líbí, ale teď to není správný čas" email, ALWAYS pick up the phone, never email back. Hearing tonality is everything.
- **"Sell the exit"** in CZ: *"Honzo, ahoj — vůbec se neboj, nebudu tě zdržovat. Můžu se tě jen na něco zeptat? A klidně se mi pak ozveš třeba za měsíc."*
- **The deframe** in CZ: *"Jak ti vlastně mám sdělit, že tohle možná není to nejlepší rozhodnutí — aniž bys mě za to nesnášel?"* (works even better in Czech because of self-deprecation default).
- **Challenge → Concern pivot** for a business consequence, e.g. a language school: *"Co se stane, když s tím nic neuděláš? Ty kurzy ti zůstávají poloprázdné a studenti ti dál odcházejí ke konkurenci, co se o ně líp stará. *(přechod do concern)* Co se s tou školou stane za rok?"*
- **Negative identity frame** for CZ owners: *"Proč to dělat teď a neodkládat to jako spousta majitelů, co po roce zavřou, protože nepřišli na to, že jim klienti tiše utíkají?"*
- **CZ note** — the amputation/wheelchair example is too visceral for Czech taste. Soften to: *"…jako spousta lidí, co pak končí na invalidním důchodu."* Adjust visceral level to audience.
