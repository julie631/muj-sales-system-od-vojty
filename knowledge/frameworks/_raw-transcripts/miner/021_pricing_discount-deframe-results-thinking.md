# Body

## Step 1 — Don't say yes/no. Dig.

[1:02] When prospect asks: "Can you give me a discount / cheaper price?"

> "Well, I mean, it depends. *(soft concern tone)* Now, you must have asked me that for a reason though. Like — what's what's going on?"

Don't commit. Don't refuse. Just ask why they asked.

## Step 2 — Catch the lies

[2:35] After they say "Competitor offered 10% less":

> "Oh, yeah. What specifically did they offer you? I mean, if you really thought about it — what did they actually offer you?"

[2:35]
> "A lot of your prospects will actually lie to you. Why do they lie? Because they don't trust you. That's an upstream issue — there's something you're saying or your tone that's triggering distrust. So sometimes they make up the cheaper offer. But how do you know? Ask 'what specifically?'"

If they can't tell you → **red flag, they're making it up.**

## Step 3 — Plant doubt on competitor quality (without bashing)

[3:35]
> "Yeah, I mean — *(neutral tone)* they're a fairly decent company. It just depends on the result you're looking for."

*(Stop. They'll ask 'what do you mean?')*

> "Well, I guess what I'm meaning is — is the **cheapest** price the most important thing to you, or actually getting [REPEAT THEIR STATED RESULT]?"

## Step 4 — The "cheapest" anchor

[4:08]
> "Notice I said 'cheapest' — not 'price.' Does 'cheapest' sound like a good thing? Like it's going to break. Like it's going to be horrible. Like it's cheap. Nobody likes something that's cheap."

**Tone is critical** — the word "cheapest" must SOUND fragile, breakable.

> "Is the CHEAPEST price the most important thing to you, or getting [result]?" *(emphasis on cheapest like it's a bad word)*

If said monotone → doesn't work.

## Step 5 — Industry-specific examples

### Marketing agency (scaling to $12M/month)
[4:38]
> "Is the cheapest price the most important thing to you, or actually getting higher quality leads so you can scale to $12 million a month?"

> "What's going to put you in the best position to scale to 12 million a month — cheap leads that don't convert, or higher quality leads so you can actually sell more?"

### Employee benefits (retaining executives)
[8:14]
> "Is the cheapest price the most important thing to you, or putting in the right benefits package so your top executives don't leave the company?"

## Step 6 — Loop with the resource frame

[6:09]
> "Because I mean — we could give you a cheap price. We could give you cheap leads. I I guess I'm just concerned because — how would we be able to dedicate the resources necessary to help you scale to $12 million a month without the funding for us to help you get higher quality leads?"

**Concerned tone.** The frame: "I'm worried we can't get you the outcome you said you want, on those resources."

### Employee benefits loop
[8:45]
> "Because we could give you a cheap plan. I guess my concern for you guys is — how would you be able to retain your top employees so you can keep growing the business if we put in a cheap plan that they don't even like?"

[9:15]
> "How would you be able to retain your top employees from leaving you to go to these other companies by putting in a cheap plan that they're probably not going to like?"

→ Loops back into the results frame. Prospect re-justifies the value.

## Key takeaways / CZ kontext

- **"You must have asked me that for a reason though"** = perfect CZ deflect: *"No, to záleží. Ale ptáš se mě na to z nějakého důvodu — co se děje?"*
- **"What specifically did they offer?"** in CZ to catch lies: *"A co konkrétně ti nabídli? Když si to fakt promyslíš — co ti tam přesně dali?"*
- **The "cheapest" anchor** = CZ: *"Je pro tebe nejlevnější cena to nejdůležitější — nebo to, abys měl jistotu, že ti nepřijde doměrek z finančáku?"* Say "nejlevnější" with disdain, like it's breakable.
- **The resource loop** in CZ: *"Klidně ti seženu nějaký levný řešení. Jen mě trápí — jak ti pak budem moct věnovat ten čas a nastavit to tak, aby ti to fakt přineslo ten výsledek, když na to nebudou prostředky?"*
- **Don't bash competitor** — "fairly decent" = *"v pohodě, ujde to"* — neutral, but tonality plants doubt.
