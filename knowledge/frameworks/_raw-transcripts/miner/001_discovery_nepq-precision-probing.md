# Body

[0:02] Most salespeople talk their way out of the sale. So what I'm going to show you today are four questions you can start using right now that will reframe your prospects and get them to chase you and actually close themselves. This is called NEPQ Precision Probing.

## When do you use precision probing?

[0:33] You use these probing questions when you're **building the gap** — from where the prospect is (current situation) to where they want to be. When you build the gap, you have to probe to open them up. These are called **Problem Awareness Questions**.

You can also use them when **future pacing** (Solution Awareness Questions — how do I get them to see what the future looks and feels like once their newfound problems are solved), in **Consequence Questions** (what happens if they don't change), and even in the presentation.

## Why probe like this?

[1:35] You probe like this for three reasons:

1. **Help the prospect relive the pain of their current situation.** Pain drives change. "No pain, no gain" — same in sales. Help them relive the pain → urgency to change.
2. **Get them to tell themselves why they need to change.** Reframe statements into questions where the prospect tells you (and more importantly, tells themselves) why they need to change.
3. **Get them below the surface.** Humans buy 100% on emotion — brain studies prove it. If they don't open up emotionally, they don't buy.

## Question 1 — Repeat back the emotional word

[3:09] Let's say a prospect gives you any emotional word. "It's just causing me so much anxiety." Or "It's causing a lot of tension at work." Or "It's just causing so much pressure for me at my job."

Take that one emotional word — say "stressed" — and **just repeat it back**:

> Prospect: "I'm just feeling a lot of stress."
> You: "Stress?"

That's all you have to do. Practice on a family member, spouse, or kid first. When they give you any emotional word, just repeat it back: "Stress?" or "Concerned?" or "Worried?"

[4:09] Notice the facial expressions show concern — empathy. That's a **concern tone**. Put your hand on your chest, even if you're on the phone. Body language affects tonality.

**Why it works:** When you echo the word back, the prospect's subconscious says "He didn't understand what I meant by stress — I need to explain that better." They expand. That's when they start to open up emotionally.

> "Stress?"
> "Oh, stressed. Yeah, I mean just the other day it happened again when..."

Now they're reliving the pain of their past history.

## Question 2 — "How long has that been going on for?"

[6:14] Immediately follow with: **"How long has that been going on for?"**

Why? Because when they say "Oh, it's been a month, 6 months, a year" — it suddenly hits them: *"Oh my gosh, I've had this same problem for THAT long."*

## Question 3 — "Has that had an impact on you?" (with verbal pacing)

[6:44] Once they tell you the duration, you come back:

> "Okay, so this has been going on for six months. *(slow)* Has that... had an impact on you?"

**This is verbal pacing.** Most salespeople ask too fast: "So this has been going on for six months has it had an impact on you?" Asked fast → no time to internalize → surface-level layown answer like "Yeah it's not been that good."

Slow down. Pause. Let them internalize.

## Question 4 — "In what way though?"

[7:49] If they answer vague — "You have no idea" — lean in and ask:

> "In what way?"

Followed by, when they emotionally open up:

> "What's that doing to you?"

[8:49] **Tone is everything.** Your tone is how the prospect interprets your intention behind every question. If you ask "What's it doing to you?" flat — they'll say "Not much." But if you ask **"What's that doing to you?"** with a concerned facial expression and empathy — they feel you actually care. That raises trust.

## Bonus probe — "Can you give me a specific example?"

[9:53] When a prospect just rattles off problems ("We're having problems with XYZ and ABC"), that's surface level. **Problems are not pain.** You need to find the root cause and how those problems affect them personally — even if you're selling to a Fortune 100 CEO.

> "Can you give me a specific example of when that actually happened?"

Now they're reliving the pain — "Oh, just last week this happened..." — drawing them back into their past pain.

Then follow with:
- "How long has that been going on for?"
- "How is that affecting you personally?"
- "Has that had an impact on you?"

## When NOT to ask feeling questions

[10:55] Are you going to probe and ask these feeling questions in the first 2–5 minutes of conversation? **Unlikely.** You haven't built enough trust or credibility, you haven't built a big enough gap. When you build a big enough gap, then you're *allowed* to ask the feeling questions — because trust has been earned.

## Key takeaways / CZ kontext

- The **one-word echo** ("Stres?" "Frustrující?" "Štve vás to?") works identically in Czech and is wildly under-used by CZ salespeople.
- **Verbal pacing** before the impact question is a top-1% move — slow the question down, pause, then ask.
- **Don't ask feeling questions early.** Earn the right by building the gap first.
- **Tone & facial expression carry the meaning.** Hand on chest, concerned face, slow cadence = trust signal.
