# Body

## The setup — pet food wholesaler

[0:32] Sales rep sells specialty pet food to retail store owners. Common problem the rep can solve: customers come in, dogs have stomach issues / diarrhea / digestive problems / gas, store owner can't help → customers don't come back → store loses revenue.

## Step 1 — Reframe "we're #1" subtly

[3:10] **Wrong:** "We're the number one in the market."

**Right:** "That's probably one of the biggest reasons store owners come to us."

[3:42]
> "Why did I say 'that's probably one of the biggest reasons store owners come to us'? Because instead of me saying 'we're the number one in the market' — I just said we're the number one in the market in a way where it doesn't sound the same."

## Step 2 — "We have solutions" is too vague

[3:42]
> "If I say 'we have solutions' — what does that mean to them? See how vague that is? The brain doesn't know what to focus on."

Replace with specific problem:
> "That's probably one of the biggest reasons store owners come to us — because they feel like their food's not moving off the shelves because of [specific reason X]. They come to us for that."

## Step 3 — Math the pain into a concrete dollar number

[4:46] Discovery:

> "Tell me the problem you can solve."
> Rep: "Stomach issues, diarrhea."
> "How does it affect the store owner?"
> "If they don't have an answer, the customer doesn't come back."
> "How often is this happening?"
> "A lot."
> "How often? Daily? How many a day?"
> "A few."
> "When they say that, how many do you feel like you're losing — where they don't come back?"
> "You could be losing a lot. A few customers a day."
> "What's your average customer lifetime value? Monthly?"
> "Probably at least $100 a month."

Then math live:
> "So you're losing two customers a day. What is that per week? That's $1,400."
> "Times 52 weeks? That's about $72,800 a year."

**Now the pain is a NUMBER, not an abstract feeling.**

## Step 4 — Surface the spouse opinion (preventing the spouse objection)

[6:49]
> "How many years has this been going on?"
> "A lot."
> "What's it doing to you guys?"
> "Keeping us from reaching our potential."
> "How does Mary feel about losing the $72,000 a year?"
> "She's leaving me." *(joke)*
> "Well, I know you're joking — I don't want you sleeping on the couch. *(playful then serious)* In all seriousness — how does she feel about losing that much funds?"
> "She doesn't know."
> "What would she say when you tell her?"
> "She wouldn't be happy."

Then:
> "Does she want you to figure out a way to stop the losses, or what does she want you to do?"
> "Oh yeah, she definitely wants me to do that."

[7:50]
> "All I'm doing is what? I'm preventing the objection from happening. I'm starting to take them out of that frame *(the 'I need to talk to my wife' frame)*."

## Key takeaways / CZ kontext

- **Math the pain in concrete CZK** — e.g. for a language school: empty lesson slots × 4/week × průměr 300 Kč × 52 = X CZK/rok. Specific, not abstract. *"To je 62 400 Kč za rok, který vám teď leží na stole."*
- **"How does your spouse feel?"** = surfaces the spouse-objection PRE-CLOSE for any owner with a partner/wife in the business. CZ: *"Jak se na ty ztráty kouká manželka? Vidí ty čísla?"*
- **"Probably the biggest reason X come to us"** = subtle, humble #1 positioning. CZ: *"Většina majitelů e-shopů, co k nám chodí, k nám chodí prostě protože…"* — works without sounding arrogant.
- **Replace vague "we solve problems"** — always plug in specific problem. CZ: *"Konkrétně řešíme to, že vám na konci roku nepřijde doměrek daně, se kterým jste nepočítali."*
- **Problems aren't real — they're stories.** Mindset shift: *"Nemám peníze" je příběh, ne realita. Důležitý je, jaký jiný problém peníze zakrývají.*
