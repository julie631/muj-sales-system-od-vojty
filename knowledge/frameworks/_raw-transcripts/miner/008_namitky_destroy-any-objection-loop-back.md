# Body

## The setup — health coaching prospect ghosting

[1:02] Miner role-plays with an Australian student (health coach for entrepreneurs with low energy, brain fog, poor focus).

**Pre-call gap-building map:**
- Stack consequences vertically:
  - Won't have energy to dominate the business
  - Lose money, lose productivity
  - Business performs worse
  - Won't be around much longer
  - Won't be able to provide for family

> "Where's the sale made? Not up here about getting healthier. Six layers deeper than that."

## The frame to break

[2:34] After booking + demo, the prospect's frame:
> "We really liked you and what you had to say, but now's not a good time for us. We'll get back to you when we're interested. Keep in touch."

(The email-death.)

Default sales response: rebuttal arsenal → "Why did you respond then? I have a solution that…" → prospect ghosts harder.

## Step 1 — Deframe with concerned tone

[3:36] Loop back via call:

> "Well, I'm not that cool, but thanks for the compliment. Can I — *(pause)* can I ask you something? And you can always get back to me down the road. Can I ask you something? *(prospect: 'Sure.')* How… how can I communicate to you that you might be making a mistake — without you getting upset with me?"

**This is the deframe question.** It's an NEPQ Consequence Question.

> "What that question just did — his frame was 'now's not a good time, get back to me down the road, keep in touch.' I'm taking him OUT of that frame with this question."

**Why concerned tone?** Seeds doubt: *he knows something I don't.* Guard comes down → open.

## Step 2 — Loop and stack consequences

[5:39] After the deframe lands, **loop back** through specific consequences:

> "Yeah, well, I don't want to get you angry at me. I guess the question I had for you, and like I said, you always get back to me — how how can I communicate to you that you might be making a mistake without you getting upset with me? *(challenge tone, then concern)* Because I'm concerned for you. Because I mean — what what happens if you don't do anything about this and you keep having these issues with your gut where you're having these like bowel movements during the day at work, and then your boss starts getting upset with you — like how long is he going to give you before he lets you go?"

> Prospect: "Not long."

> "Well — what's going to happen to you and your family at that point?"

> Prospect: "I probably won't be able to provide for them."

> "Are you going to let that happen?"

> Prospect: "No."

## Step 3 — Surface the spouse objection from inside

[6:41]
> "I mean, how does your spouse feel about it though? Does your spouse — like, does she want you to keep having these issues? Were you like having a hard time even performing at work? Like does she want you to keep going through that?"

> Prospect: "No, she's pretty pissed at me."

> "How do you mean by pissed?"

> Prospect: "Oh, she's probably not happy with me."

**Notice:** the spouse objection ("I need to talk to my wife") is now defused — she's on record (via him) as wanting him to fix it.

## Step 4 — Identity frame

[7:42]
> "I don't want you sleeping on the couch tonight, man — but in all seriousness, does she want you to get your health back so you can really get promoted into that CEO role and make more money for the family? Or what does she want you to do?"

> Prospect: "Yeah, she would love that."

> "Okay — but why now? Like why is this so important now? Why look at doing this now? Why not push it down the road like a lot of executives do who end up never fixing their health and never get promoted?"

**The identity frame:** "a lot of executives who end up never fixing it" = the identity he doesn't want to be. Pushes him toward the version of himself he WANTS to be.

> Prospect: "If I pushed it back, my health would probably just get worse."

> "And then what happens at that point?"

> "My performance would decline."

> "What should we do then?"

> Prospect: "We should do what you were selling me."

## What Miner did — checklist

[7:42] **Recap of the frames moved:**
1. **Deframe** ("how can I communicate you might be making a mistake")
2. **Consequence stack** down to family impact
3. **Spouse-objection pre-empt** ("how does your spouse feel about the negative problem")
4. **Identity frame** ("why now, not like those execs who never fix it")

> "I took him out of four frames there. You guys probably didn't know what I was doing."

## Key takeaways / CZ kontext

- **The deframe opener** is the **highest-leverage line of the whole transcript** for CZ. Direct translation: *"Můžu se vás na něco zeptat? A klidně se mi pak ozvete třeba za měsíc. Můžu se vás na něco zeptat? — Jak vám můžu sdělit, že tohle možná není úplně správný rozhodnutí, abyste se na mě nezačal zlobit?"*
- **Concerned tone** in Czech doesn't sound passive-aggressive when paired with empathy — Czech prospects respond strongly to it.
- **Spouse-objection from the inside** ("jak na to kouká manželka?") works perfectly for owners with co-owners and family business contexts.
- **Identity frame** translation: *"Proč se na to dívat teď a ne to odkládat jako spousta majitelů malých firem, co pak vlastně nikdy nezačnou a po roce zavřou?"* — uses Czech fear-of-future-pain directly.
- **6-layer consequence stack** must be **mapped pre-call** for your pitch — e.g. pain consequences for a small construction company: poptávka bez odpovědi → menší tržby → nemůže si vzít volno → vyhoří → zavře. Map it before the call so you can loop it under pressure.
