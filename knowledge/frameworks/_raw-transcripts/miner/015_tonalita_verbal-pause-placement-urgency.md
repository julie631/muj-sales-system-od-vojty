# Body

## Why verbal pauses matter — Obama and MLK

[0:03]
> "Think about the greatest speeches. Tony Robbins. Obama was an amazing gifted orator — when he spoke, he pauses a lot. *'Americans want hope. They want change. They want—'* see that verbal pause. If he just talked fast: 'Americans want hope they want change they want—' there's no emotion."

> "Or Martin Luther King: 'I have a dream… that one day… all men will be created equal.' The verbal pauses force your brain to think about the next thing he says after the pause. If he talked fast, we probably wouldn't even know about that speech."

> "The power of the verbal pause is crazy when you're trying to persuade and influence. It causes them to think deeper and internalize."

## Example 1 — Cybersecurity fraud stack

[1:39]
> "Are you *(pause)* 100% *(pause)* happy with the way your fraud stack is set up?"

Or:
> "Is your management team *(pause)* 100% *(pause)* satisfied with the way your fraud stack is set up?"

**Key:** pause BEFORE 100% and AFTER 100%. Instead of "satisfied" you can use "happy" or "content." Then a third verbal pause before "is set up."

### Why "100%"?

[2:12]
> "I say 100% because no one is satisfied with anything 100%. There's always something you want better about it. Think about the person you're dating or married to now — the first couple months they were completely perfect, didn't do anything wrong. 6 months later, you found things you didn't like. That's human behavior 101."

## Example 2 — Urgency placement ("now though")

[2:42]
> "Can I ask why having a nicer car is so important to you *(pause)* now though?"

→ Brain focuses on **now** → urgency.

[3:45] **Same words. Different pause placement. Different focus.**

> "Can I ask why *(pause)* THIS is so important to you now though?"
> "Can I ask why fixing the fraud stack is so important to you now though?"

→ Brain focuses on **this** (whatever they said they want) → self-justification of value.

> "I control what their brain thinks by the verbal pause. Remember — I'm not doing that TO them. I'm doing that FOR them. Selling is something you do FOR people."

## Example 3 — Trucking SaaS (problem awareness)

[5:49]
> "So you've been advertising on Indeed the last couple of years. *(pause)* I mean, do you *(pause)* 100% *(pause)* like the results you've been getting?"

If they've already told you about flaky drivers:

> "Besides getting some of these flaky drivers — because you've been on Indeed the last 12 months — do you *(pause)* 100% *(pause)* like the type of people that you're bringing in from those?"

## Example 4 — Recruiting / staffing

[6:52]
> "So you've been using Indeed the last 3 years. *(pause)* I mean, what's caused you to feel like *(pause)* that's not getting you the best people?"

**Two strategic pauses:**
1. Pause before "what's caused you to feel like" → focus 1 = the emotional driver.
2. Pause before "that's not getting you the best people" → focus 2 = the problem itself.

### Why "feel" not "think" or "make"

[7:24]
> "The word is FEEL — because I want to keep them on their emotional side of their brain. I don't want to say 'what's making you look at this' — I want to say 'what's caused you to FEEL like.'"

## The principle

[7:55]
> "If I can't help them get a very clear picture of what their real problems are and how that's affecting them, they feel no urgency to change. They feel no urgency to change → they don't buy → their problems don't get solved."

## Key takeaways / CZ kontext

- **The pause-placement technique translates 1:1 to Czech.** *"Můžu se zeptat, proč je *(pauza)* TOHLE pro vás teď tak důležitý?"* vs *"…proč je tohle pro vás *(pauza)* TEĎ tak důležitý?"*
- **100% anchor with double pauses** in CZ: *"Jste *(pauza)* 100% *(pauza)* spokojený s tím, jak vám ten účetní program funguje?"*
- **"Feel" → "cítit / mít pocit"** in Czech is even more emotionally loaded than English. *"Co vás vede k pocitu, že to nemusí být úplně to nejlepší řešení?"* vs. the trash version *"Co vás nutí to měnit?"* — first is emotional, second triggers defense.
- **Czech speakers naturally pause less than English speakers** — deliberately practicing pauses gives you a major edge. Record a 60-second voice note, slow down 30%.
