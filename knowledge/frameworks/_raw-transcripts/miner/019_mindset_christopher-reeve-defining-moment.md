# Body

## The 30/70 stat — correction to the 93/7 myth

[0:32]
> "There's a book that says 93% of communication is non-verbal. That comes from the 1940s. Not very accurate. They have more data now. It's about 30% of the sale is your words, not 7. So words DO matter. But 70% is body language and tonality."

## Tom Cruise / Mission Impossible analogy

[1:04]
> "Imagine Tom Cruise in Mission Impossible — 'we got to go down by the van because there is a bomb there and if there's a bomb there it could blow up and everybody could die.' Actors at the highest level are taught to keep you engaged through body language, facial expressions, and tonality. That's how. You read it in a book but nobody teaches HOW you do it."

## The defining moment story

[1:35] Miner at 23, second year in sales. Saw an offer at an event for a $126k training (~$280k inflation-adjusted). Was "pumped" to do it.

[2:07] Goes to the back-of-room sales table. Doesn't like the salesperson's voice. Notices orange shoes. Decides: "It's a sign from God, I shouldn't do it." Walks away.

[2:37]
> "It's interesting when we want to find an out or a reason not to do something — we find it, don't we?"

[3:08] Goes home to dump apartment in Orem, Utah. Newly married, daughter on the way. Reflects on telling family he'd be successful — yet he hadn't taken action.

## Christopher Reeve on Larry King

[4:40] Miner sees Larry King interviewing Christopher Reeve.

> Larry King: "Mr. Reeve, does it bother you that at one point in your life you were the strongest man on the planet? You were Superman. Men wanted to be you. Women wanted to be with you. Kids admired you. But here you are today sitting in the studio with two tubes sticking out of your neck just to breathe. Mr. Reeve, does it bother you that people have to come in here just to help you go to the bathroom? Mr. Reeve, does it bother you that you are physically paralyzed?"

> Reeve: "You know what Larry? It does not bother me. But what does bother me is this. It bothers me that there are men and women out there in this world right here, right now, that are physically able to do anything they want in their lives, yet they are **paralyzed in their mind**."

> Larry: "Mr. Reeve, are you afraid to die?"

> Reeve: "You know what Larry? I'm not afraid to die. Because the day I go out, the day I leave this planet, I'm going to leave this planet like a champion, like a victor — climbing the mountain of success and not complain about my circumstances like a victim."

## The mirror moment

[7:47]
> "I got off the couch that night and walked into my little girl's room. She was one at the time. Walking out, there was this little mirror and I caught my reflection. I went back and looked in the mirror and I said:
>
> Jeremy — are YOU the one paralyzed in your mind? Are you the one paralyzed in your finances? Are you the one paralyzed to take action on doing something you know is going to benefit you, your family, and help other people? Are you going to let a few thousand dollars prevent you from having the thing you've always wanted?"

## "Today's the day"

[8:48]
> "I started saying this statement over and over again, and I still say it to this day:
>
> Today's the day. Today's the day. Today's the day. Today's the day we move forward, and we take action and go where we want to go."

## Key takeaways / CZ kontext

- **CZ adaptation: HIGH.** Christopher Reeve / Larry King are unknown in CZ. The STORY structure is gold but needs CZ swap.
- **30/70 stat correction** — quote this when training reps in Czech: *"7% slov je mýtus z 40. let. Reálně je to asi 30% slova, 70% tonalita a body language."*
- **"Paralyzed in your mind"** = transferable concept regardless of the story. CZ: *"Jsi ten typ, co je fyzicky schopnej cokoli — ale paralyzovaný v hlavě?"*
- **CZ substitute story idea:** your own defining moment in your business. Tell a 2-minute story about a moment you could have quit but didn't, then end with "today's the day." Adapt the structure, not the reference.
- **"Today's the day" mantra** in Czech: *"Dneska je ten den."* — repeat 3-5 times in a row, builds rhythm and commitment.
