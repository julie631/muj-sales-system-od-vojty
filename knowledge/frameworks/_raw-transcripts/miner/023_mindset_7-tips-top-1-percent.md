# Body

## Miner's track record (the credibility opener)

[0:02]
> "During my 18-year sales career, I made about $33 million in straight commissions. That's about $279 million worth of product I sold for the companies I worked for."

## Tip 1 — Master non-verbal communication

[0:35]
> "It's not the words you're saying — it's how you're saying those words."

Three components:
1. **Tonality** — interprets your intention behind every question
2. **Facial expressions** — "the remote control to your tonality"
3. **Body language** — affects tone; if you sit still, hard to shift tone up/down

## Tip 2 — Skill, not experience

[2:08]
> "It's not how long you've been in sales. The myth is 'if I just stay in long enough, eventually I'll figure it out.' You can play the long game and fail."

> "Our top rep at Seventh Level is **19**. Last year he made $900,000 in straight commissions. He'd only been in sales for about a year and a half. He invested heavily in his skill — tonality, body language, the right questions, how to prevent objections."

## Tip 3 — Learn from the best (and pay them)

[3:43]
> "Your destiny will be determined by those you learn from."

[4:14]
> "My second year in sales I hired Leonardo DiCaprio's acting coach for $127,000. I had to go beg, borrow, steal. Loan against the house I'd just bought. Many credit cards. But I went all in. The coach taught me advanced tonality, facial expressions, body language. My sales 4x'd the next year and kept multiplying."

## Tip 4 — Find the chip on your shoulder

[5:17]
> "Tom Brady was drafted last out of Michigan, 6th round. Every team didn't want him. That chip drove him to spend extra hours practicing — became the greatest QB ever."

Miner's chip:
> "When I was 11, my stepdad lost his job, went disabled. We went from middle-class to poverty. Food stamps. I went to baseball practice with Walmart shoes called Voit while all my friends had Nike, Reebok, Adidas. I just remember feeling broke. I said: I am never going to be poor like that again."

> "It doesn't matter the chip. Maybe you were wealthy and people thought you were spoon-fed — that might drive you to prove them wrong. Everyone has a chip. Activate it."

## Tip 5 — Risk tolerance

[6:52] When facing the $127k coaching investment at age 23, broke, newlywed, kid on the way:

> "I went to my bishop. He said: 'Do you believe someone who has knowledge or skill you don't possess can transfer it to you?' — 'Yes.' — 'Why are you coming to me then? You already have the answer.' Then he said: **'Jeremy, God feeds the birds, but he doesn't drop worms in their nest.'**"

The reframe Miner now uses for every big decision:

> "Which is MORE risky?
> - Do nothing — keep selling the same way, same sales, same income — OR
> - Go all in — get funding, hire the mentor, train, make more sales, income changes forever?
>
> In reality — which is more risky?"

## Tip 6 — Commit to mastery (4 rep archetypes)

[9:24] **The 4 archetypes:**

### Wingers
[9:24] Wing everything, say different things to every prospect, hope something sticks. Blame leads when they don't close. Usually get fired.

### Dabblers
[10:25] Buy a few books a year, watch free YouTube/IG, "eventually I'll figure it out." Forced to play the numbers game. Average forever.

### Know-it-allers
[10:57] Got to $100k–$500k by investing once. Then stopped — "I know everything." Income CAPPED. Stop training → income stops.

> "Steph Curry — Hall of Famer. What happens if he stops training every day? Know-it-allers always get capped."

### Committed to Mastery
[12:00] Invest funding even if they don't have it. Number one thing tied to income = skill level. Don't make the same mistakes as other reps — learn from others' mistakes.

> "These commit-to-mastery people overcome the learning curve 100x faster. They're all about speed to succeed. They get into the top 0.01%."

[13:33]
> "I never hung out with wingers or dabblers — they're jealous you're outselling them. I didn't want to hang out with know-it-allers. I wanted to be around the committed-to-mastery people. The top 5 people you hang out with is where your income's going to average."

## Tip 7 — Congruency / daily routine

[14:35]
> "People who are congruent follow a daily routine. They don't wake up Monday at 7, Tuesday at 5:30, Thursday at 10:30. They're scheduled."

> "Ever since I was 19, I wake up at 5:30 AM (sometimes 5). Weekends sleep in maybe 6:30 / 7. During the work week — up early, gym, getting things done."

> "Congruency builds self-confidence — you do what you say. And if you can't, you tell them why. You don't go MIA. It builds trust with everyone."

## Key takeaways / CZ kontext

- **Track record opener** — use your own track record similarly: *"Za {období} jsem podepsala {počet} klientů, z toho {počet} platí pravidelně. To je {měsíční obrat} z téhle niche."* (čísla ze sekce Důkazy v `knowledge/moje-nabidka.md`)
- **Skill not experience** — your top moves come from how MUCH you iterate, not how LONG you've done sales.
- **The $127k coaching investment scaled to CZ:** *"Investovala bych do sales mentora 100 000 Kč, kdybych věděla, že mi to udělá 4× obrat? Bez váhání. A je to chyba většiny CZ podnikatelů, že to nevidí."*
- **"Which is more risky?"** = perfect for a close, e.g. a language school: *"Co je riskantnější — neudělat nic a mít v září stejně poloprázdné kurzy, nebo investovat {tvoje cena} a mít naplněno?"*
- **Chip on shoulder** in CZ — your own (verify): poslední rok v životě / původ / parents' job / first business failure → "this drives me."
- **Congruency** — daily routine builds Czech credibility. Show up at the same time, deliver consistently — many Czech salespeople are unreliable, this is a differentiator.
