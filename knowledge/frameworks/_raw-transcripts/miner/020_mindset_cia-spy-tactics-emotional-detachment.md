# Body

## Emotional brain vs. tactical brain

[3:38] Question: "I take a lot of sales calls and emotional connection is really important. How often are you checking their body language?"

Andrew (ex-CIA):
> "As the professional, the onus is on you. **You can't live in your emotional brain.** You have to live in a tactical brain that's moving the conversation in a direction that's deliberate. The better you are at it, the less they realize it's happening."

> "To start, you're really never in your emotional brain. You are always artificially acting like you are in your emotional brain because you are trying to find out what their triggers are. After you establish the connection and trust, then you can actually start living a little bit in your right side of the brain."

[4:40] Miner adds:
> "The more experience you have, your brain starts to recognize similar patterns. When I got on sales calls after a while, I wasn't thinking 'oh they did this' — I just recognized the pattern."

## The detached posture rule

[5:11]
> "When you are feeling emotional, you should not trust yourself. You need THEM to feel emotional. If you're emotional, you're living in a brain that's not logical. You're going to say something you shouldn't, misinterpret something. You're living in a subjective world instead of objective."

## "I haven't mastered sales"

[5:41]
> "How long did it take you to master sales?"
> Miner: "I haven't mastered sales. I'm still a student. I think we're all students. If I said I've mastered sales, that would make me a know-it-aller and I would stop learning. Always think of yourself as 'I know nothing. I'm a sponge. I'm always learning.'"

## Mirroring — subconscious trust signal

[6:12]
> "Mirroring is real. Yes, you can use it as a tactic, but it is very real subconsciously. When we're around people who are actually like us, who mirror our values, mirror our commitment, mirror our energy, mirror our effort — it feels good. And then you want to be around those people more."

> "It's very easy to identify when the person across from you is NOT a mirror. If you've ever looked at somebody and thought 'I see a little bit of me in you' — you know how good that feels and how much you want to see that person succeed."

## Counter-tradecraft — spotting manipulation

[7:13]
> "The number one thing: identify when YOU'RE getting emotional. If something about the other person is triggering that emotion — is it because they're using tradecraft, or is it because they're triggering something inside you? If you don't have that red flag, you'll be in your emotional brain."

[7:43] Miner connects:
> "It's the fastest way to spot a salesperson. For those of you who have had a bad salesperson try to sell you — you can see all the problems in it right away."

[8:15] Miner's signature reframe:
> "And there's a fine line. You have to quickly make an assessment: are they doing this TO me — literally trying to take advantage — or are they doing this FOR me to actually help? If I'm a psychologist talking to somebody who said they want to end their life — and you're trying to stop them — you have to decide: are they doing this to me, or are they doing this FOR me to actually help me?"

## Mastery through execution

[8:45]
> "There's a concept we have at CIA: you learn mastery through execution, not through study. You have to keep executing and executing — you will learn what works and what doesn't. But the longer you sit around planning, the less you're actually mastering anything."

> "The older generation didn't have the option of forever scrolling. We had to go to the library — that took time. So we just went out and started mowing lawns and selling newspapers."

## Selling to ASPD executives

[10:49] Question: "How do you identify core emotions of people who are not neurotypical?"

Andrew:
> "Antisocial Personality Disorder drives executives. People of non-entrepreneurial success often live on that sociopathy / psychopathy spectrum. They have a core emotion — and that core emotion is **anger**."

> "Sociopaths are driven by anger because they feel they are being forced to follow rules they disagree with."

**Sales application:**

> "Connect with their anger. Validate their anger. Even if you are a totally balanced neurotypical rule-follower, you can be like 'Yeah, it IS kind of stupid that we can't cross the street wherever we want — but it's because you can't trust drivers.' You validate their fear, and then build the emotional connection."

> "Especially with somebody who suffers from ASPD — they do not have people they trust. So when you get into that private life, you're already one of a handful. And if you get into that secret life, you've got an executive in your pocket for life."

## Key takeaways / CZ kontext

- **The detached posture rule** is the single most valuable mindset insight: STAY TACTICAL. The prospect's emotional state is the goal. Your job = orchestrator, not participant.
- **Mirroring values/commitment/energy** — CZ craftsmen and small-business owners build their identity around hustle and craft. Mirror their language, their pace, their work ethic-signals. *"Já taky každý den ráno..."* — even if it's a small overlap, it lands.
- **Validate the anger of tough executives** — many CZ small-business owners are angry at: software fees, recession, government, employees quitting. Validate first, then connect. *"Já to chápu, ono ti fakt vlastně zvyšujou ceny každý rok a ty s tím nic nemůžeš dělat — to mě by taky štvalo."*
- **"Are they doing this TO me or FOR me?"** = your core posture for every sales call. Repeat this in your head before each call.
- **Mastery through execution** — make 50 cold calls per week and learn from real reps, not from more YouTube.
