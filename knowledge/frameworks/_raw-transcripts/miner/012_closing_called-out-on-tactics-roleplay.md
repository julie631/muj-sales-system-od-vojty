# Body

## The setup

[0:01] Sales call where Miner is selling NEPQ training to a sales rep. Prospect pushes back mid-call:

> Prospect: "These are leading questions. I understand what the answer should be."

## Step 1 — Reframe as service

[1:07] Miner doesn't get defensive. Instead, drops to concerned tone and reframes:

> "Is selling something I'm doing to you or for you? Feels a little like — well, we don't… you don't have to censor it. Because what happens if you don't sell more? I mean, what's the number one thing that's attached to you as a salesperson that determines your income? You make my sales — right? So maybe something we might want to do — learn how to do it better, probably a good idea, right? Because if you don't, what happens?"

Notice: re-positions the "leading question" complaint as concern FOR them, not selling TO them.

## Step 2 — The 4 rep archetypes (identity ladder)

[1:41]
> "There are four types of salespeople:
>
> 1. **Wingers** — wing everything, say something different to every prospect, blame the leads when they don't close. Usually get fired eventually.
>
> 2. **Dabblers** — buy a few books, go to free events, follow YouTube/IG. 'Eventually I'll figure it out.' Average reps playing the numbers game.
>
> 3. **Know-it-allers** — invested in training once, made multiple six figures, then STOPPED investing because they think they know everything. Their income gets CAPPED.
>
> 4. **Committed to Mastery** — know the #1 thing that determines their income is the skills they learn daily. That's your top 1%."

Then the identity challenge:
> "Where do you feel you are right now?"

→ Prospect: "A dabbler."

## Step 3 — Layer who depends on them

[3:47]
> "Right. Who depends on you acquiring these skills?"
> Prospect: "Me."
> "Who else depends on you?"
> "Just me."
> "Do you have a family?" "No."
> "Do you have a mom, dad, cousins, aunts?"
> "Sure — have a mom."

[4:49]
> "Money doesn't buy us happiness — it just gives us more options. If your mom — something ever happens to her and you don't have the money to help — that's tied directly back to what you didn't learn as a salesperson to sell more."

Then expands further:
> "And your prospects depend on you. Because if we don't acquire the right skills to do that for them, what happens to them? Their problems stay the same and nothing ever changes. Whose responsibility is that?"

## Step 4 — Identity rebuild

[5:20]
> "I'm not going to call you a dabbler anymore — because who would say that, what type of salesperson?"

Lifts him out of dabbler identity.

> "So who do you WANT to become?"
> Prospect: "Obviously I want to be more successful."
> "Whose choice is that?"
> "Mine."

## Step 5 — Match the tonality

[6:57] When the prospect agrees half-heartedly, Miner catches the tonality:

> "Yes? That tone — I'm hearing your tone. You're just — you're too good at what you do."
> "Do you want to learn how to do this for your prospects, or do you just want to keep dabbling?"

Then matches the prospect's prior call-out:
> "You're really good at changing your tonality to match the vibe."

(Calibrated mirror — flips the call-out into a compliment, reduces resistance.)

## Step 6 — The close

[8:08]
> "What's the step we need to do?"
> Prospect: "Take the commitment. Just do it. Hope the best."

Then the commitment lock:
> "Can you do me one last thing before I jump off? This is a promise to me — and if you promise me this, this is a promise to yourself. When you get in the training, will you actually take it seriously?"
> "I have to."
> "And will you show up to the training calls? Because I do one every week."
> "Yeah, committed."

## Step 7 — Moral obligation close (post-call)

[9:42]
> "Selling is not something you do TO people, selling is something you do FOR people. I have a moral obligation to help get into the training programs. You have a moral obligation to get your people into what you sell."

> "Like Solomon said in the Bible — he that keepeth the corn and doesn't sell it, the sin is on your head. You have a moral obligation to help people get what they want, solve their problems, get them results. If you don't acquire the skills to do that, you're letting them down, their families down, their prospects down — everybody down."

## Key takeaways / CZ kontext

- **CZ adaptation: HIGH.** Biblical/moral references DO NOT LAND in Czech (atheist culture). REPLACE with secular framing: "máš zodpovědnost vůči klientům" / "máš zodpovědnost vůči rodině" / "máš zodpovědnost vůči sobě budoucímu".
- **4 rep archetypes** = transferable to a pitch for business owners: "Existují čtyři typy majitelů — ten co to dělá náhodně, ten co s tím lehce experimentuje, ten co si myslí, že už ví všechno, a ten kterej je odhodlanej to ovládnout." → identity ladder works in Czech.
- **"Selling is FOR people, not TO them"** is the single highest-value mindset shift. CZ: *"Není to o tom, co jim dělám, ale o tom, co pro ně dělám."*
- **Match tonality back to them** — when CZ prospect says something half-heartedly, surface it: *"Honzo, slyším ten tón. Jsi se zatím opravdu rozhodl, nebo to spíš jen říkáš?"*
- **Layer who depends on you** — e.g. for a cleaning company owner: rodina, zaměstnanci, klienti, samotný majitel. The call works when you ask: *"Kdo na tobě závisí, abys ty zakázky udržel?"*
