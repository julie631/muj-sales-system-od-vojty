# Body

## 5 Tonalities recap

[0:33] **1. Curious tone** — "your tone is how the prospect interprets your intention behind everything you say and ask"

> "So walk me through — what are you guys doing now to really build out systems to scale the company?" (curious)

vs. flat: "Walk me through Jim, what type of operations are you building out to scale the company?" (monotone → fight-or-flight)

[2:09] **2. Confused tone** — when they say "stressful":

> "Oh, how how do you mean by stressful?"

→ Subconscious: "He didn't understand. I need to explain better."

[3:11] **3. Challenging tone** — only at ½–¾ mark, after trust built:

> "So what happens if you don't do anything about this — your car keeps breaking down? What's going to happen to your job at that point?"

Note: starts CHALLENGING, ends CONCERNED. The pivot inside one question.

[5:43] **4. Concerned tone** — "the tone that shows empathy"

For stuck prospects:

> "Can I ask you something, Sally? *(prospect: 'Sure, go ahead.')* Um, and this is just off the record — what's really holding you back from moving forward?"

[6:14] Warning: tonality must be taught by someone who has mastered it. Role-playing with an untrained partner → wrong feedback.

[6:46] **5. Playful tone** — disarming. Not for jokes.

Prospect shows up late on Zoom:
> "Well, what should we — should I forgive you for showing up late? What are we going to do with you Barb? Oh gosh."

Prospect asks "how are you doing today?":
> "Just trying to stay out of trouble — what about you, are you getting in any trouble over there Julie?"

Mechanism: laughter → dopamine → calm nervous system → guard down.

## Verbal Cues — bridge questions conversationally

[8:19] **Trash pattern** (scripted, prospect's brain spots it):
> "Okay cool. Um, gotcha gotcha. Uh, John, I'm curious…"

**Verbal cues that bridge naturally:**
- "Ah, really?"
- "But when did you do that?"
- "Oh, really? But what happened then?"

Use these RIGHT before the next question — feels like best-friend conversation, not interrogation.

## Verbal Pacing — the MLK example

[8:50]
> "Have you heard of Martin Luther King's 'I Have a Dream' speech? How did that start? Did he just talk really fast — or did he say 'I have a dream… that one day… all men are cre—' See how he paces that out? If he said it really fast, you would have never heard of that speech because it would have never triggered any emotions."

## Verbal Pausing — pause placement controls focus

[10:54] **Same words. Different pause placement. Different brain focus.**

### Version A — pause before "that":

> "Can I ask why *(pause)* THAT is so important to you now though?"

→ Brain focuses on **that** (whatever the thing was) → self-justification of value.

### Version B — pause before "now":

> "Can I ask why that is so important to you *(pause)* NOW though?"

→ Brain focuses on **now** → urgency / timing.

Same words. Identical sentence. Different prospect thought:

> "Now is right here, but they're more focused on THAT because they verbal-paused right here and right here. Now their brain thinks: why is it important right NOW — which builds urgency in the sale."

## Plug-in version

> "So can I ask why higher quality leads is so important to you *(pause)* now though?"

(Marketing agency example — plug in whatever the prospect said they want.)

## Key takeaways / CZ kontext

- **The "off the record" concerned-tone escalation** is a Czech-perfect line: *"Můžu se vás na něco zeptat, Honzo? Mezi námi — co vás od toho vlastně drží?"* Czech "mezi námi" is even more disarming than English "off the record."
- **Pause placement controls focus** — this technique translates directly to Czech. *"Můžu se zeptat, proč je *(pauza)* TOHLE pro vás teď tak důležitý?"* vs. *"Můžu se zeptat, proč je tohle pro vás *(pauza)* TEĎ tak důležitý?"*
- **Verbal cues bridging** — "Aha, fakt?" / "A kdy jste to dělal?" / "A co se stalo pak?" — Czech equivalents land naturally; replace the dreaded "Tak jasný, Jasný, Honzo…" scripted bridge.
- **Playful tone for late-arrivers** in CZ: *"No tak co s tebou uděláme, Honzo, mám ti to odpustit?"* (use "tě" / "Honzo" only if first-name basis).
