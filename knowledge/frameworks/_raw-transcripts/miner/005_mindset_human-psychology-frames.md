# Body

## Frames = belief systems

[0:02] Every human walks in with a frame — a belief system formed from ages 4–13 by parents, religion, friends, today TikTok influencers. The frame about salespeople: "How much is this going to cost?"

[0:32] Why? Billboards, social media ads, TV, radio — society installed it. Every prospect has built defensive frames. The moment they feel sold to → guard up.

## Other typical buyer frames

[1:33] Common belief systems that produce predictable objections at the close:
- "I need to talk with my spouse before I decide."
- "I always do more research before I decide."
- "I need to talk to other decision makers."

## D-frame → Reframe

[1:33] **D-frame** = take them out of their current belief system / way of thinking.
**Reframe** = install a new belief system / new way of thinking.

[2:06] If their frame is "How much will this cost?" and you don't D-frame it, the close will produce: "Too expensive." / "Price is too high." / "Can you give me a discount?" / "I can't afford it." — even if they could.

> "It's not like the money doesn't exist. They're already spending that money on something else that in their mind is more of a priority than your solution."

Your job = shift their priority so YOUR solution becomes where they want to ship the money.

## 3 jobs every question must do

[3:10] To get into the top 1%, every question must do **one of three things:**

### Job 1 — Seed doubt

Trigger doubt about what they currently have or don't have. If they don't doubt their current state, they won't be open to your solution.

### Job 2 — Prevent objections

[3:42] **Demo — spouse objection prevention.** Selling marketing services to a real estate agent (Dan) who gets 2 listings/month but wants 5:

> "So how does your spouse feel about you only getting 2 listings a month?"
> — "Oh, she doesn't like it."
> "Does she want you to get up to 5 listings a month so you guys can make more money?"
> — "For sure she would want that."

→ Spouse objection at the close is now **pre-empted**. She's already on record (via him) as wanting the outcome.

### Job 3 — Let them see an end out (future pacing)

[4:45] Your questions must let them see what life looks like once the problems are gone.

## Future state — logical then emotional

[5:47] **Network marketing example.** After feeling current pain (not enough money, no time with family):

> "So let's say we're able to come in, we help you start the business, and we help you get up to [whatever they said they wanted to make — repeat their number]. What would you do with the extra money coming in?"

→ Logical answers: "I'd buy a new car. Help my grandma into a better nursing home. Help my uncle Joe build his house."

Then drop into concern tone:

> "Okay — you being able to do those things, what would that do for you personally?"

→ Emotional answer: "Oh my gosh, it would relieve so much stress."

> "Stress?" *(echo + concern)* → emotions fly.

## Closing techniques are a myth

[6:49] Forget "101 closing techniques."

> "If you use an option close — 'do you want the red one or the blue one? Wednesday at 4 or Thursday at 5?' — that's not when the prospect decides. If they say 'I want the red one' — they had already decided before you asked. The question didn't magically trigger the buying decision."

What triggers the decision = the human-psychology work earlier in the call.

## Key takeaways / CZ kontext

- The **3 jobs framework** is a fantastic mental check for every question you write into a sales script: does this question do at least one of the three?
- **Spouse-objection prevention** translates perfectly to small-business owners with co-owner partners or wives in the business: "Jak se na to dívá manželka / parťák? Souhlasil by s tím, že si vyděláte víc?"
- **Future pacing logical → emotional** is the highest-leverage question stack: "Co byste s těma penězma navíc dělal?" → "A co by to s vámi osobně udělalo?"
- **Closing technique trap** — don't optimize for closing tricks. Optimize the middle of the call (D-frame, seed doubt, future pace), and the close happens by itself.
