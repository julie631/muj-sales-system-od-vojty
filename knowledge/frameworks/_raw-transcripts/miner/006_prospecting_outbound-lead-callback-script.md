# Body

## The wrong way (default outbound script)

[1:36] Default opener:
> "Hey Annie, it's it's James Miller with XYZ company. I saw you requested some information about our blank services. Is this a good time to talk?"

→ Works for the 5–10% layowns. The other 90% trigger sales resistance:
- "I remember doing that, I'm really busy right now."
- "Can you call me back later?"

## Three lines NEVER to say

[5:14] **1. "Is this a good time to talk?"** → Most prospects answer "No, it's not."

**2. "Do you have 2 minutes?"** → They don't believe you. Lowers status.

**3. "How are you doing today?"** → Average prospect doesn't believe you genuinely care. Triggers resistance — every salesperson asks this.

## Why the rebuttal fight makes it worse

[10:53] When prospect pushes back: "I'm not interested." Default sales response:
> "Well, I called because with our XYZ solution we can help you cut costs by X and increase revenue by Y. In fact, we've been ranked the #1 service company three years in a row…"

Every salesperson claims #1. Triggers more resistance.

## The framework — 3 NEPQ Connection Questions

### Connection Question 1

[13:30] Right when they answer:

> "Yeah, is John there? Yeah John, hey, it's it's James. James Miller with XYZ company. You asked us to call you back — looks like you responded to the ad this morning on YouTube about [END RESULT]."

**Why this works:**
- **Familiar tone** with the double-name pause ("James. James Miller.") → triggers "do I know this person?" → buys time.
- **Verbal pauses** between sentences → slows down, doesn't sound like a salesperson dumping words.
- **"You asked us to call you back"** → anchors that they raised their hand. Hard to push back.
- **End result of what you sell, NOT the product itself.**

### End-result positioning examples

[16:39] Examples per industry — say the END RESULT, not the product:

- Marketing agency: "…about getting like a higher quality lead so you could grow the business" (end result = growing the business)
- Solar: "…about maybe locking in your rate and lowering your bill" (end result = lower bill)
- Financial services: "…about maybe getting a higher rate of return so you could retire earlier"
- Business consulting: "…about maybe looking at some help with putting in operational systems to really grow the business"

> "Imagine what you sell — what would be the end result of what you sell? Not just the thing you sell. That's the difference."

[18:12] **Why?** Default prospect frame: "How much is this going to cost?" The end-result framing **D-frames** them from cost-thinking → results-thinking.

### Connection Question 2

[20:50] Second question:

> "So when you saw the ad where they were going over X and Y and Z, what was it that they were talking about that caused you to, you know, want to want to look into this further?"

Note:
- **Neutral language** ("caused you to look into this further") — not "really caused you to want to talk to me" (too assumptive too early).
- Nobody objects to "look into this further" — it's safe.
- They start telling you why → also telling themselves.

### Connection Question 3 (Status Frame)

[23:27] Third question is a **status frame**:

> "Yeah, the first part of this call, it's it's pretty basic. It's really more for us to find out kind of what you're using now for [X] and the results you're getting from that, compared to maybe where you're wanting those to be, to kind of see what that gap looks like. And then towards the end of the call, if you feel like, hey, this might be what you're looking for, you know, we can talk about possible next steps. Would that help you?"

**Why this works:**

[24:29] **Downplay** ("the first part is pretty basic") → positions you as expert, not eager rep. Experts don't need to sell.

**Hand movements visually create the gap** even if they can't see you — body language affects tonality.

> "Your facial expressions are the remote control to your tonality."

**Status posture** = calm, collected, confident. Not too excited (sounds desperate). Not too timid (loses authority). Right in the middle.

**Neutral language at the end:**
- "might be what you're looking for"
- "possible next steps"

Nobody objects to "possible." Avoids the premature "I'm just looking, gathering information" pushback.

### Bonus Connection Question 4 — Reverse the qualification frame

[26:33] After they've engaged:

> "Just so I understand — what were you hoping could possibly happen by possibly working with us?"

**The critical reframe:**
> "Most salespeople qualify TO the prospect. But the prospect has the problems. So I flip the script — what were you hoping could happen? Now the prospect starts qualifying to YOU."

Answers you'll get: "I'm hoping we can get more leads / increase coverage / reduce rate hikes / get a better vehicle."

[28:04] Note the neutral hedges ("possibly working with us") — never triggers objection.

## Key takeaways / CZ kontext

- **End-result framing:** e.g. for a language school selling corporate courses: "…o tom, jak dostat vaše obchodníky do stavu, kdy zvládnou jednání se zahraničním klientem bez tlumočníka" (not "…o jazykovém kurzu").
- **"You asked us to call you back"** = perfect when calling back FB Lead Form / IG / website leads in CZ.
- **Status frame Q3** = direct CZ translation: "První část toho hovoru je vlastně docela jednoduchá, jde mi hlavně o to si zmapovat, co teď používáte, jaké výsledky to dělá, a kam byste se chtěl/a dostat — pak na konci, pokud byste cítil, že to dává smysl, pobavíme se o případných dalších krocích. Dává to smysl?"
- **"What were you hoping could happen?"** = best single question to use after a demo: "Co jste si od toho dnešního hovoru sliboval?"
