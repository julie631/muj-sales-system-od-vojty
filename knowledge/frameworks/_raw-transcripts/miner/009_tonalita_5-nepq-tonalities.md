# Body

[0:03] **"Tonality is 10 times more important than the words you use."**

There are 5 tones to master. Each has a specific context. Some have subcategories.

## Tone 1 — Curious Tone

[0:34] **Context:** Triggering genuine curiosity in your questions. Pattern interrupt vs. the monotone telemarketer the prospect expects.

**Bad (monotone, triggers guard up):**
> "Hi, is this John? Hey John, it's Jeremy Miner with XYZ company. Hey, the reason why I'm calling you is…"

**Good (curious tone):**
> "So, it looks like you booked on the calendar about, I guess, maybe getting higher qualified leads to really grow the business, right?"

Or:
> "What were you hoping could… I guess possibly happen by working with us?"

The "I guess" / "possibly" softens it — implies genuine wondering, not pitching.

### Subcategory — Familiar tone

[7:16] For cold-callers / outbound. Sound like the prospect should already know you.

> "Yeah, hey, it's Jeremy. Jeremy Miner with XYZ company. You asked us to call you back about the 2026 blue Audi, right?"

**Mechanism:** prospect's brain spends 5–10 seconds processing "do I know this person?" → pattern interrupt → guard down → they won't be rude in the opening seconds.

> "Have you ever got a call: 'Hey, it's Amy. Amy Smith — how are you?' What do you do? 'Uh, yeah, I'm good — how are you?' Your brain is thinking 'Who is Amy Smith?' All that's doing is buying time."

## Tone 2 — Confused Tone

[1:36] **Context:** Opening prospect emotionally, especially after they use an emotional word.

NOT confused like "I don't know how the product works" — confused like *I don't understand what you mean*.

**Example:**

> Prospect: "XYZ problem is just causes so much stress in our department."
> You: *(confused + concerned)* "Hold on — how how do you mean by stress?"

**Mechanism:** "He didn't understand what I meant. I need to explain that better." → prospect emotionally opens up.

[2:39] If you ask the same question with just curious tone:
> "Oh, uh, what did you mean by stress?"
> → "Well, it just sometimes gets stressful."
> (Surface answer — no emotional opening.)

## Tone 3 — Challenging Tone

[3:10] **Context:** ¾ of the way through the call, AFTER you've built the gap, future-paced, and they feel urgency. NEVER in the first few minutes — triggers defensiveness.

**Example (full challenge):**
> "So what happens if you don't do anything about this? *(harder)* And this actually gets worse?"

**Example (soft challenge):**
> "What if this doesn't change?"

### Subcategory — Skeptical tone

[9:21]
> "Oh, so how long have they had you actually do that for?"
> "Oh, so they're still making you do that manually? What's caused them not to actually automate that, so you don't have to spend four hours a day doing that manually?"

> "I'm not being skeptical toward THEM. I'm being skeptical toward what they're being FORCED to do."

→ Seeds doubt: there's a better way out there.

## Tone 4 — Concerned Tone (the tone that shows empathy)

[3:41] **Context:** Most important tone for trust-building. Used selectively (not every question — would be awkward).

**Examples:**

Life insurance, when they mention a death in family:
> "Oh, what actually happened? *(soft, concerned)*"
> "Oh wow, you seem so young. What actually happened to your mom?"

Sales-stall escalation:
> "Can I — can I ask you something, John? *(prospect: 'Sure.')* And this is just between you and I, you know, off the record. What's really holding you back?"

**Mechanism:** the prospect feels you genuinely care for them, not just trying to sell.

> "Is selling something you do TO people or FOR people? It's FOR people — because if we don't master selling, they don't buy from us, their problems don't get solved, and they don't get where they want."

## Tone 5 — Playful Tone

[6:15] **Context:** Strategically used to disarm. NOT to crack jokes or be the class clown.

**Example:**
> Prospect: "How are you doing today?"
> You: "Oh, you know — just trying to stay out of trouble over here. What about you? Are you getting into any trouble?"
> → Prospect laughs → guard down.

**Mechanism:** Laughter releases **dopamine**. Dopamine calms the nervous system. Calm nervous system → prospect is open, not guarded.

## Recap — 5 tonalities + their subcategories

| Tone | Use when | Subcategory |
|---|---|---|
| **Curious** | Opening questions, ad-callback intros | Familiar (cold calls) |
| **Confused** | Echoing emotional word ("Stress?") | — |
| **Challenging** | After gap is built (¾ in), consequence Qs | Skeptical (seeding doubt) |
| **Concerned** | Empathy / trust moments, "what's holding you back?" | — |
| **Playful** | Disarming small talk, "doom and gloom" question | — |

## Key takeaways / CZ kontext

- **CZ adaptation: low** — these tones work in Czech, and Czech is arguably MORE tone-sensitive than English because Czech has fewer politeness hedges.
- **Familiar tone** for CZ cold call: "Honzo, ahoj — tady {tvoje jméno}. {Tvoje jméno a příjmení}. Co ses ptal o té službě, co děláme pro účetní kanceláře..." — verbal pause sells it.
- **Confused tone** for "frustruje vás to?" → "Frustruje? Jak to myslíte?" — works perfectly.
- **Playful tone** is the most under-used tone in CZ sales — Czech prospects are reserved, dopamine release breaks the ice fast.
- **Concerned tone** — Czech business owners (owner-operators especially) respond strongly because few CZ salespeople ever sound like they actually care.
- **Skeptical tone toward their current vendor**, not toward them, is the gold-plated trust-building move: "Vážně vás nutí každý rok zvedat ceny? A nemůžou to nějak automatizovat?"
