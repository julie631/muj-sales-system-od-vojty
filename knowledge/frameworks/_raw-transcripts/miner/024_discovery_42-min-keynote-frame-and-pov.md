# Body

## Setup — 30-second elevator pitches

[0:34] Miner pulls 4 reps from audience. Each gives their 30-second pitch (solar, home improvement, financial, roofing). All sound the same to a prospect.

> "In your prospect's mind, do they know all the differences between what you do and your competitors? No. So in their mind, you all kind of sound the same."

## POV — Perception Of Value

[2:40]
> "Write down POV. POV stands for the **Perception of the Value**."

> "Sales people say 'I gotta show them more value.' The problem is all your competitors are showing them the same value. So it's how the prospect PERCEIVES that value that separates you from closing or not."

> "You ever seen products that are really really good but hardly anybody's buying them? On the flip side — products that are really really bad but they sell like crazy? What's the difference? Those companies know how to FRAME the offer. The perception of the value far higher."

## The Family Picture Frame analogy

[3:42]
> "Picture two identical family photos. You have to buy two frames. One is gold, immaculate. The other is from a flea market — cobwebs, dirty, beat up. Same exact picture. Which one are you choosing? The one that's framed better."

> "It's how you FRAME what you're doing that determines if they buy or not."

## Fear of change = root cause of every objection

[8:23]
> "The brain was never designed for you to thrive. The brain was designed for you to SURVIVE. So anytime there's something new, unknown, or change — the brain plays tricks. Tries to convince us not to do something even though we know we should."

[8:54]
> "Every prospect that does not buy comes down to the core of every human being having a **fear of change**. Every objection — too expensive, we don't need it, talk to my spouse, think it over, push it down the road, not the right time, more research, not interested, already have it — every single one comes down to the root cause: fear of change."

## "Did they plan the objection out before you got there?"

[10:27]
> "When the prospect closes down or gives an objection — did they plan that out before you got there? Or was it something YOU said, asked, your tone — that triggered their guard up? Once we understand this, what does it give us? Now we know how to PREVENT it."

## NEPQ Identity Frame (the closed-off other guy)

[12:32] After 2–3 connection questions:

> "Hey John — happy we're connecting. You know, it's it's so interesting. I just came from this house three or four blocks away. You know, the type. So closed off, one-word answers, thought they knew everything there was to know. And we all know people like that — that just comes from fear. We all know people like that who can't open up and tell somebody their problems. It's like — how did they ever get where they want to go in life? You know what I mean? So what were you hoping could happen by possibly going solar?"

**Mechanism:** Builds the prospect into the identity of someone who doesn't want to be the closed-off-one-word-answer guy. Now they're committed to being open.

[13:36]
> "What if I did this — *(rushed, no tone)* 'just talking with this guy three or four months ago, the type, closed off, not interested, those people never succeed — you know what I mean?' — that's NOT an identity frame. That's what happens when you're a dabbler."

## Live role-play — life insurance lead callback

[18:18] Rep tries the wrong way:
> Rep: "So I guess for you, just what kind of makes this important?"
> Miner-as-prospect: "I just never had the time. I'm in really good health. My dad lived till 96."
> Rep: "I'm always looking for, you know, I know I need to do it eventually."

Miner's reframe — if you find out they have a $50k work policy:
> "So help me understand — you've already got this $50,000 work policy. *(confused-skeptical-concerned tone)* What's causing you to feel like that might not be enough?"

[23:27]
> "Now what does the prospect do? 'Well, yeah, I got the policy, but…' and he tells me why it's not enough. More importantly — who does he tell? **HIMSELF.**"

> "If I said it monotone — 'so you've got this $50,000 policy, what's causing you to feel it might not be enough man?' — 'I don't know, I'm just always looking.' Same words. It's HOW I say the words."

## Why "problems" (plural)

[24:32]
> "Reason number one — what are the prospect's REAL problems? Notice I didn't say 'problem.' I said 'problems'."

> "Most of the time prospect tells you a problem and you go 'let me show you how to solve that.' How big is that gap? Maybe here. What if I didn't take the bait? 'Yeah, that's a big problem. That's the biggest reason companies come to us for sure. What else do you feel like might be going on?' I want to find MULTIPLE problems."

## 100% love anchor + concerned tone

[26:38]
> "Are you 100% in love with the returns you've been getting?"
> "Well, I wouldn't say 100%."
> "Not 100%? What don't you like?"

[27:10] Tone matters:
> "If I said it monotone — 'do you 100% like the thing?' — 'yeah, pretty good.' Curious tone vs concerned tone separates."

> "Concerned tone triggers doubt — *I know something you might not know*. Curious tone triggers *he's genuinely curious*."

## "Fairly decent" / "not too bad" — let tonality plant doubt

[30:18]
> "I mean, you've been with XYZ company the last 12 years. They're *fairly decent*. *(slight skepticism in tone)* I mean, they're not too bad. *(more skepticism)* What's causing you to feel like you might want to look at someone else?"

> "I don't want to say 'they're a great company' — reinforces their frame to stay. I don't want to say 'they suck' — they know I'm trying to sell them. So *fairly decent* / *not too bad* — in the middle, but my tone shifts and seeds doubt."

## 72/28 stat update

[30:48]
> "Updated study: 72% of you making the sale is your tonality — how you say it, how you ask the question — and your body language. About 28% is the words. Words still matter."

## Why "making you pay" not "how much are you paying"

[32:21]
> "Solar reps default: 'oh the bill's really high.' Defensive mechanism — they know you're selling. So I downplay: 'I know a lot of the year it's pretty low, but what have they been making you pay lately?'"

> "*Making you pay* — does anybody like to be made or forced to do anything? I'm shifting them to not like the company that's been forcing them to pay."

> "But if I said 'how much are you paying?' — does nothing. No doubt. Lots of objections."

## Objections are PREVENTED, not handled

[33:22]
> "You ever heard sales trainers say 'the more objections they give you, the more interested they are'? How does that make sense? What about all the layowns you got that gave you no objections — they seemed pretty easy to sell. The more objections you get, the LESS likely they'll buy because they're triggered. Their guard's up. I'd rather PREVENT the objection."

## The Comparative Frame (live role-play with "it's too expensive")

[37:01] **For home organization service ($2,500/day × 4 days = $10k):**

> "In reality, if you really thought about it — which is costing you MORE? Is it costing you more to like get the funding together, you know, we come in, we organize your entire house, you're saving about 6 hours every weekend so you can focus on work — or is it more expensive to do nothing at all, the clothes are all over the place, you're having to spend your entire weekend working? *(concerned tone)* In reality, which is costing you more?"

> "Shift down to concerned tone at the end. Why? Because that would really suck to have to spend the whole weekend."

## The Intention Frame (loop after the comparative)

[41:08] Right after the comparative frame:

> "How much do your services cost?"
> "$2,500 a day."
> "Four days? $10,000. Okay."
>
> "So *(back to comparative)*… in reality which is more expensive? *(then intention)* Because I'm sure it's not your real intention just to keep the $10,000 in your bank account for the rest of your life and hope and pray it just stays there forever with all the inflation. I'm sure your real intention is to never have to work on the weekends again in your life. So what's going to put you in the best position where you can save that time and be with your family and have somebody else do all that work for you?"

[41:43] **Business coaching version:**
> "Is it more expensive to get your funding together — we come in with our internal team, build out your systems, you scale to $10 million this month, or in the next year — or is it more expensive to do nothing, stay with the same systems, never end up scaling?"
> "I'm sure it's not your real intention to keep the $76,000 in your bank account and hope it stays there forever, especially with inflation. I'm sure your real intention is to really grow the business where you never have to worry about money again. What's going to put you in the best position?"

## Key takeaways / CZ kontext

- **POV / Family Picture Frame** = highest-leverage mental model for your positioning. CZ: *"Stejný produkt, jiný rámeček = úplně jiná hodnota."*
- **Fear of change = root cause** — treat every objection as fear-of-change in disguise. CZ: *"Není to o penězích. Je to o tom, že to znamená změnu."*
- **"Did they plan that objection out before you got there?"** — flip your diagnosis of failed calls from "the prospect was bad" to "what did I do upstream that triggered the guard?"
- **NEPQ Identity Frame** for outreach: *"Honzo, věřil bys to — ráno jsem mluvila s jedním majitelem, znáš ten typ, totálně uzavřený, jednoslovný odpovědi, myslel si, že už ví všechno. A já si vždycky říkám — jak se ti takoví majitelé chtějí někam dostat, když ani neumějí říct, co je vlastně trápí? Co tě dnes vlastně vedlo k tomu si o tom zavolat?"*
- **The Comparative Frame for "it's too expensive"** is CZ-perfect: *"V reálu — co tě stojí víc? Sehnat těch {tvoje cena} a mít daně z krku, nebo neudělat nic a trávit stejně každý březen víkendy nad doklady navždycky?"*
- **The Intention Frame** as a loop, e.g. for a cleaning company: *"Tvůj skutečný záměr přece není mít tu padesátku v bance navěky a doufat, že ti tam zůstane. Tvůj skutečný záměr je mít tolik stálých smluv, aby ses už nemusel bát léta. Co tě dostane do tý nejlepší pozice na to?"*
- **"Fairly decent" + tone-as-doubt** in CZ: *"Ta agentura, co vám teď dělá marketing — ujde to. Není to nic strašnýho. Ale co tě vede k tomu, že to možná není úplně to nejlepší řešení pro to, co potřebuješ?"*
