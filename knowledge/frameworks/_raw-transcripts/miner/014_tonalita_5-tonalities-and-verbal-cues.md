# Body

## The 5 Tonalities — quick reference

[0:34] **1. Curious tone** — sounds like you actually want to know.

> "So walk me through — what are you guys doing now to really build out systems to scale the company?" (curious)

vs. monotone: "Walk me through Jim, what type of operations are you building out to scale the company?" (telemarketer → fight or flight)

[2:09] **2. Confused tone** — *I don't understand what YOU mean*.

> "Oh, how how do you mean by stressful?"

→ Prospect's subconscious: "He didn't get it. I need to explain better."

## The Challenge → Concern pivot (inside one question)

[3:41] Used at ½–¾ of the call. NEVER early.

**Setup:** prospect's used car is breaking down, they need a new one, afraid they'll lose their job.

**Consequence question (one sentence, two tones):**

> "So what happens if you don't do anything about this — your car keeps breaking down? *(CHALLENGING)* What's going to happen to your job at that point? *(CONCERNED)*"

[4:43]
> "Notice I started with a challenging tone, and then voila — what did I end with? A concern tone."

Mechanism:
- Challenge raises emotion → "you have to defend yourself on why you have to change"
- Concern lowers their guard → "you're empathetic for the consequences"
- Trust goes UP, not down.

## Concerned tone for stuck prospects

[5:15]
> "Can I ask you something, Sally? *(prospect: 'Sure go ahead Jeremy.')* Um, and this is off the record — what's really holding you back from moving forward?"

(Lowered tone = soft tone = tone that shows empathy.)

## Playful tone

[5:48] Used to disarm. Not to crack jokes.

Late prospect on Zoom:
> "Well, what should we — should I forgive you for showing up late? What are we going to do with you Barb? Oh gosh."

Prospect asks "how are you doing today?":
> "Just trying to stay out of trouble — what about you, are you getting in any trouble over there Julie?"

→ Dopamine release → calm nervous system → guard down.

## Verbal Cues — bridge naturally

[7:56] **Trash bridges:**
> "Okay cool. Um, gotcha gotcha. Uh, John, I'm curious…"

Prospect's brain spots the script.

**Verbal cues that bridge:**
- "Ah, really?"
- "But when did you do that?"
- "Oh really? But what happened then?"

Use right before the next question — sounds like conversation, not interrogation.

## Verbal Pacing — MLK reference

[8:58]
> "Have you ever heard of Martin Luther King's 'I Have a Dream' speech? How did that start — did he just talk really fast, or did he say 'I have a dream… that one day… all men are cre—' See how he paces that out? If he said it really fast, you would have never heard of that speech because it would have never triggered emotions."

## Verbal Pausing — pause placement controls focus

[10:23] **Same words. Different brain focus.**

### Pause before "that":
> "Can I ask why *(pause)* THAT is so important to you now though?"
→ Brain focuses on **that thing** (whatever they want) → self-justification.

### Pause before "now":
> "Can I ask why that is so important to you *(pause)* NOW though?"
→ Brain focuses on **now** → urgency.

> "Now is right here — but they're more focused on THAT because of where I verbal-paused. Now their brain thinks: why is it important right NOW — which builds urgency in the sale."

Plug in industry: "Can I ask why higher quality leads is so important to you now though?" (marketing agency)

## Key takeaways / CZ kontext

- **Challenge → Concern pivot** = single most powerful tonal move in CZ sales. *"A co se stane, když s tím nic neuděláte — ten kalendář vám zůstává poloprázdný? *(challenge)* Co se s tím vaším salonem stane za rok? *(concerned)*"*
- **Verbal cues bridging** — Czech: *"Aha, fakt?"* / *"A kdy se to stalo?"* / *"Vážně? A co jste s tím udělal pak?"* — natural, conversational.
- **Pause placement** in CZ: *"Můžu se zeptat, proč je *(pauza)* TOHLE pro vás teď tak důležitý?"* (focus on issue) vs. *"Můžu se zeptat, proč je tohle pro vás *(pauza)* TEĎ tak důležitý?"* (focus on urgency).
- **"Off the record" concerned tone** = *"Mezi námi…"* — instant Czech disarmament.
