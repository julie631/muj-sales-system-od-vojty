# Body

## What NOT to do

[0:02]
> "Don't sound like everyone else who cold calls. Your prospect's brain picks up on the pattern. Monotone → 'salesperson, sell me something.' Too enthusiastic → recognized. Timid / nervous → triggers fight-or-flight."

[1:05]
> "Don't say 'Hi my name is, the reason why I called…' Don't say 'this is a cold call, give me 2 minutes…' Their brain recognizes that pattern."

## The framework

[2:36] **Step 1 — Familiar tone opener with double-name pause:**

> "Hey John, it's it's Jeremy. Jeremy Miner with XYZ company."

> "I'm not going to say 'Hi, is this John?' — that implies I'm a stranger. I'm going right in like I already know them. It's *it's* Jeremy. *Jeremy* *Jeremy Miner.* Interrupting the pattern in her brain."

## Step 2 — "I'm holding a copy of your [X]"

[3:39]
> "I'm holding a copy of your, uh, blank. I was wondering if you could possibly um help me out for a moment." *(slightly confused tone at the end)*

### Industry examples

**Real estate (expired listings):**
> "I'm holding a copy of the MLS from three months ago with your home at 55 Willow Lane. I was wondering if you could possibly help me out for a moment."

**Marketing agency (calling business):**
> "I'm on page six of your VSL where you were talking about XYZ. I was wondering if you could help me out for a moment." → "Which VSL? What are you talking about?"

**Hospital SaaS:**
> "I'm looking at some online reviews from some of your previous doctors who used to work there. I was wondering if you could help me out for a moment."

**Distressed property investor:**
> "I'm holding a copy of your property taxes on your home at 55 Willow Lane in Savannah. I was wondering if you could help me out for a moment."

### How to design YOUR opener

[10:27]
> "Go to ChatGPT, plug in: 'what could you hold in your hand that would have something to do with problems you solve or consequences you prevent, that would trigger the prospect to be curious?' Like 'oh what do you mean by my property taxes?' They can't say 'not interested.'"

## Step 3 — Sell the exit

[5:13] When they ask "Sure, what's going on?":

> "Well, I'm not even sure if it makes sense for us to talk yet."

Or:
> "Well, I'm not even sure if you're the right person I should be talking to. I called to see who would be responsible in your company for looking at any possible hidden gaps in your [X] that could be causing you guys to [Y]."

## Step 4 — "Responsible" — hard to deflect

[5:43]
> "Why do I say 'responsible'? Because it's really hard for them to say 'Oh, I'm not responsible for that, that's Jim.' Very hard."

## Step 5 — "Hidden gaps + might be / possible"

[6:13]
> "I called to see who would be responsible for looking at any **possible** hidden gaps in [X] that could be **causing** you to [Y]. Who should I be talking to about that?"

> "Why 'possible'? I'm being neutral. I have zero trust in 30 seconds — can't be assumptive."
> "Why 'hidden gaps'? Triggers curiosity — 'what do you mean by hidden gaps?'"

### Industry example
> "Any possible hidden gaps in the way your firm is doing your taxes that could be causing you guys to be forced to overpay the IRS. Who should I be talking to about that?"

## Step 6 — "Who should I be talking to?"

[7:21]
> "At the end I'm saying 'who should I be talking to about that?' — like I'm confused."

## Full SaaS demo — Hospital AI dictation

[8:24]
> "John, it's it's Jeremy. Jeremy Miller with XYZ company. I'm looking at some online reviews from some of your previous doctors that used to work there, and I was wondering if you could possibly help me out for a moment."

When they ask:
> "Well, and I'm not even sure who I should be talking to. I called to see who would be the person over at the hospital responsible for helping the doctors reduce their time they're **being made to** dictate their notes — that might be preventing them from seeing more patients, where the hospital like loses billable hours. Who should I be talking to about something like that?"

[9:26]
> "I'm repeating back the NEGATIVE consequence — billable-hours loss. That's the consequence. Doctors don't see as many patients = less revenue."

## Full distressed-property demo

[10:58]
> "Sally, it's it's Jeremy. Uh Jeremy Miller. I'm holding a copy of your property taxes on your home there at 55 Willow Lane in Savannah. I was wondering if you could possibly help me out for a moment."

> "Sure, what's going on?"

> "Well, I'm not even sure if it makes sense for us to talk. I represent a group of buyers — they're purchasing like six or seven different properties in that four-block area where your Willow Lane home is. After they looked at your property taxes, they had me reach out to see if you guys would be against, you know, maybe having a brief conversation about maybe getting an offer on that property. Would you be opposed to having a brief conversation on that?"

## The "opposed not open" close

[12:01]
> "Hard for them to be against something. What's a safe word a prospect wants to use on a cold call? The word **NO**."

> "If you say 'would you be open?' — yes is a dangerous word for them. They're more comfortable saying NO. And NO in this context is really a YES for you."

> "'Would you be opposed?' — hard to say 'yes, I'd be opposed.'"

## Why slow down / verbal pause / neutral language

[12:32]
> "Slowing down — 'I represent a group of buyers… they're purchasing… like five or six different properties…' — I'm pacing. I'm biased. After they went through your tax records, they had us reach out to see if you'd be, hey, I don't know, maybe against possibly having a brief conversation about maybe getting an offer on the home."

> "Lots of neutralizers — 'hey I don't know,' 'maybe,' 'possibly.' Nobody objects to neutral."

## Key takeaways / CZ kontext

- **Double-name familiar pause** in CZ: *"Honzo, ahoj — tady {tvoje jméno}. {Tvoje jméno a příjmení}."* Works identically.
- **"I'm holding a copy of"** for local businesses: *"Honzo, mám tady otevřený váš Google Maps profil — koukám na ty recenze za poslední 3 měsíce. Chtěla jsem se na něco rychle zeptat..."* — instant curiosity.
- **"Possible hidden gaps"** in CZ: *"...volám, abych zjistila, kdo je u vás zodpovědnej za to, řešit nějaké možné skryté mezery v tom, jak máte nastavené zálohy a odpisy, který by mohly způsobovat, že na daních platíte víc, než musíte. Koho bych s tím měla oslovit?"*
- **"Made to / forced"** — strong CZ: *"nutí vás, abyste…"* / *"musíte..."* — taps into Czech resentment of being controlled.
- **"Would you be opposed?"** Czech soft equivalent: *"Bylo by úplně mimo si o tom 5 minut popovídat?"* — works because saying "no" feels safer, and "no" here = "yes, let's talk."
- **ChatGPT trick** — you can plug in, e.g.: *"What document could an accounting-firm rep hold in their hand on a cold call to a small construction company that would trigger curiosity about tax problems they're losing money on?"* Build 10 industry-specific openers this way.
- **CZ adaptation: medium** — the basic structure is universal; the "opposed not open" close needs softening into Czech politeness conventions.
