# 4 Lessons from Building 5 8-Figure Sales Teams

## Lesson 1: How to Identify Stars vs Superstars

### 3 traits to hire

1. **Specialized Knowledge** (Naval) — knowledge you can only get from DOING the thing. Sales experience in YOUR specific sales process (transactional / enterprise / relational / D2D — they're different).
   - Common mistake: hire a savage real estate agent for your marketing agency. Different sales process = bombs.
2. **Industry Acumen** — can they speak your market's language?
   - Car-sales veteran selling hormonal women's clinic → bombs (can't relate).
   - Take existing clients with some sales experience and train them up on sales = better in some cases.
3. **Buy-In** — into mission/vision/values. Non-negotiable.

### Superstar vs Star

- **Superstar** = all 3 traits. Rare.
- **Star** = buy-in + ONE of (specialized knowledge OR industry acumen).
  - Decision: which can you train internally better?

Cole's own hiring: business acumen across 100+ industries → hires people who GET BUSINESS, then trains sales.

Jasmine (Cole's client, not natural salesperson, real-estate expert) → hires sales veterans, trains them on real estate.

## Lesson 2: Recruiting Stars at Volume

### The Bench Principle

> "If you have 5 reps and 2-3 people on the bench you're dying to put in but don't have marketing capacity for yet — you're going to be SO much less tolerant of bad performance. You won't settle."

### Group Interview Hack

10 people in Zoom gallery, 1 per week, you + EA.
- 1/week × 4 weeks = 40 candidates/month.
- Comparison bias: studs vs duds visible in 2 minutes.
- Look for: who takes initiative, originality vs copying, timid vs aggressive.
- End: DM the 2-3 you want to stay. Roll into 1-on-1s immediately.

Works for: salespeople, coaches, setters. NOT for CFO/developers.

## Lesson 3: Effective Interviewing

### Situational vs Competency questions

- **Situational** (commonly taught): "If I hired you as a salesperson, how would you build your pipeline in 90 days?" — they can fake/prepare.
- **Competency-based** (what Cole prefers): asks what they've ALREADY DONE.
  - Past behavior predicts future performance.
  - Can't fake answers.

### Killer competency questions (use 2 per interview, no more)

> "Tell me about the last 2 deals you won via extreme proactive sales effort. Walk me through those deals end to end step by step. What happened?"

> "Were there times at XYZ company where lead flow wasn't up to par? Tell me about the last time. Specifically, what actions did you take that month to still hit your numbers despite bad lead flow?"

> "What was your worst performing month? How many units did you sell? How does that compare to your best month? So that was 50% of what you're truly capable of. What did you learn? And in [next month], how did you apply those lessons to bounce back?"

> "You can't escape the truth with these questions. We're trying to get to the TRUTH, not just a good interview."

## Lesson 4: Create a Prodigy (1-3 rep stage)

### The mistake Cole used to make

When team had 5 reps (3 crushing, 2 mediocre):
- Cole would spend most public meeting time reviewing WORST reps' calls.
- Result:
  - Negative meeting energy
  - Investing time in people most likely to churn
  - Top performers tuning out (irrelevant to their level)
  - Top performers leaving (felt no growth)

### The fix

In PRIVATE: spend equal time with all.
In PUBLIC (group meetings, off-sites, call reviews): focus on the **TOP 1-3 reps**.

- Reinforce wins with praise → better meeting energy
- When top rep makes a mistake, it's relevant to everyone
- Reps learn through MODELING — gold standard reps to copy
- You're building your next sales manager

## Lesson 5: Promoting Top Rep Without Losing Production

### The trap: Player-Coach

> "If your best candidate for manager is also your best salesperson, there's no way around production hurting short-term. The big mistake: making them a player-coach to have your cake and eat it too. You now have 2 jobs, not 1."

Why fails:
- Their comp is still mostly tied to production → they prioritize production over coaching
- They lead by example → if production lags, they're chasing production not coaching

### How to keep their production while making them manager

**Manager's job description**: 80% of time = call reviews + watching team. Boring but necessary.

3 tactics to recover ~half their production:

#### Tactic 1: Manager Callbacks

When manager spots a deal that SHOULD'VE closed:
> "Hey, so sorry — I know your conversation with [Rep] went like this. I think they missed one of these key components. Do you have 5 minutes? I want to correct the conversation because we can totally help you, but there are a few things he missed that I think would be super insightful."

10 min later → $20K close. 5-10 of these/week → 4-8 extra deals/month.

#### Tactic 2: Manager as Bait (follow-up calls)

Rep stuck on follow-up 1 → 2. Book FU2 on MANAGER's calendar.
- New face = increased show rate
- Manager often says same thing as rep — but trust goes up
- Closes by virtue of "real company, multiple people."

#### Tactic 3: Manager Case-Study Cherry-Picks

3-5 calls/week, manager DMs a rep:
> "Hey, [rep] isn't feeling good today. I'll take care of you."

Conduct the call. Effects:
- 4-8 extra deals/month
- AMAZING case study for daily call review (modeling)
- Builds manager's authority with team

Malá firma (1–2 lidi) potřebuje sales team, až počet klientů přeroste to, co uzavřeš sama.

Tvůj první hire by měl být:
- **Industry acumen** PRIMARY — někdo, kdo se vyzná v oboru tvých klientů (např. když prodáváš fyzioterapeutickým praxím, bývalý fyzioterapeut, který chce upgradovat).
- Pak ho trénuješ na sales skills.

NEBO opačně — najmout SALES veterána z B2B service prodeje a trénovat ho na tvou niche.

Group interview week-1 + competency questions:
- "Vyprávěj mi o posledních 2 dealech, který jsi zavřel. Step by step."
- "Měl jsi měsíc, kdy ti chyběly leady. Co konkrétně jsi udělal, abys přesto trefil čísla?"

Manager promotion v budoucnu: dokud sama prodáváš a zároveň učíš ostatní, funguješ jako player-coach. Až budeš pure manager, ne player-coach.
