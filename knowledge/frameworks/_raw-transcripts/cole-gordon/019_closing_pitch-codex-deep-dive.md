# Pitch Codex Deep Dive — How to Pitch Anything in <10 Min

## Where this fits in the call

```
Introduction (rapport + frame) → Information Gathering → Transition → PITCH → Committing Phase → Investment → Objections
```

Pitch = 3 parts:
1. **High-Level Promise** — 1-2 sentences stating END RESULT (hard offer)
2. **Bridge** — 3-4 Pillars (milestones from current → desired)
3. **Delivery** — what they actually get (use a Delivery Map visual)

## Part 1: High-Level Promise

> "1-2 sentences that state the end result whatever you're selling is designed to provide."

### Hard Offer formula

Specific outcome + specific time frame (if you can):
- "Get you to 30% closing ratio in 8 weeks."
- "Four things to recruit and hire a sales team so you can get off the calls and scale revenue to $250k/month."

Don't overthink it — feed back to them in ONE good sentence the desired situation they shared.

### Setup script

> "Cool — so still have that pen and pad? Great. On your pen and pad, write out one through four — one, two, three, four. [WAIT — literally watch them write]. Okay great, well let me preface this by saying everything we do is all customized for the client. For you specifically it's going to be four things. Four things to take you from [current situation] to [desired situation with hard outcome and time frame]."

## Part 2: Bridge (the most important part)

3-4 Pillars (Cole prefers 4) that take prospect Hell Island → Heaven Island.

### Pillar structure

- **Pillars 1-2** = paradigm shifts. Why they failed in past + why this is different.
- **Pillars 3-4** = future pacing. Fix problems that arise AFTER pillars 1-2 work (eliminate fear of success).

### Pillar naming

- Sound proprietary, NOT BS
- Good: "Sales Systems", "Recruiting Pipeline", "Ramp Up", "Pitch Codex", "Productized Offer", "Authority Campaign"
- Bad: "Phase 1: Discovery", "XYZ Blackbox System"

> "Use common sense — your prospect's probably not, unless you have an amazing idea, going to hear a phrase and be like 'okay here's my credit card.' The benefit of being on a sales call vs writing a VSL is you have dialogue to explain unique/different/superior."

### Per-pillar formula

```
Look, when it comes to [desired outcome], here's the problem.

Most people out there [incorrect behavior]
→ which means [consequence 1]
→ which means [consequence 2]
→ ultimately they [end consequence].

So instead what we do is [specific method / proprietary name]
→ which allows you to [benefit 1]
→ which means [benefit 2]
→ ultimately [benefit of benefit].

Does that make sense?
Cool — what are your thoughts on that?
```

**Us-vs-Them + New-vs-Old frames**. Problem-link + Benefit-link.

> "The pitch should be the simultaneous explanation of why everything they tried in the past has failed and why this is going to be different."

### Tie-downs every 45-90 seconds

Per pillar (especially 1-2): "Does that make sense? Cool — what are your thoughts on that?"

- "Does that make sense?" elicits YES (low bar)
- "What are your thoughts on that?" — direct command, actually elicits FEEDBACK

For pillars 3-4: lighter tie-downs to avoid redundancy.

Add proof per pillar 1-2:
> "For example, when we implemented this in my company in December 2021, [result]." OR "For example, we had a client in your industry experiencing [problem], and once they implemented this, they got [result]."

### Example: Sales Systems pillar (live demo)

> "Look, so to give you context, there's two parts to winning sales team. (1) The people — your sales talent. (2) The vehicle — the system the talent is operating within. Both equally important.
>
> Think about winning a race. You can put the best Formula One driver in the world in a Honda. You think he still going to come in last place? You have to have a great driver AND a great car.
>
> The problem with most business owners: they get super-focused on the talent alone — which look, we're a talent business, so it IS important, but it's only 50%. The issue is a great sales vehicle is what allows you to attract the great talent to begin with. Best salespeople want the best offers, best companies, where they can make the most money and have the most support.
>
> Despite that, many companies neglect this — which prevents them from getting good reps and causes those reps to churn.
>
> So with all that in mind, the question becomes: what makes a great sales vehicle? Well, [list deliverables briefly]. That's the first thing we'll make sure is dialed with you. Then we'll move on to number two. Does that make sense? Cool — what questions do you have on that, if anything?"

## Part 3: Delivery Map

Don't just list deliverables boringly. Build a visual workflow:

```
ONBOARDING CALL → SETUP PHASE → IMPLEMENTATION PHASE 1 → IMPLEMENTATION PHASE 2 → ONGOING SUPPORT
   ↓                ↓                 ↓                       ↓                       ↓
[items]          [items]           [items]                  [items]                 [items]
```

Walk them through:
> "So here's the flow of how we work together. You'll have an onboarding call, on which these things happen. Then we move to this, simultaneously to this. At this stage these things happen. At this stage these things happen…"

Visual dimensionalizes the deliverables vs boring list.

## Part 4: Committing Phase

After pitch:
> "Great. So that sums up everything. Now in terms of the delivery map and those four pillars specifically, **what questions do you have?**"

### Critical wording

> "If you say 'any questions on that' they'll say no. If you say 'what questions do you have on that specifically' it's a direct command — they will literally give you questions most times. Profound difference. I've tested it myself."

### Handling questions — Clarify → Answer → Lead

For every question:

**Clarify** (find the real question):
- Prospect: "Is this group coaching?"
- You: "There's elements of what we do that are community-based, but is there a specific reason why you're asking?"
- Prospect reveals: "I did group coaching before, 150 people on call, no support."
- Now you know real concern.

**Answer** (short, 1-2 sentences max):
> "Got it. Well our support is from our community — 24h response time. Group calls 5x/week, max 15 people, submit question beforehand, on and off in 5 minutes. Opposed to what you went through, see how that would work better?"

**Lead** (direct conversation forward):
> "Great question. What else? What other questions do you have?"

CRITICAL: never sit silent after answering. You stay leading, not them.

### Why "answer short"

> "A lot of salespeople just start talking and talking, overselling. It's like dude, just answer the question, stop selling. That breaks trust. Very common."

## Part 5: Temp Check (Before Price)

After all questions answered:
> "Got it — so how do you feel about the process specifically? Money and everything aside, do you feel like these four things are what you need to [outcome]?"

**Look for assertive tonality**:
- Green: "Oh dude definitely, this is 100% what we need, just a matter of how we make it work."
- Yellow: "Yeah, I think so" → call it out: "I think so? Tell me what's really going on."

> "I want them 100% sold like 'dude I want to do this, sounds perfect' BEFORE I even drop the price. Once I drop the price, the hardest thing to build is certainty in the thesis."

## Part 6: The Transition (before the pitch itself)

End discovery:
> "Look, I don't feel like I have any more questions. Is there anything else you feel like we haven't covered that I need to know? [No] Okay great, well [Name], based on what you told me we can definitely help."

Then drop a THOUGHT (case study, personal story, or valuable insight on the WHAT not the HOW):
> "For instance, you actually remind me a lot about a client X who came in, did Y, and in Z time frame got [result]."

Then pass control:
> "Gotcha — so that said, where do you want to go from here? I can pretty much walk you through exactly how we would work together A to Z if you would like, but where do you want to go?"

**Question + Suggestion + Question pattern** — they always take the suggestion, but the dialogue feels like they're in control.

## Part 7: Slide or Google Doc Adaptation

Two ways to deliver pitch visually:

### Google Doc method

Type pillars live during the call. Prospect sees notes building. Highly customizable.

### Slide deck (8 slides)

For each pillar: ONE slide showing the tee-up + deliverable. Not webinar style — script-transferred to visuals.

## CZ adaptation poznámka

- Funguje 1:1.
- Hard offer ("30% close rate in 8 weeks") perfekt pro CZ — Češi mají rádi konkrétní čísla a deadline.
- Per-pillar formula "Most people / so what we do instead" → "Většina firem v tvém oboru řeší X — což vede k Y. My to děláme jinak — místo toho A, což ti přinese B."
- "What questions do you have" → "Co tě k tomu napadá?" (direct, elicit-mode)
- Clarify → Answer → Lead → "Jen abych ti odpověděla správně — proč se zeptáš?" / krátká odpověď / "Co dalšího tě napadá?"

## Pro tebe — aplikace

### Pitch Codex šablona

Ukázka na účetní kanceláři, která vede účetnictví malým firmám — strukturu převeď na svou nabídku (`knowledge/moje-nabidka.md`).

**Hard offer**:
> "Pro tebe konkrétně to budou 4 věci. 4 věci, jak mít do měsíce účetnictví a daně pod kontrolou tak, abys doklady neřešil víc než hodinu měsíčně — bez nového softwaru a bez práce navíc."

**Pillar 1 (paradigm shift)**: Průběžná kontrola nad nárazovým doháněním
> "Většina firem řeší účetnictví jednou ročně před přiznáním, s krabicí dokladů. Výsledek: stres, penále za pozdní podání, žádný přehled o cashflow. My to děláme jinak — doklady zpracováváme průběžně každý měsíc, takže víš, kde jsi, dřív, než přijde problém. Výsledek u klientů: {tvoje case study s čísly}."

**Pillar 2 (paradigm shift)**: Napojení místo přepisování
> "Většina účetních chce papíry v šanonu. Výsledek: přepisování, chyby, dohledávání. My napojíme tvou banku a fakturační systém, takže doklady chodí samy. Každé hlášení = z aktuálních dat."

**Pillar 3 (future pacing)**: Nastaveno jednou = klid
> "Jednou nastavené, běží to bez tvé práce. Jednou za kvartál to spolu projdeme. Žádné každodenní dohledávání."

**Pillar 4 (future pacing)**: Přehled + úspora + termíny
> "Když to běží: víš, kolik si odložit na daně, neplatíš zbytečné penále a máš čísla pro rozhodování. Tři pilíře hodnoty, kvůli kterým to klienti mají."

**Delivery Map**: Úvodní hovor → 14 dní nastavení → první měsíční uzávěrka → měsíční reporting.
