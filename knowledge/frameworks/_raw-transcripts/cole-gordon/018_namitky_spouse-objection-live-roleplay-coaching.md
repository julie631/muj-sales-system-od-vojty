# Overcoming the "Spouse" Objection (Live Roleplay)

## Part 1: PREVENTION in Discovery

[00:00] Student demoed discovery on a prospect (roofing salesman wanting career change, married, wife not very involved in his finances).

Cole's key prevention probe:
> "What does your wife think about this? Does she agree you're not making what you're worth?"

[06:40] Why: surfaces spouse perspective EARLY. Most spouses have already said "you're undervalued" — that's gold ammo for later.

### The killer follow-up probe Cole forgot to demo

> "Is your wife in sales too?"

Why "is your wife in sales" vs "what does your wife do":
- "What does she do" → defensive ("why do you care?")
- "Is your wife in sales" → assumes/lighter → naturally tells you their household income context

If yes → household has higher income potential. If she's a stay-at-home mom → may be a tougher financial sell (relevant for closing).

## Part 2: Three Tie-Downs (conversational, not forced)

[28:04] Student notices Cole tied down 3 times before getting into close:

> "I noticed that — really wanted to do it three times. And you notice how I did it: NOT WEIRD. Very conversational. But also very direct."

Important: most reps stack tie-downs in a way that sounds forced. Cole's version is natural dialogue:

1. "Take the money, take the wife out for a second — I want to know about you. Do you really want to do this?" → Yes.
2. "Are you positive?" → Yes (with some hesitation).
3. "Those things aside, you're 100% in?" → Yes.

Watching for:
- **Eyes**: drift = trouble, locked = certainty.
- **Cadence**: faster = certainty, slower/halting = uncertainty.

## Part 3: Order matters — Uncertainty → Money → Spouse

[31:00] Critical sequence:
> "I always handle uncertainty first. Then in B2C usually money. Then spouse. You can't handle the spouse until you handle the money — because if he goes to his wife and she says 'how are you going to pay for that?' and he says 'I don't know,' it's done."

For B2B with business partner where finances aren't the issue, you can sometimes open-loop payment plans to next call and just handle the partner now. But for spouse + tight finances → fix money first, ARM the prospect.

## Part 4: Financing as a Tool

[22:00] Cole's logic for offering financing:
> "If he goes back to his wife with 'it's 10K up front,' she'll veto. If he goes back with 'it's $800/month, refundable deposit if you hate it' — he can sell that."

The financing offer:
> "Look, obviously I don't think going into 5K negative is the best move. At the same time, I have no doubt the skills we teach will make you more money immediately. So I don't think doing nothing is best either. We have in-house financing options up to 12 months — breaks payment to $800/month. Based on your 5K cash on hand + 1-2K incoming monthly + 1.5K available credit, you can 100% make that work. If I was willing to set that up — I know you have to approve with your wife — would you be willing to move forward with that given she approves?"

If yes → "Okay. What do you think she's going to say?"

## Part 5: Refundable Deposit Step-Across

[25:31] Cole's signature close for spouse cases:
> "Look, let's put down $800 — refundable deposit, your first payment. You go talk to her. Meantime I'll set up another call between us for the financing, which'll take care of the other payments coming in monthly. If for any reason she throws your clothes out of the yard and she's like 'I hate you' — we'll just refund. It's just a deposit. But for YOU, what's important is you can draw the line in the sand, step across it, and make a commitment that the last 5 years of doing a job you don't like and being undervalued aren't going to be the next 5 years. If we can do that for you, are you cool doing that right now?"

Key elements:
- "Refundable deposit" (no risk)
- "Draw the line in the sand" (identity frame)
- "Last 5 years ≠ next 5 years" (cost belief)
- Exchange close

## Part 6: Spouse Two-Versions Coaching

[36:50] Cole's wimpy-vs-confident contrast script (already in video 15) — but here he demos with refundable deposit added:

> "You could go running with your tail between your legs going to her and being like 'I think I found this thing, $800/month, I think it'll help me get a new position.' OR you could go to her and be like: 'Look, I put down a deposit. It's 100% refundable so if you hate this I'll just refund it. But here's the deal — I've been doing this 5 years, I'm undervalued, underpaid, I don't get to spend time with our kids because I'm knocking on doors all day. My body hurts. So I'm paying this firm — who placed 1,100 reps last year for the top companies in the industry — to help me find a new role and train me to be better in my current role. This is a monthly payment, refundable deposit. I want to lock in my spot. But I want to make sure you'll support me on this. What do you think?'"

## Part 7: Timing Objection (Vacation Reframe)

[40:30] Student says: "I want to start in 2.5 weeks — going to Thailand."

Cole's reframe:
> "Can I tell you something crazy? I actually think the timing is PERFECT.
>
> Number 1: You're about to go on vacation. That gives you space. You'll get into observer mode, rethink career, life, revisit goals. The fact you can make a commitment NOW, draw the line in the sand, step across, and then during 2.5 weeks start rethinking these goals — very powerful.
>
> Number 2: Your flight is 16 hours there and back. Plus your daily workouts. Plus morning beach walks. You have a LOT of time to knock out the training that you wouldn't have when working full-time. Drink a couple coffees on that flight — you can probably be DONE with the training by end of those two flights. Let alone listening during workouts, walks, runs.
>
> So you're in the perfect time. But here's what I'll do for you. I'll set your official start date for Oct 27th — and **I'll buy you an extra 2.5 weeks in the program for free** because you won't be utilizing our resources during vacation. Happy to extend your program time. Then Oct 27th you come back, training already knocked out, meet your coach, way ahead of everyone, hit the ground running. So you cannot lose. If I was willing to do that for you, would you be willing to move forward right now?"

Note structure:
- Reframe the obstacle as ADVANTAGE
- Add free 30 days as EXCHANGE
- Close

## Part 8: Humor (Grant Cardone Theory)

[47:30] When student replies with "I just want to relax on vacation":
> Cole: "Oh come on, man. You can sleep on the flight? You can sleep 16 hours? Come on, dude — even 3 hours on 2x speed. You're going to be bored on that flight."

[48:00]
> "I closed a guy yesterday who wanted to renew in 2 weeks. I literally went 'ah dog come on, dude please.' He was like 'all right, I'll just do it now.' That's literally how I handled it."

**Grant Cardone theory**:
> "Some people poo on Grant Cardone but I think he has fun with it — he knows he's closing you, but he's having a good time. When you can relieve the pressure a little and use rapport, you can pressure HARDER, and it doesn't even feel like pressure. Requires you to be less rigid, more free-flowing, confident."

## CZ adaptation poznámka

- Triple tie-down + conversational delivery 1:1, ale tonalita méně agresivní.
- "Is your wife in sales too" → "Je tvoje partnerka taky v tomhle oboru?" funguje.
- Refundable deposit step-across — pokud nemáš deposit model, použij garanci: "tady je {tvoje garance} — uděláme commit dnes, a když výsledky nepřijdou, garance platí."
- Vacation reframe → "Hele, neber to jako překážku. Tu dovolenou bys měl využít k rozhodnutí. Pak v září jedeme od prvního dne."
- Humor v ČR jemnější — "no tak hele, no tak…" víc než "come on dude". Ale princip funguje 1:1.

## Pro tebe — aplikace

Když prospect řekne "musím to probrat s manželkou / společníkem":

1. **TROJITÉ tie-down conversational**:
   - "Tak hele — manželka stranou, ty osobně do toho jdeš?"
   - "Jasné. A peníze taky stranou — pořád jdeš na 100%?"
   - "Cool. Takže jediná věc, co tě brzdí, je ten rozhovor s ní?"

2. **Sekvence**: uncertainty (jdeš do toho?) → money (jak to vidíš s cashflow?) → spouse (kdy s ní mluvíš?).

3. **Best terms BECAUSE she needs ammo**: "Look, abys to s ní probral, dám ti nejlepší podmínky. {polovina setupu} teď + {polovina setupu} za 30 dní, plus {tvoje garance}."

4. **Wimpy vs Confident frame**: "Hele, jdou tam dvě cesty: buď přijdeš k ní jako 'ahoj zlato, našel jsem na internetu nějakou firmu, stojí to {tvoje cena}, co myslíš' — anebo přijdeš tak, že jí řekneš proč jsme to spolu rozhodli, že máš {tvoje garance}, že {tvoje case study s čísly}. Která verze ji víc přesvědčí?"

5. **Risk reversal step-across**: "Co kdybychom dnes podepsali — máš {tvoje garance}, a když výsledky nepřijdou, garance platí? Tím draw the line — že tenhle rok nebude jako ten loňský."
