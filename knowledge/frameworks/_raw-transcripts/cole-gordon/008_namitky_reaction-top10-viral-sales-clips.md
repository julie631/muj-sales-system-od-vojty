# I Reacted to The Internet's Most Popular Sales Advice

## Recurring meta-pattern: CATEGORIZE first

Cole's #1 critique across all 10 clips: most people skip the categorization step. "Too expensive" / "let me think" / "I need to talk to spouse" can mean:
- **Uncertainty / value** — they don't think it's worth it / will work
- **Financial logistic** — they want it, just need cash flow help
- **Spouse respect** vs **spouse decision** — politeness vs. real authority

You handle each ENTIRELY differently.

## Clip 1 — "$7,500 too expensive" handler (McDonald's vs steakhouse metaphor)

[00:00] Other person's approach: metaphor (steakhouse vs. McDonald's), then trial close payment plan.

Cole's improvement:
> "When you say that, do you mean you don't see the value in what we're doing? So $7,500 is too much for this value? OR do you really want to do it, it's just finances standing in the way — so if you could make it work financially, you'd 100% move forward?"

Then per branch:
- **Financial**: double tie-down → "Finances aside, you're 100% in?" → open wallet probe → break into split AS EXCHANGE.
- **Value**: tie back to thesis → "Based on what we covered, are you 100% certain this is what you need to get to [outcome]?"

## Clip 2 — Prospect wants 24h to think ($4.8k offer)

[04:35] Other person: bullies prospect ("Elon Musk doesn't ask his dad", "you need to take risk").

Cole's critique: works in theory but unrealistic. Better approach:
> "Before we get into logistics, let's answer the most important question. Pricing everything aside — do you think based on what we covered, this is 100% what you need to get to [outcome]?"

Then if hedging:
> "Like, do you 100% believe this is exactly what you need to get this outcome? It's got to hit them in the face. Can't be weak."

If they hem and haw: "Okay, tell me what's really going on." → isolate the concern.

## Clip 3 — $14k drop, "expensive compared to what?"

[07:38] Other person's framework: "If 5K for dinner expensive, 5K for car cheap. So does it really matter what we pay if what we get in return is everything we've ever wanted?" → then asks "what's the hesitation?" → identity close: "Are you committed to being the person who gets the results? **That's what determines it.**"

Cole loves the identity flip. His own approach:
> "Gosh, makes total sense. I get that you're not 100% sure it's going to work, which is why it seems like a big price for your level of certainty right now. So let me ask — what exactly, based on what we covered, is keeping you from being less than 100% certain this is going to work for you?"

Then narrow → if can't articulate → identity close: "Who do you want to be?"

## Clip 4 — Cold call "fully booked" (construction)

[12:43] Other person was persistent, kept asking questions, found the angle (more high-quality customers).

Cole's preferred direct opener:
> "John, hey John — this is Cole. Look, I'm calling a little bit out of the blue but just give me 30 seconds — I'll tell you why I'm calling, then you can tell me if it makes sense to keep talking after that. Is that fair enough? Okay — like I said, we're from closers.io. You're probably familiar with brands like [big case study 1], [big case study 2], [big case study 3]. Essentially we help them with one of these three things: [list]. Which of those, if any, are you actively working on in your business right now?"

Key: drop big-name social proof EARLY + offer 3-problem menu.

## Clip 5 — Spouse objection (security cameras)

[16:14] Other person uses gendered framing ("women just want to know you got a good deal"), reverse-risk close.

Cole's structure — first ISOLATE:
> "No problem, totally get it. So just that aside though — if it was just you, just your decision, would you 100% do it? Okay, so investment's not an issue. And you 100% think it's going to work?"

If 100% certain themselves, then categorize spouse type:
> "When you go talk to her — have you basically already decided you're doing this, you're just letting her know out of respect? OR are you guys going to sit across the dinner table and collectively make the decision for the household?"

- **Respect** → risk-reversal close (give incentive to start now, if spouse hates it remove for free)
- **Decision** → deeper probe: "What do you think her biggest concern would be? What about price?" → equip THIS prospect to convert the absent spouse + 24h follow-up

## Clip 6 — "Too expensive" panic vs disagreement

[20:20] Other person's advice: disagree gently — "Completely get it. It's a significant investment. When you say too expensive, do you mean it's not worth it OR you just don't have budget right now?"

Cole approves the categorize step but disagrees with "What would need to change for this to feel worth it?" — too transactional for non-corp.

Cole's preferred:
- Financial branch: double tie-down → "Finances aside, you're 100% in?" → open wallet → payment split as exchange.
- Value branch: "What's keeping you be from being less than 100% certain this is exactly what you need to get to [outcome]?"

## Clip 7 — Jeremy Miner identity close

[22:21] Jeremy: "Who do you want to become?" → "More successful." → "Whose choice is that?" → relentless Socratic dialogue, tonality shifts to match the prospect.

Cole's review: total beast. Notice he asks questions almost exclusively. Identity close is one of Cole's favorites too.

## Clip 8 — "Think about it" — joke + categorize

[23:52] Other person: "How long do you need? You sure that's enough? Take 2 years if you want." → "Need to think about program or price?" → if program great, just price, then walk through value mental rehearsal → payment plan offer.

Cole's preferred:
> "No problem. Just so I know, and we can answer the most important question — investment and everything aside, do you feel like this is actually what you need to get the result?"

→ double tie-down → financial branch (open wallet, split as exchange) OR uncertainty branch (isolate specific concern).

## Clip 9 — Direct opener for inbound discovery call

[26:55] Other person dives into business questions too fast, energy flat.

Cole's preferred:
> "Hey, how's it going? [point out something funny about background]. How's your week been? Good busy or bad busy? Working on cool stuff? Okay cool — ready to jump in? So, like I know you reached out about X. I'm prepared to go into all the details. The thing about what we do, which we'll get into later, is everything is customized. So to make this call as proficient as possible, let me get the lowdown on your business — what you're doing, sales process, acquisition, sales team. Then I can share with you the parts of what we do that are relevant to our conversation specifically. Is that fair?"

Frame: leadership energy, take control, customize promise.

## CZ adaptation poznámka

- Categorize step (value vs financial) v ČR ještě silnější — Češi neradi otevírají peněženku bez důvodu.
- Identity close ("kým chceš být") v ČR funguje, ale jemněji. Ne "do you want to be the person who lets fear guide them" — spíš "chceš nechat tu nejistotu rozhodnout, nebo se rozhodnout sám?"
- "Hit them in the face" tonalita → CZ "rovně a jasně, ale ne agresivně".
- Direct cold opener s case studies funguje 1:1.
- Spouse respect vs decision split funguje 1:1 — relevant u manželských párů, které podnikají spolu.

## Pro tebe — aplikace

Pro "to je drahé" ({tvoje cena}):
1. **Categorize**: "Když říkáš drahé — myslíš, že tohle za to nestojí (nemyslíš, že ti to vrátí investici), NEBO máš pocit, že tě tahle částka teď v cashflow zaskočí?"
2. **Value branch**: "Tak hele, peníze stranou — myslíš, že tohle ti vyřeší {problém z discovery} líp, než to děláš teď?" → po YES dvojitý tie-down → pak řešit cenu.
3. **Financial branch**: "Aha, takže pokud by ti to cashflow vyšlo, jdeš do toho?" → open wallet ("kolik máš na účtu, kolik dělá obrat za 30 dní") → payment split jako EXCHANGE ("rozdělím {jednorázová cena} na 2 platby, ale dnes podepisuješ").

Identity close (když vyhořívá): "Hele, otázka není 'jestli to funguje' — {tvoje case study s čísly}. Otázka je, jestli chceš zůstat na úrovni, kde jsi, nebo si dát ten next step."
