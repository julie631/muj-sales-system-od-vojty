# Prospects Ghosting Your Follow-Up Call? Watch This

## Foundational caveat

These tactics only work if the BASICS are already right:
- Good rapport + energy
- Proper discovery (clear gap)
- All 7 beliefs covered
- Pitch built simultaneous explanation of why past failed + why this is different
- Prospect 100% SOLD on the idea/plan (not yet paying)

> "Closers with the highest follow-up show rate are almost always the best closers on the team."

## Strategy 1: Open Loops

After scheduling follow-up, drop teases:

> "Awesome, let's get that call booked for tomorrow. [Schedule]. Okay, hey one last thing — I'll go through 2 specific case studies of clients in your industry tomorrow. Exactly where we started with them, what we implemented, and what their ROI was and in what time frame. Plus I'll talk about some payment options that'll make it easier to break up."

Three open loops here:
1. 2 specific industry case studies
2. Payment plan options (even if no financial objection — gives reason to show)
3. ROI model

For Enterprise/complex sales: also "I'll prepare a financial ROI model for you."

### Why this works

> "As long as you still have information to provide, you're still going to be in control of the sales process. Once you've given away all the information, you really have no value as a salesperson. You have no leverage… well, what I really mean is: the value you have to the prospect is information, clarity, and leadership. Once you've given that all away, there's no reason for them to show up."

## Strategy 2: Reframe the Follow-Up

[06:38] Critical reframe:
> "Don't even feel like you have to come to this next call with a decision. All I want you to do is come to this call with questions. Then we'll cover your questions and the rest of the information that I have, and we can see what's the best decision for you — whether that's a now thing or a later thing. I'm good either way."

### Why this is counter-intuitive but works

Default mode: if you give prospect NO reason to show up other than "come with a decision," then…
- YES = show up
- NO or NOT YET = don't show up
- → 50%+ will cancel or just ghost

Cole's logic:
> "If their answer is not a yes or even not yet, more likely a lot of times they'll cancel or delay. And then now you're chasing."

By REMOVING the decision frame + GIVING reasons to show (open loops), you flip:
- YES = show up
- Curious = show up (because open loops are valuable to them)
- Even if hesitant = show up (because no pressure to decide)

## Full script (combined)

> "Awesome, let's get that call booked for tomorrow.
>
> [Schedule the time]
>
> Hey, one last thing I'll go through with you tomorrow:
> 1. Two specific case studies of clients in your industry — exactly where we started with them, what we implemented, what the ROI was and in what time frame.
> 2. Some payment options that'll make it easier to break up.
>
> So I still have a lot to cover with you, so don't feel like you have to come to this next call with a decision. All I want you to do is come to this call with questions. Then we'll cover your questions and the rest of the information I have, and we can see what's the best decision for you — whether that's a now thing or a later thing. I'm good either way.
>
> Also — you'll get off this call and you'll think of some stuff you wish you would've asked or forgot to ask. Write those down. On the next call we'll cover them along with the case studies and payment terms. Don't worry about making a decision before the call — we haven't even really finished our talk yet."

## Recovering canceled callbacks

[04:04] If they cancel:
> "Hey, just wanted to keep our follow-up — I'd like to go over those payment options I brought up with you. Can we still meet [time]?"

Open-loop reactivates.

## Cole's apparent contradiction (resolves)

In the close itself, Cole presses HARD: "If you wake up tomorrow feeling the same exact way you feel right now, then we're 100% moving forward, right?"

Then he RELAXES at scheduling: "Okay great, you don't have to come to the call with a decision."

> "I have to press them like that in the close because I need to see if they're actually sold. But then as we get to scheduling the follow-up, I reel it back and give them permission — 'I don't even feel like you have all the information yet, so let's cover your questions tomorrow. Then once you have all the info, we can make a decision of now or later. No worries either way.'"

## CZ adaptation poznámka

- "Open loops" → "otevřená témata" / "věci, co probereme příště"
- "Come with questions, not a decision" → "Přijď s otázkama, ne s rozhodnutím. Ještě nemáš všechny info."
- 1:1 funguje. Češi ocení "no pressure" frame ještě víc než Američani.

## Pro tebe — aplikace

Follow-up after first call (when prospect needs to check with partner / wants time):

> "Super, máme to v kalendáři na úterý ve dvě. Co probereme:
> 1. Dva konkrétní case studies ({tvoje case study s čísly}) — kde jsme začali, co jsme implementovali, jaký byl výsledek.
> 2. Možnosti rozdělení platby, pokud by to v cashflow pomohlo.
>
> A ještě jedna věc — fakt nepotřebuju, abys přišel s rozhodnutím. Přijď s otázkama. Ještě jsme neprobrali úplně všechno a chci, abys měl všechny info. Pak se rozhodneme, jestli to jede teď nebo později. Mě je to fuk, jen ať máš jasno."
