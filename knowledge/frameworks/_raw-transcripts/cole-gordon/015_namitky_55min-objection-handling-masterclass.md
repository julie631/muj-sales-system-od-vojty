# 55 Minutes of Objection Handling Masterclass

## Foundation: 3 Objection Buckets

Pyramid:
- **TOP — Uncertainty objections** (think about it, doesn't work, due diligence, no time, tried-it-failed, guarantee, too risky, just nervous)
- **BOTTOM — Support** (spouse, partner, board, team)
- **BOTTOM — Financial** (too expensive, can't afford, structure)

Once uncertainty is cleared, support and financial become LOGISTICS to navigate.

## Universal Pattern (every objection)

1. "No problem" (diffuse)
2. "Just so I can stay organized on my end…" (reason to probe)
3. Tie-down to thesis with 100% language
4. Double tie-down isolating the new objection
5. Handle the actual logistic OR force the hidden concern out
6. Exchange close

## Objection 1: "Let me think about it"

> "No problem. Hey, before we get into that, just so I can stay organized — how do you feel? We covered X, Y, Z. Do you feel like this is what you need to get to [outcome]? Like 100% what you need and this is going to work to get you there?"

→ If yes → "Got it. So just to be clear, when we talk TOMORROW, if you wake up feeling the same exact way you feel right now, we're 100% moving forward and processing payment, right?"

Key technique: **PRESUPPOSED FOLLOW-UP** — anchor "tomorrow" before even asking to schedule. Otherwise prospects default to "same time next week."

## Objection 2: "What if it doesn't work?"

> "I hear you. So just so I can stay organized — what you're saying is despite X case studies and Y client success rate, you're worried you're going to be the small percentage that this doesn't work for?"

Then flip:
> "Can I ask you an honest question? If you come in and do this, are you 100% going to put in the work? And when you run into a problem or challenge (which you will), are you going to raise your hand and reach out for help OR bury your head in the sand and disappear?"

→ "I'll raise my hand."

> "Gotcha. Well look, if we have an X% success rate and you're 100% going to put in the work and you promise me you're going to raise your hand — tell me, why wouldn't this work for you?"

Throws back to them. If they get stuck → label as "a little bit of nerves" → Nerves Close.

## Objection 3: "I need to do research"

Cole's brutal truth: this means YOU messed up the call earlier.

> "No problem at all, happy for you to do that. Just so I can stay organized on my end — what's keeping you from being less than 100% certain that this is going to help you get to X, Y, Z?"

Assume they don't think it'll work → forces them to either give specific concern OR correct ("no, I do think it'll work, just want to research").

If they give specific concern → handle it.
If they truly want research → "Got it. So when you say research, what were you hoping to find out more about specifically?"
→ Reveals due-diligence objection (sub-pattern).
→ If they refuse to articulate → ACCOUNTABILITY CALLOUT:

> "Earlier you said you're 100% certain this is what you need after a little research. But when I ask what specifically you want to find out more about, you say nothing in particular. So let me be clear — I'm totally fine letting you do research, don't need to decide now, I'm also fine if you're not interested at all. But one of my core values is transparency and honesty. So I just can't help but feeling like you're not really interested in this, which is okay. Maybe you want to circle back in 6-12 months. What's really going on?"

Empathetic, not confrontational. Last-ditch.

## Objection 4: "I want to do due diligence" (text-only reference)

After tie-down:
> "So theoretically — if you spoke with one of our clients and they validated everything I told you, you're basically good to go, processing payment?"

Two routes:
- **Connect them**: text-only reference + 48h covertly-framed follow-up (see Cole video 010).
- **Cite policy + redirect to case studies**:
  > "Here's our policy regarding that. We have 100+ people reaching out monthly. It wouldn't be appropriate, professional, or realistic to fill our clients' calendars — especially since they're wealthy business owners charging $1-5k/hr for their time. **That's completely off the table.** But — it's the exact reason we have 500+ case studies, especially ones just like you. What specific type of case study were you looking for?"

Key phrase: "**That's completely off the table.**" Definitive. Then immediately to solution.

## Objection 5: "I don't have time to implement"

Diagnostic logic, not script:
- They came to call to fix THE #1 constraint
- They confirmed our method is THE thing they need
- As business owner, your job = spend time on biggest constraint
- ∴ If not this, what else? If not now, when?

Script:
> "You mentioned the #1 problem is lead-gen and our solution is best path. How many hours/week do you work? I'm not asking you to work more — I'm asking you to redirect those hours into the #1 constraint. Using our solution, this'll take less time than DIY. If you're going to grow your business, why would you not focus on this now? If not now, when?"

### Circumstances vs Vision Close

> "Look — the biggest problem in your business is X. Your biggest bottleneck to solving X is time. But the reason you don't have time is BECAUSE OF X — if you had more X, more cashflow, hire employees, not stuck in business. So the very problem you're on this call to solve IS the exact thing keeping you from solving it. You're in a self-perpetuating loop, stuck.
>
> The real question isn't when's the right time — it's never going to feel like the right time. The real question is — when are you going to stop making decisions based out of current CIRCUMSTANCES and start making them out of a place of VISION? Because if you keep deciding from circumstances, you get more of the same. When you decide from vision, you open to a new world of possibility. So who do you want to be?"

## Objection 6: "I tried something similar, didn't work"

PREVENT in discovery. If it slips through, go back into discovery:

> "What did the previous solution have you doing? What was the METHOD?"

Methods matter. Diagnose 3 possible causes:

> "Generally when people invest and don't get results, it's one of three things: (1) the methodology was ineffective; (2) the methodology was great but support/execution wasn't there; (3) you didn't put in the work. Which one of those three was it, if not a combination?"

→ If method → differentiate your method in pitch.
→ If support → emphasize your execution/support.
→ If didn't work → re-qualify ("are you ready to put in the work?").

### Last-resort: honest conversation

> "Look, can I be honest? I might not tell you what you want to hear, but I'll tell you what you need to hear. I totally understand being apprehensive — I've been burned tons of times, way worse than most. What I've learned is the real question is what lesson you're going to learn. Are you going to learn that one bad experience means never get help again? Or learn that one bad decision is the tuition you pay to make a better, more informed decision next time? So look — let's not make one bad decision sabotage what's going to be a great decision."

## Objection 7: "Is there a guarantee?"

OVERHANDLED objection. First diagnose:

> "We can definitely talk about that. Just curious — is there a reason you're asking that question?"

Two cases:
- **I've been burned** → handle that objection instead (objection 6 framework).
- **Just curious** → handle minimally: "No, we don't, because there's too much out of our control. But great question. What other questions?"

If they're hellbent on guarantee, offer CONDITIONAL guarantee — only to those who ask, add to contract.

Also valid: "I can't guarantee results (illegal to promise). What I CAN guarantee is we give you all the tools you need to get [outcome]. Case studies prove people just like you used them to succeed. What really matters: (1) Are you going to 100% show up and work? (2) Are you going to raise your hand when you need help?"

## Objection 8: "It's too risky" — Identity Close

Almost always B2C. After other handles failed:

> "Can I share what I notice between people who succeed and people who go in circles? Your goal is to make X/year — top 1% of income earners. Why so few people get there? **Fear of risk.** Tons of people SAY they want it but few take the risk. So the real question — do you want to be part of the 99% whose actions never align with their words because they won't take risk? Or part of the 1% who hit the glass wall of fear and break through? Which one do you want to be?"

## Objection 9: "I'm just nervous"

Feed them this label if they're stuck/floundering:
> "Do you just think it's a little bit of nerves?"

Then Nerves Close:
> "Good [pattern interrupt]. The nerves are there for a reason. They're a GOOD thing. There's some weight, some meaning. Nothing ever great was built by somebody who wasn't a little nervous at first. So being nervous is okay, but deep down you should know despite the fear this is right. Earlier when I asked, you said this is what you wanted, right? So who do you want to be — the type who gives into fear or the type who feels fear and does it anyway? Let's get started."

## Objection 10: Spouse/Partner

After tie-down + double tie-down + "Hypothetically if your spouse said 'do whatever you think is best,' would you be 100% moving forward?":

→ Categorize via respect vs decision question:
> "Just for clarity — is this a respect thing where you've made the decision, just letting them know? Or a real collaborative conversation?"

If decision-type → coach them through the conversation:

### Spouse two-version coaching script

> "I want you to talk to your spouse, but I want to remind you there are TWO ways to have this conversation.
>
> **Wimpy version**: 'Hey honey, that business of mine I spent a ton of money on that's only caused stress and ups and downs? Well, I met a guy on the internet who might be able to help and it's like 7 grand. What do you think?'
>
> **Confident version**: 'Hey babe, I know the business hasn't been what I've promised it could be, and I really haven't been committed at the level I should. Despite that, you've been amazing supporting me. I want you to know I'm going to fix that starting now. I've decided and committed to grow this. I found an amazing coach. I'm asking for your emotional support and buy-in on this decision I've already made because this is extremely important to me and I need to do this for us. Want to have a serious conversation between us so we're aligned before I move forward.'
>
> Which guy is going to convince his wife? Obviously the second. Now — if you're not certain, she won't be either, nor should she be, because you're the leader and you're uncertain. So the real question — when you go to her, which of those two are you going to be?"

If "not certain" → loop back to thesis tie-down: "There's something else keeping you. Let's cover that — I need you 100% certain going into that conversation."

## Objection 11: Boss/Board (upward authority)

Refundable deposit hack: ask what their approval threshold is. Get a refundable deposit BELOW that threshold to start them. "Once in motion stays in motion" — almost never refund.

Or: get them to the call together. If CEO going to board, they can usually sell board if they're 100% sold themselves (it's not their money).

## Objection 12: Team (downward authority)

Tricky — teams throw grenades:
- "We don't have bandwidth"
- "We can do this ourselves"
- "I don't think this should be a priority"

Why: team sees solution as attack on how they work. Already busy. Fear outside consultant will fire them.

Frame for prospect:
> "If you believe this is the #1 constraint, your job as CEO is to determine problem, priority, vision, and align everybody. You shouldn't go ASK PERMISSION. If you want their buy-in, fine — but if they say no, are you prepared to tell them 'no guys, we ARE doing this. I'm not asking for permission, I'm asking for buy-in'?"

## Objection 13: Financial (full sequence)

[50:24] Same sequence as video 014 — but with critical add-ons:

- **Cash on hand correction**: "I'm not asking what you can spend right now. I don't even know if you could do this. I'm asking what's in your checking account."
- **Don't ask "what payment can you do?"** — YOU lead. They tell you their numbers, you decide the offer.
- **Offer high first**, then drop. Higher cash collection.
- **Always ask for MORE on better terms**: "If I go to 3-pay instead of 2, will you give me a case study when you get results?"
