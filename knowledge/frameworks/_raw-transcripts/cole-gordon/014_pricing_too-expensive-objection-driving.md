# Handling "It's Too Expensive" Objection (While Driving)

## The full sequence (drill version)

### Step 1: Determine type

True financial = "I want it, finances are standing in the way logistically."
Smokescreen = "$X is too much for the value I think I'll get."

You handle these ENTIRELY differently.

### Step 2: Tie-down to thesis

Prospect after price drop: "Oh, this sounds great but I don't know how I can afford this. Is a lot of money."

> "Hey no problem, we can talk about that. But what I want to know is — do you actually want to do this? Do you 100% believe that what we outlined (X, Y, Z) is what you need to get to [outcome]?"

WHY 100% language: "I'm actually trying to get them to say NO. I'm trying to sus out a specific concern. If I handle the money objection at face value, I won't get the close because the real thing's under the surface — and I'll dish out my cards (payment plan, negotiate terms) and not close anyway."

### Step 3: Double tie-down

> "So finances aside, you're 100% in. Okay."

Same purpose: force out hidden concern OR lock confirmation.

### Step 4: Permission frame

> "Look, no problem at all. Most of our clients do the investment up front, but for certain clients depending on their financial situation, who really want to do it like you but don't know how to make it work, we can break it up. But all of that is customized and depends on what's best for the client. So are you open to having an honest conversation financially, getting everything on the table so we can see if there's a way to make this work now — or at the very least, we can create a game plan for you to work towards in the future?"

Key components:
- Restate desire
- Restate money is only obstacle
- Hint at possibility ("we can break it up")
- Permission ask
- Risk reversal ("game plan in the future")

### Step 5: Open Wallet (3 numbers)

> "Hey, so what's your net incoming cash flow in the next 30 days? After paying bills, what's essentially left over?"

Easy starter. Then the real question:

> "And what's your cash on hand right now? EXACTLY."

If they're vague ("about a thousand"): they're telling you what they can SPEND, not what they have.

> "Well, I'm not asking what you could spend — I'm asking what you actually have access to so we can figure out if there's a pathway to do this now. It's totally possible we can't, but I want to leave you with a game plan to work towards this in the future. Because you really want to do it, right?"

Then go further: checking, savings. Then credit.

> "And what's your access to credit?"

Write all numbers down.

### Step 6: Final temp check

> "And you really want to do this, right?"

Every single time prospect says yes. This is the final lock before close.

### Step 7: Finessed payment offer

> "Great. Because look, I don't think the best thing for you if you're struggling financially is to do the investment all the way up front and put yourself in a bad position even if this is the thing you really want to do. I also don't think the best thing given what you told me on this call is for you to do nothing. So what I'd be willing to do for you is break it into X, Y, Z so that you can only come in for [initial down payment]. That way you can get [outcome 1], [outcome 2], [outcome 3], and then when that second payment comes around X days later, **it's almost basically an afterthought.** So if I was willing to do that for you, are you willing to move forward right now?"

Three finesse elements:
1. **Acknowledge their constraint** (don't ignore)
2. **Future-pace** ("by the time second payment comes around, it's an afterthought because you'll have momentum")
3. **EXCHANGE** ("if I'm willing to do that for you, are you willing to move forward right now?") — never giveaway

### Step 8: If resistance → loop with BETTER term + ASK MORE

After ~2 min back-and-forth, you can drop to even better term (3-pay instead of 2-pay). But ASK FOR MORE in exchange:

> "Hey, if I'm willing to do that for you, will you move forward right now AND give me a case study when you get amazing results?"

Better terms = higher value exchange. Don't give freely.

## Detection: smokescreen

If during open wallet they:
- Won't tell you cash on hand
- Get vague repeatedly
- Try to get off the phone
- Ask you to send something

→ It's UNCERTAINTY, not finance. They're not sold. The tie-downs in step 2-3 were weak. Loop back, isolate uncertainty.

## CZ adaptation poznámka

- Sequence funguje 1:1.
- "Honest financial conversation" → "férová konverzace o financích" — Češi tohle frame přijmou.
- "I'm not asking what you can spend, I'm asking what you have access to" → "Neptám se kolik chceš teď utratit. Ptám se, kolik máš k dispozici."
- Payment split + future pacing + exchange — všechno funguje stejně.

## Pro tebe — aplikace

Tvůj drill při "{tvoje cena} je hodně":

1. **Categorize**: "Když říkáš drahé — myslíš, že to za to nestojí, NEBO máš pocit, že tě to cash-flow zaskočí?"
2. **Tie-down**: "Peníze stranou, věříš, že tohle ti pomůže {výsledek, který klient chce}?"
3. **Double**: "Peníze stranou, jdeš do toho na 100%?"
4. **Permission**: "Hele, většina mých klientů to platí jednorázově, ale občas pro klienty jako jsi ty rozdělím na 2 platby. Je to customized. Co kdybychom se přes ty čísla podívali férově?"
5. **Open wallet**: "Za 30 dní co máš čistého z business? Kolik máš teď přesně na business a osobním účtu? Máš dostupný úvěr?"
6. **Temp check**: "A pořád do toho chceš jít, jasné?"
7. **Finessed offer**: "Look, nechci, abys teď zazdil cashflow. Taky nechci, abys nic neudělal. Co kdybychom udělali {polovina setupu} teď + {polovina setupu} za 30 dní? Při tom prvním měsíci budeš mít první výsledky, druhá platba bude jen afterthought. **Když ti to takhle udělám, podepisujeme dnes?**"
