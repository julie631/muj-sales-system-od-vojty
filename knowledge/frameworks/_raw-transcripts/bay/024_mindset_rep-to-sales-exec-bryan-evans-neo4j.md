# From Rep to Sales Exec — Bryan Evans (VP of Americas, Neo4j)

## Background — career stops

[00:59] Bryan's journey:
- Tech recruiting (2009) — bad fit but learned phone skills
- ADP (door-to-door selling payroll to SMB)
- Akamai (6 years inside sales)
- Everbridge (first sales leadership role)
- GoGuardian (VP of Sales)
- Neo4j (VP Americas Sales — present)

## Style learned at ADP — value-first persistence

[04:47]
> "My style was: try to understand if the business owner had a problem that was in any way payroll-related. If yes, I'd be persistent. If I couldn't sense it — I'd just move on."

Bryan's discovery instinct: where's the interest vs. where's this a waste of everyone's time? Honed over 2.5 years of high-volume prospecting.

### East-Coast direct style

> "I didn't try to do the opposite — build a relationship FIRST. That can come across disingenuous. We don't have a relationship yet. Let's not fast-forward."
>
> "Is there a problem here? Is there value I can provide? Is there a reason you would take this meeting where you're going to get something out of it? If yes — great. We've got the foundation for a real relationship."

## First-manager mistakes (the lesson)

[10:21] Bryan promoted from rep to first-time leader at Everbridge. Three mistakes:

**1. Too much "are we cool?" with former peers**

Some peers had interviewed for the same role. Bryan tried to make sure relationships were intact instead of leading.

**2. Pride-driven focus on underperformers**

> "I took it from a prideful place — 'the underperformers, I'm going to get them to club, get them over plan.'"

**3. Top performer feedback (the light bulb)**

Annual review from his top performer:
> "I want to spend more time with Brian than we do. We don't spend enough time in deal coaching, one-on-ones. You'd been a really successful IC here — show me the path. Teach me some new things."

**The realization:**
> "Sometimes you can't turn a chicken into an eagle. Help them develop and coach, sure — but A-players is where you spend the majority of your time. Top performer had 150-200%-to-plan ceiling. THAT's worth your time."

## Bay's matrix — Effort × Impact

[13:12] Bay's project prioritization framework applied to coaching:
- High effort + low impact = working with C player
- Low effort + high impact = +1 hour/week with A player → 200% of target

> "Spending an extra hour or two with the A player is high ROI."

## Taking over a new team — value first, not friendship

[14:11] At Neo4j Bryan took over from a leader with deep long-standing relationships.

His approach:
> "They don't need another friend. They don't need the comedian. They need someone they can trust who can remove obstacles and help them achieve what they want personally and professionally."

What sellers actually want from a new leader:
1. Coach + develop them
2. Help them hit + exceed goals
3. Make money they've never made before
4. **Remove obstacles** (every business has dysfunction)

**Friendship as byproduct, not goal.** Won't happen first week or month. And likely won't happen with someone underperforming.

## One-on-one rhythm (new role)

[17:15] Bryan's process:
- Make 1:1s with AEs a priority
- Send heads-up agenda topics beforehand ("not going to surprise you")
- Anything else they want to bring up — this is the time

**What he's looking for:**
- **Who's bought in?** Are you mentally in this? Are you proud to be here?
- Do you really care about the team, culture, product, customers?
- Written notes that get revisited at Q1 the next year

### Performance vs Culture vs Activity

[16:23] Three things Bryan evaluates:
1. **Activity** — doing the right things daily
2. **Culture** — fit, jumping on board, healthy challenges to status quo
3. **Performance** (binary in sales)

> "If you're a really good culture contributor doing the right activities — I'm confident we'll get your performance there. However, sales is binary. Over a long period without activity → tough conversations. If you don't have it, you're holding them back from being successful elsewhere."

## Sales leader's role in deals — adding REAL value

[21:38] When Bryan joined Neo4j as a leader (never sold there):

> "I asked myself — why would I bring this guy to the meeting? They'd be like, 'Is Bill in trouble? Should we be concerned?' That bothered me."

**Solution:** learn the product + pitch fast enough to provide value.

## Pitch practice sessions (culture win story)

[22:43] Bryan introduced **10-minute "around-the-horn" pitch practice sessions**:
- New people loved it
- Bryan participated alongside them
- **Tenured reps eventually asked: "What is this? Why are we not included?"**

> "That's when I knew we had something. The people on board WANT to win. They want to hone their craft. New leader leaning in, no ego — that took a couple quarters, but culture was strong."

## Why this matters at Neo4j

7 out of 10 meetings = developers, architects, app builders. 3-4/month = CIO/CTO/economic buyer.

**Money question:**
> "In those CIO meetings, are you prepped? On a moment's notice, do you know what to say? What's your 5-minute value prop or 15-minute value prop? How are you going to talk their language so they don't just say 'go back to the developers'?"

## Deal Review (NOT forecast call) — the qualifier

[25:38]
> "Why is this qualified? What's the business problem they're going to PAY to solve that EXECUTIVES care about?"

### The anti-pattern — "science experiments in PoCs"

Neo4j's challenge: graph DB is cool tech. People say yes to meetings to learn.

> "They want to fine-tune queries, build data models, etc. — but if it's not solving a business problem someone with money cares about, it might not be the best use of our time."

**Suss out: is there a real business problem? Are the right people aware? Is there money?**

## Multi-threading playbook

[27:30] Early-stage deals where Bryan gets involved:
- Work with AE / manager to **craft the note Bryan sends to the customer exec**
- Continue to follow up
- Match titles when possible (limited by company size)

Power + influence check, not just title.

## Pro tebe — meta-aplikace (HIGH cz_adaptation_needed)

**Pokud nemáš tým, tato lekce HIT-uje až ve chvíli, kdy najmeš prvního obchodníka.**

### Až najmeš prvního obchodníka

**Mistake #1 to avoid:** Ty a kolega (např. junior obchodník) budete kamarádi. Bryan's bod: friendship přijde NÁSLEDNĚ, ne first. Vést přes:
- Coach mu sales calls (poslouchat, dávat feedback)
- Pomoc s konkrétními deals (vstoupíš do callu s VIP klientem)
- Odstraňování obstacles (např. nastavit přístupy do nástrojů, domluvit podklady s kolegy)

**Mistake #2 to avoid:** Focus na underperformers (= junior obchodník, co nezavírá). Bryan's bod: pokud najmeš 2 obchodníky a 1 zavírá 2× víc, **investuj víc do toho top performera**. "Can't turn chicken into eagle."

### Pitch practice (i bez týmu)

Bryan's 10-min around-the-horn pitch practice **= co můžeš dělat dnes sama:**
- Před každým týdnem si nastavit 10 min sólo pitch session (= role play sama se sebou nebo s kolegou)
- Recording, poslouchat zpětně
- Až najmeš obchodníka, zaveď tuhle praxi od day 1 → kultura

### "Don't lead with friendship" v kontextu klientů (KRITICKÉ)

Bay-Bryan's lekce má direct aplikaci na **vztah s tvými klienty**:

Pokud jsi s klienty často v relativně přátelském režimu, Bay-Bryan's bod aplikovaný:
- Klient nepotřebuje friend, potřebuje **value + obstacle removal**
- Když voláš klientovi, nemělo by to být "ahoj, jak se máš" — mělo by to být "viděla jsem tvoje čísla, mám pro tebe konkrétní insight"
- Pokud zvyšuješ ceny / přidáváš službu — frame jako value, ne jako "kámoš tě poprosím"

### Deal Review framework pro tvoje sales calls

Bryan's "deal review není forecast call" → můžeš dělat sama se sebou (nebo s kolegou):
- 1× týdně 30 min na všechny active leads v pipeline
- Pro každý: "Co je business problem? Koho boli? Kdo má rozpočet? Kdy se hne?"
- → Ne forecast ("nastoupí to v M2?") ale qualification ("Co je tu reálné?")

### "Science experiment in PoC" anti-pattern (tvoje verze)

Bryan's bod: lidi řeknou yes na meetingy = learning. Tvůj ekvivalent:
- Klient řekne "udělejme 30-day pilot zdarma" = "science experiment"
- Pokud děláš pilot, musíš mít **clear business problem** identifikovaný před pilotem (= "co se přesně mění po pilotu")
- Bez toho = ztracený čas

### Pokud zvažuješ roli "VP Sales" ve vlastní firmě

Bay-Bryan's lekce = roadmap. Klíčové insights:
- Activity + culture > výkon v krátkém termínu (pro koho hire-uješ)
- Match titles s klientem (pokud chce mluvit s CEO / majitelem firmy s více pobočkami, jdi ty, ne junior)
- "Remove obstacles" je VP's main job, ne motivace

### Jiná linie (jednotlivci vs větší firmy)

Bryan's bod o "match titles" znamená, že u větší firmy s 10+ lidmi musíš být jako majitelka ty ta, kdo s nimi jedná. Pro jednotlivce (OSVČ) = stačí jeden člověk z tvé strany.
