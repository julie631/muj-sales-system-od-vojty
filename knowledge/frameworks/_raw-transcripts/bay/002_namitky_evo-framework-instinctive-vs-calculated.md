# Objection Handling — 15-Minute Mini Course

## The core problem

[00:00] Prospects lie. Always have. It's easier to lie than to be blunt about not wanting to talk to you. Most buyers aren't blunt New Yorkers — they'll give you an easy excuse.

So when someone says "not interested" or "send me an email," that's not what they mean. They're not objecting to you or your product — they're objecting to **being cold called**.

Most calls don't make it past the first 60 seconds because reps **accept the excuses**. You don't need to overpower every objection — but you need to recognize that in the first 30–60 seconds, the objection isn't real.

The second area reps get stuck: **legitimate objections** — "using a competitor," "no budget," "just signed with XYZ." Reps don't have a great talk track here either.

## Key Point 1 — Pattern recognition (2 buckets)

[02:14] Cold calling is predictable. Two categories:

**Instinctive objections (75%+):**
- "Not interested"
- "I'm busy"
- "How'd you get my number?"
- "Send me an email"
- "We're all set"

→ They need **acknowledgement**. They're objecting to the interruption, not the offer.

**Calculated objections (later in call, more logic):**
- "We already have a solution"
- "No budget"
- "Not the right person"
- "Bad timing"
- "We're under contract"
- "We've built this internally"

→ They need to know **what value they'll get in return** for the time, even if they don't buy.

## Key Point 2 — The EVO Framework

[03:07] **E**mpathize → **V**alidate → **O**ffer.

**Empathize:** When someone says "not interested," put yourself in their head. They picked up thinking you were someone else, realized you were a salesperson, feel irritated, busy, impatient.

**Validate:** Simple acknowledgement. Variations that work:
- "Sounds like I caught you right in the middle of something."
- "All good, no problem."
- "Hey, I hear you."

**Offer:** Now you give them a reason to keep talking.

## Handling Instinctive Objections

### "Not interested" / "I'm busy" / "We're all set"

[04:56] **Generic response:**
> "All good — before I let you go, we're hearing from finance leaders that compliance issues often stall growth. Is that something you're running into?"

> **Steal this phrase:** "Before I let you go..."

**Tailored response (with research):**
> "All good. Before I let you go — I noticed you just launched [Product] in France and Italy. ABC Company's team was spending a lot of time in compliance that was getting in the way of growth. How does that compare to your situation right now? Are you experiencing that as well, or am I totally barking up the wrong tree here?"

### "I'm about to head into a meeting"

[05:48] Give them the choice:
> "All good. Before I let you go, I'm hearing from finance leaders that compliance issues often stall growth. Do you have a minute right now to see if this is relevant, or should we plan to chat later today after your meeting?"

**Critical:** Do not leave that call without a **calendared next step**. If they pick "later," respond: "Okay, cool — how about 4:00 PM? I'll send you a 5-minute calendar hold."

### "How'd you get my number?"

[06:37] Feels like invasion of privacy. Be **upfront and honest**:
> "Fair question — I actually got it from ZoomInfo. I can send you an email with details on removing yourself from their database if you'd like. But the reason I called you specifically is because many finance teams are running into international compliance issues that often stall growth. Do you mind if I take a minute to see if this would be helpful for you?"

Or blame marketing: "I actually got it from our marketing team. I can let you know how to remove your number from our list after this call if you'd like..."

### "Send me an email" — the 3-part pattern

[08:30] Between you and me, they don't actually want the email. (Bay says: out of thousands of times he's done this, less than a hand-count of people actually responded to the email and booked.)

**Part 1 — Get more info (agree to send, then pivot):**
> "Hey, so I can get you the best information — I'm curious. Many finance teams and their CFOs run into tax compliance issues, and we just helped them free up some of their team's time to avoid stalling international growth. With the recent expansion into France and Italy, how have you been dealing with international compliance? How's that been going?"

**Part 2 — Ask for the meeting:**
> "Great — that's exactly what we've helped software teams like XYZ company achieve: full tax compliance and growing sales by 20%+. Most of what I'd email you honestly would be super generic, and I think you'd find it more valuable if we looked at this through a lens specific to your business. Do you have your calendar handy?"

**Part 3 — Calendar fallback if they resist:**
> "No worries. How about we put 5 minutes on the calendar for tomorrow when you've had a chance to review the email? If you like what you see, we keep the meeting; if you don't, you cancel it. Sound cool?"

## Handling Calculated Objections

### "We already have a solution" — Pros & Cons technique

[10:44] Prepare 2–3 pros & cons for each competing solution.

> "I'm super glad to hear that managing payment workflows is a focus for you. Do you mind sharing more about the solution that you're using?"

In cybersecurity etc. people won't share. Acknowledge:
> "I know I might be asking for a lot here, but I'm curious — can you share what solution you're using?"

Then drop the pros/cons:
> "With the recent expansion into Italy, some of our clients find that ABC competitor is very simple to set up (pro), but doesn't scale well as they add new countries (con). It doesn't keep up with tax fraud prevention... How are you thinking about the scalability of your current setup?"

### Pricing objection

[12:20]
> "Fair question. Pricing really depends on your needs and what you're looking to accomplish. How about this — we're helping SaaS finance teams at companies like XYZ grow revenue by 20% while keeping margins healthy. What if we found a time to walk through your needs so I can get you accurate pricing? Do you have your calendar handy?"

For unusual models:
> "Totally fair question. We actually do pricing quite a bit different from what you've probably seen. We don't charge based on users, we charge based on usage..."

### "Bad timing" / "Not a priority" / "Got our hands full"

[13:09] **Use the objection as the reason for the meeting.**
> "Totally understand. That's exactly why I reached out. A lot of the finance teams we're working with right now run into international compliance issues — and I noticed you guys just expanded into France and Italy. It just eats up a ton of the team's bandwidth. What if I showed you some ways we could help you and similar companies reduce the busy work and be more strategic with your time? Do you have your calendar handy?"

### "No budget" — same reframe

[13:57]
> "That's exactly why I called. We're working with a lot of finance leaders right now around protecting margins and looking for every opportunity to consolidate their tools. How about this — let's grab some time so I can show you how similar companies are reducing their tech and support costs. Do you have your calendar handy?"

## Pro tebe — aplikace (low CZ adaptation)

**Instinctive objections u majitelů malých firem (75%+):**
- "Nemáme zájem" / "Teď to neřešíme" / "Pošli mi to mailem"
- "Mám tu zákazníka, nemůžu mluvit" / "Spěchám"
- "My máme dost zákazníků"

→ **CZ "before I let you go":** "Hele, klidně tě nezdržuju — než zavěsíš..." nebo "Než ti to pošlu mailem..."

**Send-me-email reframe (CZ):**
> "Pošlu ti to. Ale abych ti poslala něco, co dává smysl — můžu se zeptat, co teď nejvíc řešíš: spíš získat nové klienty, nebo udržet ty, které už máš?"
> (pause, get talking)
> "Super — přesně tohle řešíme s klienty, jako je {tvoje case study s čísly}. Mail, co ti pošlu, by byl hodně obecný. Mrkneme se na to spolu 15 minut, ať ti můžu ukázat něco fakt relevantního? Máš kalendář otevřený?"

**Calculated objection — "Účetní už máme" (ukázka: účetní kancelář):**
*Pros/Cons reframe:*
> "Super, účetní ti pohlídá, aby přiznání odešlo včas — to je jeho silná stránka. Ale kdo ti během roku řekne, kde zbytečně přeplácíš na daních? To ti samotné zaúčtování neukáže. Jak to teď řešíš?"

**"That's exactly why I called" CZ:**
> *"Nemáme čas / teď to neřešíme."*
> "Hele, přesně proto volám. Většina majitelů, se kterými začínám, nemá čas — proto to mám nastavené tak, že většinu práce převezmu já a od tebe potřebuju jen minimum. Můžeme se na 15 minut potkat, ať ti to ukážu?"
