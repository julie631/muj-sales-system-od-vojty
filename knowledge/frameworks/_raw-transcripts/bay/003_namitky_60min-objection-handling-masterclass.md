# Objection Handling — 60-Minute Masterclass

## The setup — pattern recognition is your edge

[00:00] **Cold calling is predictable.** Six or seven objections cover the field.

> "It's like driving an F1 car — going 100 miles an hour, not a lot of room for mistakes. Knowing what we are going to say is really key."

## The psychology — Kahneman System 1

[01:50] Department store analogy: "Can I help you find something?" → "No thanks, just looking." That's **reflexive**, not thoughtful.

Daniel Kahneman's *Thinking, Fast and Slow*:
- **System 1:** brushing teeth — automatic
- **System 2:** another car swerves in front of you — engaged, present

**Most sales interactions trigger System 1.** "I'm just looking" is automatic — they don't think about it.

> "Many prospects won't tell the whole truth because it's just easier and saves them time to avoid confrontation. What they really want to say is 'stop bothering me' — but easier to say 'I'm good.'"

So when a prospect says "not interested" or "I'm in a meeting" within 15-30 seconds, **that's not what they mean**. There's an underlying feeling to address.

## The Gong data — 3 buckets

[10:23] **300M cold calls** (Gong Labs + 30MPC):
- **49.5% dismissive** objections (not interested, I'm in a meeting, send me email) — up to 75% in some industries
- **42.6% situational** (no budget, no bandwidth, feature gap)
- **7.9% competitor** (already using XYZ)

## Two categories

**Instinctive objections** = knee-jerk. They don't know your solution. They're objecting to **being cold called**.
- Need: **acknowledgement**

**Calculated objections** = legitimate reason, comes later in call.
- Need: **what value will I get in 30 minutes even if I don't buy?**

## The EVO Framework

[20:00] **E**mpathize → **V**alidate → **O**ffer.

For instinctive objections, E+V is one sentence acknowledging what's on their mind:
- "Hey, I know the last thing you were probably expecting was to get a call from a salesperson."
- "Hey, I bet you thought I was expecting someone else."
- "Hey, I know we haven't met before."
- "Hey, I know you're probably super busy."

Channel **unconditional positive regard** (Bay reference to therapy): tone is **understanding and validating**.

## Two Gong-backed best practices

### 1. Pause 5× longer after receiving an objection

[18:42] Top performers who book meetings **pause up to 5× longer** after receiving an objection. Most reps cut the prospect off as they're still sharing.

> "Just give it a pause. This shows confidence, charisma — none of that sales-y commission breath."

### 2. Respond WITH a question — 54.3% of the time

Top performers follow up objections with a question **54.3% of the time** vs **31% for average reps**.

> "We never want to just respond to the objection. Oh yeah, we don't price based on user, we actually price based on usage. Then pause. → That lets the prospect take the conversation anywhere. I want to control the conversation through questions."

## Handling Instinctive Objections (detailed)

### "Not interested"

Live workshop examples Bay riffed from chat:

**CMO who's selling Nina's solution:**
> "Hey, all good. Before I let you go — I'm hearing from CMOs at companies like Zoom that they're seeing rising cost per acquisition and just struggling to get engagement with their content. Is that something you're running into?"

**Curriculum director (Wolfgang's example):**
> "We're hearing from curriculum directors at LA School District that they're having a challenging time with achievement goals to ace their district report cards."

**Attorneys (acquisition-cost angle):**
> "Hearing from other attorneys that they're seeing rising acquisition costs and traditional SEO + paid media + video branding isn't working the way it used to. Is that something you're running into?"

**Performance leaders (OKR visibility):**
> "Hey, all good. Before I let you go — we're hearing from performance leaders at companies like Zoom that it's really hard to get visibility into projects and they tend to miss their OKRs, especially big ones like XYZ project you have at ABC company. How does that compare to your situation?"

**Closed vs open question:** Bay prefers closed early (yes/no), open-ended deeper in. "Tell me/Explain/Describe how that compares to your situation."

### "I'm in a meeting right now"

Construction firm example:
> "All good. Before I let you go — we're working with a few construction firms here in Seattle, and one of the toughest things they come across is cost certainty on projects, especially when there's a profit swing with materials. That ends up pushing timelines. **Do you have a minute right now to see if that's relevant, or should we plan to chat later today after your meeting?**"

If they punt: **send 5-minute calendar invite immediately.** Don't leave a cold call without a calendared next step.

### "How'd you get my phone number?"

> "Hey, fair question. Got your info from ZoomInfo." (Or marketing team, or webinar.)
>
> "Happy to send you an email with details on removing yourself from the database if you'd like. But the reason I called you specifically is [problem statement]..."

**Don't be snarky.** It feels like privacy invasion to them. Be **transparent**.

### "Can you send me an email?" (the last instinctive variant)

**Get more info first:**
> "Hey, happy to send an email. So I can get you the best information — many HR leaders we work with who are hiring internationally often run into tons of compliance burden. How are you approaching international hiring right now?"

**Then ask for the meeting:**
> "Great — that's exactly what we've helped teams like Gong achieve. Most of what I could email would be pretty generic. You'd likely find it more valuable to look at this through a lens specific to your business. Do you have your calendar handy?"

**5-min calendar fallback:**
> "No problem. Let me send you that email. In the meantime, let's put 5 minutes on the calendar for tomorrow at the same time. If you like what you see in the email, we keep the meeting. If not, cancel. Sound fair?"

## Handling Calculated Objections

### "We use a competitor / already have a solution"

**Empathize + ask for the solution name:**
> "Hey, great to hear that reducing administrative work around compliance is a focus for you. Do you mind sharing more about the solution you're using?"

**Pros & Cons pattern (Bay's preferred):**

Example with Epic (database tool):
> "Awesome — Epic, great solution. We've heard great things. I talk with a lot of database leaders, and one thing they really like about Epic is it's an excellent source of truth tool. But sometimes what we hear from customers like A and B is it doesn't really allow them to look into the future or be proactive — they can really only look at the past. How are you thinking about that?"

**The trick:** Bay takes your value prop ("we let you look forward") and **flips it into a problem the competitor doesn't solve.**

If you don't know the specific competitor → group them (enterprise solutions vs ad-hoc piecemeal). The pros/cons cluster within each group.

### "How much does this cost?"

This is a **buying signal**.

> "Hey, fair question. Pricing really depends on your needs and what you're looking to accomplish. We're helping data leaders at companies like X, Y, Z get more insight into where their database is heading so they can predict trends versus retroactive reporting. Let's find a time to talk through your needs so I can get you accurate and customized pricing. Do you have your calendar handy?"

### "Bad timing / not a priority"

[49:11] **Use the objection as the reason for the meeting:**
> "Totally understand. That's exactly why I reached out. Many HR leaders we talk to that run into international hiring challenges talk about how much time it eats up — they'd love to get that time back. Let's grab some time so I can show you how similar companies are solving for that specific challenge."

### "We don't have budget"

> "Hey, that's exactly why I called. A ton of HR leaders we're talking to are running on tight budgets — have to get more done with less, looking for every opportunity to get time back, especially compliance-related tasks and costs. Let's grab some time so I can show you exactly how companies like Zoom and Gong are handling that right now."

## The Offer — "Selling the Blind Date"

[53:03] **The offer = what you do to make the buyer feel irresponsible for saying no.**

Variants:
- **Sell the AE's expertise** (if SDR booking for AE): "I'd love to connect you with Tom — here's all the experience he has, here's what you'll learn."
- **Sell your own expertise** (if AE): "I was a former teacher. I can share what we saw with the teachers we help."
- **Sell solution expert / VP of Marketing meeting** (power-to-power)
- **Insights:** Bay's example — client did mystery shop of 300 e-commerce companies, found things like chat response times. Open call with: *"Your chat response time is about 3× longer than competitors at North Face and Columbia. Got a minute for me to share why I'm calling?"*
- **Assessments / benchmark reports / 1:1 custom offers**

> "It's the equivalent of giving your email address on a website — you get something in return."

## Displacing existing vendors

[55:00] When prospect has an established vendor team (consultancy, software, etc.) and there's overlap:

**Displace through INSIGHT.** What do you know about their situation or industry that the current competitor does not?

- Lead with offers (above)
- What can you share about their business?
- What ideas can you bring?

> "That's how you displace a competitor."

## Outcome vs problem framing

[56:03] Steve's question: outcome over problem?

> "I think you should do both. People say you shouldn't talk about your solution. Yeah, I do think you should talk about your solution. Just don't talk about what it does the first time you meet someone. They might need a category — service or software."
>
> "Focus more on outcome (pipeline generated, accuracy of forecasting, revenue) vs feature dump (sequences, automation, AI)."

## Pro tebe — aplikace (low CZ adaptation)

**Tato lekce je v podstatě extended #002. Aplikace stejná — viz #002. Co tento longer-form přidává navíc:**

### 1. "Pause 5× longer" — self-coaching priorita

Když děláš sales calls online (Google Meet, Zoom) a nahráváš je — self-review pravidlo:
- Při poslechu vlastního callu: kdy jsem skočila prospektovi do řeči po objection?
- Kolikrát jsem následovala objection question vs vlastním pitchem?

**Cíl:** 50%+ objections → followed by question. Gong data: top reps 54.3%.

### 2. "Selling the Blind Date" v praxi

Tohle můžeš používat MASIVNĚ. Když prospect dělá rozhovor a hesituje:

> "Hele, z klientů, se kterými pracuju, už vidím jednu věc, která se pořád opakuje: {vzorec, který vidíš napříč svými klienty — např. ‚většina firem zjistí, že přeplácí na daních, až když už se s tím nedá nic dělat'}. To je insight, který nikde jinde nedostaneš. Pojď, dáme 15 minut na call, ukážu ti to naživo na {tvoje case study s čísly}."

→ Sells: tvoje data (insight napříč klienty) + živou ukázku výsledků.

### 3. Insight-driven displacement vs konkurenci

Pokud prospect říká "my na to máme aplikaci, to nám stačí" (ukázka: jazyková škola → firmy):
> "Cool. Hodně firem, se kterými mluvím, aplikaci zkoušelo — vidím opakující se pattern: první měsíc ji používá skoro každý, po třech měsících už jen pár lidí. Místo aby řešili, proč lidi odpadají, kupují další licence. To vidím u většiny firem, se kterými pracuju. Bavili jsme se o tomhle u vás?"

→ Bay's "displace through insight" princip.

### 4. Pros & Cons proti "stačí nám to, co máme"

Pokud klient řekne "ale my lidem platíme kartu na sport, to stačí" (ukázka: firemní fyzioterapie):
> "Super, karta na sport je dobrá věc — lidi ji využívají, chodí si zacvičit. Ale nikdo s ní neřeší záda od sezení u počítače — to je to, co dělám já. Karta dá všem stejnou možnost, já projdu každého zvlášť a nastavím mu cviky na jeho konkrétní problém ve chvíli, kdy ho začne řešit. Jak to teď řešíte s těmi zády?"

→ Pro = "karta na sport je dobrá věc". Con = "není zacílená na konkrétní problém, jen obecný benefit".

### 5. Outcome vs problem (v praxi)

Bay's bod aplikovaný (ukázka: účetní kancelář):
- ŠPATNĚ outcome: "optimalizace daňových procesů"
- DOBŘE outcome: konkrétní čísla z case study ({tvoje case study s čísly})
- NEJLEPE outcome: "žádné víkendy nad papíry + víc tvého času"
