# "We increased pipeline by 170% this month!"

## The setup — saying no is the unlock

[00:02] Clients' biggest fear when narrowing: "We don't want to say no to business."

By **not saying no, you're not allowing yourselves to say yes in a really big way**.

What Bay guarantees about the future:
- Contact rates will go down (fewer people pick up the phone, lower email reply rates)
- It'll take more activities to reach someone

So if you're trying to boil the ocean across industries, personas, anyone who could possibly participate in a deal — you're requiring your reps to do way too much.

## The martini glass framing

[00:54] Don't want pipeline to look like a pipeline. Want it to look like a **martini glass**:
- **Shrink the top of funnel**
- Go really deep on fewer opportunities
- Higher response likelihood
- Higher win-deal likelihood (much better fit)

What it requires: **say no in a really big way**. From outbound standpoint = say no to most of your accounts.

## Hot take — intent data is a waste

[02:56] *"Intent data is a waste of money. It should not be used to inform your outbound approach."*

Why: "Someone at Microsoft is looking for a sales engagement platform. Awesome. What do I do with that information? I don't know **who** it is. This account is surging — what the hell do I do with that?"

Many senior sales leaders not renewing with the big intent data provider in the last 2-3 months.

## Case study — 11 verticals + 30 plays → 170% pipeline growth

Enterprise BDR team, enterprise content management. Fortune 500 + mid-market. Selling a complex solution that COULD be sold across:
- 11 industry verticals
- 30+ plays

Different verticals use the product differently — hospital pulling medical records + customer history vs. Nordstrom using same capability for retail experience.

> "You can't wrap your head around 30 different types of conversations to have this week."

## The narrowing playbook

### Step 1 — Pick 3 industries (use RevOps data)

Use data to pick:
- Where win rates are **highest**
- Where deal cycles are **shortest**
- Where ACVs are **highest**
- Where you get the **most repeat business**
- Optionally: TAM/growth opportunity sizing

### Step 2 — Refine personas

> "When people tell me one of their buyers is IT and they don't sell an IT solution — I'm like, we're not going to outbound to IT. They get brought in **after** the fact for technical fit. That's not where you start a sales conversation."

In most cases there are **2 maybe 3 buyer types**.

**Reverse engineer 10 deals that closed this year:**
- Who were the first meetings with?
- Who were the champions?
- Who were the economic buyers?
- **Follow the same golden path each time.**

### Step 3 — Triggers (3–5)

[06:02] After narrowing accounts (3 verticals instead of 11) and personas (2 instead of 5):

Cybersecurity client example — trigger = "opening new manufacturing plants" (IT security in the plants). Look for accounts with that activity in last 6–12 months. Other triggers: hiring, expansion.

**Determine 3–5 triggers, narrow even more to what's relevant right now.**

### Result

[07:46] **170% pipeline increase: $3.12M → $8.43M in a single month.** Within 6–9 months.

Just by saying NO to 8 industry verticals and 3 personas.

## Getting leadership buy-in for narrowing

> "People buy on emotion and justify with logic."

Empathize:
> "Hey, I totally hear where you're coming from. We are going to say no to certain things, and short term we could leave a little revenue on the table that might be hard to sit with."

Then logic:
> "A rep only has so many mental calories per week. For an AE, maybe 1-2 hours per day on outbound. Do you want them spending that time on a 20% opportunity or a 3% opportunity?"

Then show the data:
- Win rate by industry
- TAM by industry
- Deal size by industry
- Sales cycle length by industry

## Social proof — Gong study

[10:48] Gong + Outbound Squad study of **85 million cold emails**:
- Social proof in emails = **40%+ reply rate increase**
- Same effect on cold calls and in-deal storytelling
- "We work with Nordstrom" to a retail company = powerful

If you don't have a Nordstrom for retail and you mention you work with a bank — the language doesn't match. People need to feel you understand **their industry** and who they are as a buyer.

## The 3-story memorization technique

> "The best thing I did as a seller was **memorize 3 customer stories** I could use across different objections, verticals, and personas."

Have a backlog of 2-3 customer stories that could apply to different use cases / different results. Memorize them. They're your go-to.
