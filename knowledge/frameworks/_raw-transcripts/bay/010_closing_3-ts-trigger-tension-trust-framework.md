# Mastering the Framework that Triples Cold Call Conversions

## The 3× gap

[00:04] Most reps: 5% connect-to-meeting rate. Top performers: 15%. **3× higher.**

## Why most cold calls fail — bad first impression

[01:07] **No one picks up a cold call on purpose.** They thought you were someone else.

### Two killers in the first 60 seconds

**1. Double / Triple Clutch**
> "Hey, is this Kevin? Kevin, how's your day going?" — asked 2 questions that delivered zero value to the buyer.

Then add: "Do you have a minute?" → 3 questions in 30 seconds, no value delivered.

**2. Prospecting Narcissism / Product Voice**
> "We have a platform that so-and-so's are using to create more visibility and efficiency and leverage..." — gobbledygook the buyer can't connect to their day-to-day.

> "Prospecting narcissism — taking the microphone and talking all about ourselves."

### Avoid corporate-talk transformation

> "We become a different human at work. We say things in a register we'd never use with friends/family."

ZoomInfo Kevin: during candidate interview role-plays, candidates transform into a different person. Look for someone equally confident in the role-play as on cold calls.

## The 3 questions buyers always ask

[08:05] Whether outbound or in-deal:
- **Why change?** — why should I care about doing anything differently?
- **Why now?** — why this moment vs the 100 other priorities?
- **Why you?** — why should I trust YOU specifically?

The intro must answer all 3.

## The 3 T's Framework — Trigger / Tension / Trust

[09:00] After the PBO, give them the **Reason to Meet (RTM)** in 3 sentences:

### Trigger (Why now)
What did you see in their business? Strat outreach = company-specific (new product launch). SMB outreach = industry-wide.

### Tension (Why change)
What problem do you think they might be having?

### Trust (Why you)
How do you demonstrate in one sentence that you've worked with their peers?

> "99% of cold calls people get are from people they honestly shouldn't trust. Establish credibility instantly."

### Full example

**HR solution:**
> "Hey Mike, it's Jason. Saw that you guys are doing a bunch of international hiring right now [TRIGGER]. Again, it's Jason with so-and-so company. You got a minute for me to share the reason why I called you specifically about that.
>
> [Yes.]
>
> I'm not sure if you're running into this, but a lot of the HR teams expanding in places like France and Italy often get really encumbered with manual work and compliance and a bunch of things that keep them from focusing on hiring top talent [TENSION]. We just worked with another company in Seattle called XYZ on this exact problem [TRUST]. How does that compare to your day-to-day?"

## Michael's peer-to-peer research opener

[12:55] Strat SDR Michael couldn't reach anyone at a target account. **Spoke to SDRs at the company first** to understand the pain — then opened with that intel:

> "Hey Sarah, I'm calling because a few SDRs told me they're primarily using LinkedIn for prospecting and it's taking a lot of time, reducing sales activities. Seems like a lot of reps are frustrated and would love to learn more about ZoomInfo."

> "You're creating your own triggers and signals." — Bay's commentary

## How to nail the tension

[15:39] Take 10-15 of your last sales call transcripts → drop into Claude/ChatGPT → ask for common problem themes. Use those as your tension statements.

Newer reps: leverage your company's historical conversation library (thousands of calls).

## Pivot from objection to discovery

### "We already have a competitor"

[18:22] Michael's earlier mistake: this stopped him. Now he sees it as an opportunity to ask questions — they're already investing in the problem. **They're aware they need a solution like this.**

## Instinctive vs Calculated (EVO refresher)

[20:16] Same as #002 / #003. Bay drops it more concisely here:
- 75% instinctive — needs **acknowledgement**
- Calculated — needs **value-in-return-for-time**

## Send-me-an-email handling — 2-phase

[22:15] **Phase 1 — get more intel:**
> "Got it. Yeah, happy to send. Hey, I'm really curious — I saw you guys are hiring internationally right now. How are you approaching the compliance pieces? How much time does that take?"

**Phase 2 — ask for the meeting:**
> "Hey, to be frank — I'm happy to send the email. What I think would be much quicker is for me to just show you how this might help you. It's something we've helped XYZ company with. How about we carve out some time when I'm not cold calling you in the middle of your day? What's later this afternoon look like, or tomorrow morning?"

**5-min calendar hold fallback:**
> "How about this — I'll send you an invite for tomorrow at 10:30am. Same time we just talked. If you like what you see in the email, we keep the time. If you don't, you cancel."

## The Offer — "Selling the Blind Date"

[25:10] **Buyers are jaded:**
- RAIN Group survey: B2B buyers say **58% of meetings with sales people are a waste of time**
- Gartner: buyers experience **buyer's regret on 50% of purchases**

So buyers don't want more meetings. **You must give something in return.**

### "Back of the baseball card" offer

For Michael's situation (selling for Kevin, 25-year sales veteran):
> "Hey Michael, I totally get where you're coming from. I'd like to put you in touch with my account manager Kevin. The reason: Kevin's for the last 25+ years been talking to executives exactly like you at companies like X, Y, Z on their go-to-market motion, how they generate pipeline, and everyone we talk to right now is starving for pipeline. There's 2-3 strategies he can share with you and insights from other customers we're working with. I want to set you up with Kevin."

→ Sell the **expertise** of the person they'll meet.

### Custom offer — TAM insight (ZoomInfo-style)

> "How about this — long shot. I'd like to put you in touch with Kevin. We've done a deep analysis on your total addressable market and found about a thousand companies actively searching for products and services exactly like yours. First thing in the meeting: we show you who those companies are and how to reach them. Second: how some of those sales orgs operationalize the go-to-market motion. Third: if there's an avenue we could help, I'll tell you how."

### Custom offer — cart audit example

E-com client did **cart audits** before sales calls:
> "We actually purchased several items through your cart and noticed a couple things missing that might be affecting average order value and abandoned cart rate. For example, I added X to my cart but didn't get an email later that day. Want to walk you through that — even if nothing else, you'll get really tangible ideas to bring back to your team."

### The hidden insight

[31:48] **97% of people you talk to are NOT actively looking to switch.** You have to PRESENT the problem to them — that answers the "why change" question.

## Eliminating no-shows

[42:23] Three key tactics beyond the obvious confirmation email:

### 1. Accept the calendar invite LIVE

Most execs have a setting where the invite doesn't hit their calendar unless they accept through the email. Ask them on the call.

### 2. Custom calendar event title (internal language)

> "I'm doing a call with a company. They call it Sales Impacted Opportunities (SIO). The calendar event title is **'SIO Acceleration'** because that's the internal language they use."

DO NOT use "[Vendor] Intro" as the title. **The easiest meetings for execs to cancel are external vendor meetings.**

### 3. Custom agenda in the invite description

- We're going to talk about [their specific problem]
- We're going to talk about how we're helping [specific customer] with that
- We'll talk about how and if we might be able to help

Don't use defaults from Calendly. Edit before sending.

## Confidence techniques

[36:40] Bay's confidence stack:

**1. Reason to Meet (3 T's mastery)** — feel like you have a really strong reason for them to meet, even if they say no.

**2. Body energy:**
- Don't sit during calls
- Stand and **pace** in the office
- Sitting up > sitting down in terms of energy

**3. Momentum carryover:**
- Just closed a deal → hit the phones immediately
- Just had a great VP-of-sales conversation → reach out to similar buyers right after
- Use that confidence

**4. Practice with AI:**
- Talk to AI tools out loud
- Rehearse your RTM statement

**5. Practice walking around:**
> "I'll just walk around my house practicing what I'm saying. Not on a live call. We get so much repetition on live calls — we forget standalone practice."

### Kevin's "post-meeting momentum" advice

> "Reps book a meeting and immediately stop to do the spreadsheet/email/calendar logistics. You put all the positive energy into admin. NO — keep your call block going. Your prospect can wait 15 minutes for the follow-up. Roll with that confidence."

## Call reluctance — CBT Triangle

[46:45] Cognitive Behavioral Therapy framework:

```
    Thoughts
       /\
      /  \
     /    \
Emotions  Behavior
```

**These influence each other but DO NOT HAVE TO BE CONNECTED.**

Just because you don't feel like making a call (emotion) doesn't mean you can't make the call (behavior).

### Reframe techniques

**1. Shrink the goal** — instead of "book a meeting" → "see if they want to have a conversation."

**2. Reframe as research call** — "I'm a researcher documenting my experience" (Bay's improv class trick).

**3. Worst-case acceptance** — Jon Jones UFC mindset (radical acceptance). "Worst case in a cold call: someone hangs up and you feel awkward."

- **Jak dlouho to děláš**
- **Kolik máš klientů (a kolik z nich aktivních)**
- **{tvoje case study s čísly}**
- **Druhá case study / živá ukázka výsledků**
- **Co přesně dodáváš (rozsah služby)**

> "Hele Petře, mrkněme se na to spolu 15 minut. Ukážu ti naživo, jak to vypadá u {klient z tvých case studies} (případně vezmu kolegu, který to u nás technicky dělá), a řeknu ti konkrétně, co by to přineslo tobě. I když do toho nepůjdeš, odejdeš s konkrétními insighty, co máš dělat sám."

### Custom offer — "TAM analysis" ve službách

Ekvivalent ve službách: **audit zdarma před rozhodnutím**

> "Hele, můžeme udělat tohle. Mrknu se, jak máte nastavené počítače a zálohy. Konkrétně: kolik zařízení nemá aktualizace, kde chybí záloha, kdo má k čemu přístup, co by se stalo, kdyby zítra odešel disk. Pošlu ti report, co tam vidím — i kdybys do spolupráce se mnou nešel, dostaneš obrázek čísel, která ti nikdo jiný nedá."

→ To je to, co tě odliší od konkurence, která pošle jen ceník.

### CBT Triangle pro tebe

Máš outreach KPI (např. X oslovených denně). Bay's CBT:
- **Thought:** "Dnes se mi nechce sednout k IG DMům"
- **Emotion:** Únava, demotivace
- **Behavior:** ALE I PŘESTO sednu a udělám 3 DMy

Decoupling = klíč. Behavior nemusí čekat na motivaci. Stačí 3 DMy → po 3. už motivace přijde naturally.
