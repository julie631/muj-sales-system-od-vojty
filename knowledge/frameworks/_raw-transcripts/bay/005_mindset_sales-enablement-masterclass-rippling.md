# Sales Enablement Masterclass with Jonas Master (VP of Sales Enablement, Rippling)

## Background — Rippling scale

[06:08] When Jonas joined Rippling 3 years ago: 350-400 sales reps. Today: 1800. Heading to 2200 by EOY next year. Enablement team grew from 7 → 33 people.

Rippling = workforce operating system (HR, finance, IT back-end automation).

## The 5-part framework

1. Prioritize
2. Train (overrated)
3. Measure
4. Practice
5. Reinforce

## Part 1 — Prioritization

### Non-negotiable vs. discretionary

[08:33] Non-negotiable items the business dictates:
- Hiring 700 people this year → onboarding takes priority
- Tier-1 product release → everything drops

**Where enablement needs the most help: prioritizing everboarding / ongoing optimization** that isn't business-dictated.

### Reverse-engineer from the outcome

> "On a quarterly basis: if we could change ONE metric this quarter, what would it be? Put muscle behind that. The inverse: 'let's run a training every week and hope something sticks.' At the end of the quarter you say you did a ton of stuff. But what metric did you impact? Nothing."

### Proactive insight surfacing

Same as #030 — fail mode is sitting back waiting for sales leader direction. **At minimum 50/50 collaboration:**

> "I noticed S2→S3 conversion issues. I listened to 15 Gong calls — reps aren't setting next meetings well. Maybe a refresher."

Vs. "Q1 — what do you want trained on?" → instant credibility loss.

> "Showing up for a sales call unprepared and asking 'what are your priorities for the year' = same loss of credibility. Enablement is doing internal sales — discovery, research, propose informed."

## Part 2 — Training is overrated

### Ebbinghaus forgetting curve

[15:27] **10% retention 24 hours after training.**

Fail mode: optimizing for **the training**, not **the outcome.**

### Who should facilitate

[18:39] Be the training. Top 10% SDR who became enablement = great cold call trainer. **Enablement person who hasn't cold called in 3 years training SDRs = zero shot.**

> "You will win sellers over forever by showing 'this is how I do it.'"

Enablement person's role: **MC + framework engineer + what comes after the training.**

The expert seller often **doesn't know what makes them great** (Jordan can't coach).

### Active vs passive

> "Put up slides on PowerPoint, talk for 45 minutes, everyone passively sitting → worst way. Best way: get repetitions, get people doing the thing."

Leader = framework + modeling. Enablement = facilitate practice + workshopping + role-playing.

## Part 3 — Measurement

### Skin in the game

[22:07] More uncomfortable to be on the hook for a metric, but that's how you drive impact.

> "You're not going to bat 1000. If you're batting 400, you're a fantastic hitter and you ARE driving meaningful impact."

### Picking the metric

Two fail modes:
1. **No metric at all** (most common)
2. Picking the wrong metric (too broad)

**Don't measure end revenue.** Too broad, no attribution.

**Concrete example:** Rippling ran competitive series vs specific competitor. Tracked:
- **Behavior change:** are reps saying the messaging in Gong calls?
- **Win rates vs that competitor** week over week → 4 months later, up 25%.

### Kirkpatrick Model (4 levels)

[26:08] Sales enablement standard:
1. **Learner sentiment** — stars on training survey
2. **Knowledge** — can they pass a quiz?
3. **Behavior change** — are they using the trained language? (Gong/Chorus makes this measurable)
4. **Metric impact** — actual business outcome

> "Pre-Gong, behavior change was the hardest level — you'd literally sit knee-to-knee. Now you measure if people are saying the words to customers. Don't pay for Gong without using this."

## Part 4 — Reinforcement & Frontline Managers

### Managers are the end customer (not reps)

[28:21] **Numerical reality:**
- 1 enablement person : 100 sellers
- 1 manager : 5 sellers
- Manager spends 5 hours/day with the team, enablement gets 1-2 hours/month

> "The frontline manager is the END CUSTOMER. Not the rep. If you're imposing things on managers — 'this is my bright idea, this is my strategy, you had zero input' — you're setting yourself up for failure."

### Co-create with champions

Build advocacy by bringing 1-2 weighted managers into creation. They become the trainers. Massively more credibility rolling out.

> "Look — you'd never sell a big deal without strong champions. Internally we sell without champions all the time and wonder why nothing sticks."

### Manager coaching cadence

[33:32] **Best Rippling managers do:**
- 2 calls listened-to + coached per week
- 2 one-on-ones (one deal-focused, one coaching-focused)
- Live role-play + recorded-call review

### Gong AI scoring + calibration

[32:41] Rippling experimented with Gong AI call scoring:
- Initially: AI was over-complimentary, scored disaster calls as "amazing"
- After **manager calibration loop** ("AI, you're dead wrong, this is a 2/5 because X") → got it into pretty good spot
- Now used to **identify spikiness** at extremes — 5/5 calls → golden library; 1.5/5 calls → manager slack alert: "go listen and coach."

> "Doesn't abdicate manager's 2-calls-a-week responsibility. Makes it more intelligent. Instead of spending time LOOKING for calls to coach, manager spends time COACHING."

## Part 5 — Practice (AI Roleplay caveat)

[35:44] Jonas piloted every major AI roleplay tool. Bay's worry: AI coaching replacing manager coaching.

**Jonas's finding:**
> "Reps quickly learn how to GAME the AI to get the right results — not necessarily improve. Five-out-of-five scores. We'd ask 'how did you get there?' → 'I noticed if I did it this way I got a better score.' Cheat codes to the video game. Not actual practice."

This is why Rippling **didn't** onboard one of these tools 12 months ago. Open to it if it actually works — but if manager-coaching gets abdicated, "I'm out 100% anyway."

**Current state:** old school — 2 calls/week manager-coached, paired with deal + coaching one-on-ones.

## Just-Go-and-See (Rippling leadership principle)

[31:44] One of Rippling's guiding principles: if there's any problem in the business, bring it all the way to ground. **Even our CEO listens to Gong calls** to understand problems.

> "Permeated throughout the entire organization. If your sales managers aren't living on Gong listening to calls — yeah, you're going to be lost."

## Advice for small enablement teams

[38:42] Specialize over generalize.

**The bad model:** instructional designers in the background + horizontal trainers handed packets → guaranteed "compliance-training boredom."

**The good model:** even with a small team, carve out **one person who owns AE motion**. They become deeply domain-expert. Sales leaders pull them in. Eventually they're stretched, you get budget for #2.

> "The way to get more resourcing is internal advocates saying 'we need more.' That happens when you deliver deep integral value, not theoretical philosophical classroom sessions."

## Stress / work-life balance (Jonas's takes)

[42:08]
- **Assume positive intent** — most sniping comes from people being stressed, not malicious
- **Block off no-go zones** — drop-off mornings (8-9am, no Slack), 6:30-8pm family time, non-negotiable
- **Work-life balance is a myth at high-aspiration companies** — but know what mode you're in (newborn baby phase = different from grind phase)
- **Always do the thing** — even high up, you're doing end-work on high-leverage areas

## Pro tebe — meta-aplikace (jsi sama sobě enablement team)

Tato lekce má vysoký retention i bez týmu. **JSI enablement pro sebe + pro klienty, kteří s tvou službou dál pracují.**

### Pick ONE metric (tvoje verze)

Typicky máš ve své tabulce leadů / CRM dvě klíčové metriky:
- **Close rate / oslovené leady**
- **Resolved-positive rate** (podíl pozitivních mezi vyřešenými odpověďmi)

→ Tento kvartál focus na JEDNU. Např. zlepšit close rate z 5 % → 7 % (= 40% improvement). Vše ostatní = secondary.

### Co Bay'ovo "behavior change" měření znamená pro tebe

Jonas říká: stage 3 Kirkpatrick = jsou reps says the words? Tvoje verze:
- **Po každém Bay videu** si zaznamenej 3 fráze, co chceš přidat do svého callu (např. "Before I let you go", "That's exactly why I called")
- **Po týdnu** poslechni 5 vlastních callů a počítej, kolikrát jsi tyto fráze použila
- → Behavior change measurement

### "Frontline manager = end customer" pro tebe

Nemáš frontline manažery. Ale máš **klienty, kteří s tvou službou dál pracují ve svém týmu** (např. majitelka salonu, která nový postup předává kolegyním). Bay's analogy:

- Tvůj 1:1 onboarding s klientem (= enablement)
- Klient pak **1:1 s vlastními lidmi** (= frontline management svého týmu)
- **Klient je end customer** tvého "enablementu" (tvých onboarding callů)

→ Onboarding nesmí být "tady to máš, kuk." Musí být co-created. Klient se musí naučit, jak svým lidem říct "podívejte se na tohle, tady to je důležité." Pak teprve tvoje služba dlouhodobě funguje.

### Just-Go-and-See (CZ aplikace)

Bay's Rippling: CEO poslouchá Gong calls.

**Tvoje verze:** Sleduj aktuální stav své tabulky leadů / CRM týdně, ne měsíčně. Pokud něco nefunguje (např. close rate poklesne), **NEHÁDEJ proč** — jdi poslechnout reálné calls / přečíst přepisy zpráv.

### Internal champions framing

Bay's bod: build internal champions before rolling out. Pro tvoje klienty:
- **Onboarding není rolling-out direktivy.** Je to spolu-tvorba s majitelem.
- Majitel se musí stát champion-em u svých lidí (= frontline managers).
- "Hele Petře, my společně vymyslíme, čím začneme. Ne že ti já řeknu 'dělej to přesně podle mého manuálu'."

### Internal sales framing

Klíčový insight Jonase: "enablement = internal sales".

**Aplikace:** kdykoli měníš něco s klientem (cena, rozsah, nová služba), aplikuj sales framework:
- Why change? Why now? Why you?
- Discovery — neproposuj řešení bez slyšení potřeb
- Build internal champion uvnitř klientovy organizace
