# 60-Minute Cold Calling Masterclass

## Invisible scripts — the buyer's baggage

[01:05] **Invisible scripts** in society — Bay grew up Asian-American, the script was "you become a doctor, lawyer, white collar." Sales was definitely not on the list.

Your buyers have invisible scripts about salespeople from thousands of past interactions:
- "It's annoying"
- "I don't take cold calls"
- "I don't trust cold callers"
- "How did you get my number"
- "Send me an email"

> "They bring all of that baggage into the single interaction they have with you when they say hello. Have empathy for where the buyer is coming from."

## The data — top 25% book 3.8× more

[03:44] Gong + 30MPC study of 300M cold calls:
- **Top 25% reps book 3.8× more meetings than average**
- Higher connect rates (workflow + data)
- Higher set rates
- Much higher show rates

## Where reps get stuck — 3 "ick" factors

### 1. Commission breath (desperation)

[06:18] *"Super Troopers reference: 'desperation is a stinky cologne.'"*

If you come into the call needing the meeting to hit quota → emotional burden on the prospect, like watching someone bomb at an open mic.

### 2. Wasting their time

Lazy questions: "What's keeping you up at night?" / "What's your biggest challenge?" → instant ick.

### 3. Sounding like a sales rep instead of a respectable peer

> "For some reason, a lot of us — I didn't come from a large corporate company — when we talk to buyers we become a different human being. We start saying 'Hey, how's it going?' in a way our friends or family would say 'Who are you, dude?'"

**Remove corporate speak, teacher voice, up-tones. Be conversational. Most importantly: be yourself.**

Show:
- **Authority** (mutual problem language)
- **Social proof** (customer stories)
- **Sales risks** — confident but respectful

## Framework — Intro → Hook → Close

### The Intro — first 30 seconds

Average cold call lasts ~80 seconds. Only 10% go past 2 minutes. **It's all about the intro.**

#### Avoid the double/triple clutch
> "Hey, is this Nina?" / "Hey, how's it going?" — these are dead questions.

Bay doesn't ask "is this Nina?" — **assumes it's the right person, lets them correct.**

#### 5 PBO variants

**1. Classic PBO** (works best SMB):
> "Hey Dave, it's Jason. I know you weren't expecting my call. Do you mind if I take a quick moment to share why I called you specifically?"

**2. Front-Load Relevance** (executives, with trigger):
> "Hey Tim, I was calling about the recent launch of ABC product. It's Jason with Outbound Squad. Do you have a minute? I promise to be brief."

**3. "Heard the name tossed around"** (Armand Farrokh, 30MPC — best for SLED/local):
> "Hey Jason, we work with a few other house painters here in Seattle like ABC Painters. It's Josh with XYZ Construction Pros. Have you heard our name tossed around?"

**4. J-Base / JB Opener** (context + social proof + problem statement):
> "Hey Katie, I was reaching out about the AEs you're hiring and the self-sourcing they're being asked to do. It's Jason over at Outbound Squad. We work with sales teams like Shopify, Rippling, and Gong to get their AEs self-sourcing. Do you got a minute to hear why I'm reaching out to you specifically?"

**5. Cross-sell into existing base:**
> "Hey Jason, it's Clara over at XYZ company. We're working with Ryan over in the Seesaw team on reducing downtime. Do you got a quick minute?"

**Iconic PE study:** account managers / CS reps who self-source opportunities have ~52% win rates — outbound into your existing base is critical in 2026.

#### Bay's recommendation for getting started
> "Front-load relevance is the easiest to implement — you just say 'I'm reaching out because I saw [trigger]. I'm with XYZ. Got a minute?'"

### Connect rate workflow tactics (data + when to call)

[25:50] **Workflow:**
- Most teams call when pickup rates are **lowest**
- **8-9am** first thing in prospect's time zone = highest pickup
- **Mondays + Fridays underutilized** — most reps skip those days
- **5 minutes before the hour** or at top of hour — catch people between meetings

**Data tactics:**
- **Cell phones only** — never generic lines
- **Caller ID setup** — name + company shows on the prospect's screen
- **ORM hot numbers** — AI prioritizes high-pickup numbers
- **Titan X** — feeds list, returns 200-300 with 30%+ pickup rate

Bay's personal experience with Titan X: 30 dials → 10 executive connects (VPs of sales, CROs) = **33% connect rate.**

### Reverse pitch

[30:52] After PBO + yes → **don't pitch your solution. Pitch the problem.**

300M call study: problem-based language **triples** conversion vs buzzwords.

> "Yes — hey, great. I'm calling you because I saw you just launched XYZ product. Fortnite was in a similar position when we were working with them, running into two challenges. One: cost control and margin protection — they wanted to avoid tool sprawl as they grew new product lines, like ABC product you're doing. Two: predictable growth — wanted to grow MRR without complexity, reduce churn, forecast accurately. How does that compare to what you're seeing at XYZ company?"

**The hack to nail this:** make buyer feel like you've been a fly on the wall in their internal meetings.

How: pull recorded sales call transcripts → drop into Perplexity / ChatGPT / Claude → have it pull problem patterns → use those statements.

### Without trigger (mass version)

> "Hey, I was giving you a call because I speak with a lot of cyber security leaders like yourself and here are the two problems that we hear..."

**Bay's 37-second monologue insight:** Gong data shows successful cold calls had up to 37-second monologues from BOTH buyer AND seller. **Calls are 50% more successful with these monologues** — as long as the monologue isn't about you/your company, it's about insight you're seeing in the field.

## The Hook — money questions

[37:33] Gong B2B exec survey: **91% of sellers buyers interact with "know nothing about them, their company, or their role."**

### Bad questions
- "What's your biggest challenge?"
- "What don't you like about [current solution]?"
- Anything that makes prospect admit weakness to a stranger
- Anything that shows you don't know them

> "Never call their baby ugly." — Colton Grace

### Good questions — 4 money question frameworks

**1. User Point of View** (Bay's favorite for software):
> "If I was your finance ops manager, how would I figure out which parts of your revenue stack cost the most to maintain?"
> "If I was in charge of global expansion on your HR team, how would I know which countries are compliant?"

**2. Spotlight Question:**
> "I'm really curious — you've probably already put a lot of thought into this, but how do you connect your P&L to the logistics side of the business so that if you needed to, you could get immediate insights?"

**Softener phrases:**
- "You've probably already thought about this..."
- "I'm really curious..."
- "This might not even be something you're worried about right now..."

> "Bay's Dad example: half-Chinese, half-white, six-foot-four ex-Marine. Could get triggered if you present a new idea. Assume he's already thought about it — invites him to correct you."

**3. Pervasive Problem:**
> "We're seeing it's really challenging to find welding talent right now and many orgs are struggling to fill that gap with automation. How does that compare to what you're seeing at ABC company?"

**4. Tell Me a Story** (emotional driver):
> "Hey, what happened the last time leadership needed an updated revenue projection? How quickly does that take for your team to turn around?"

> "People buy on emotion, justify with logic. Stories drive emotion."

## The Close

### Summarize + Customer Story + Assumptive Close

Once they share a problem your solution helps with → cut the call short.

> "Sounds like the team is having a heck of a time recruiting welding talent, and you're looking for automation, especially traditionally hard-to-automate parts like trailer hitches. This is exactly what we helped XYZ company with — here's the result. **Do you have your calendar handy?** The next best step is to schedule a quick call with my AE Tom who can show you how other teams are tackling this. How's tomorrow at 2pm PT?"

### "Selling the Blind Date" (key concept)

[48:27] Sell the **expertise of the person they'll meet with**, not the product:

> "Hey, the next best step is to set you up with our expert Tom. Tom's actually been in the game for about a decade and he's recently worked with Caterpillar on a very similar issue. He'll be able to talk about how companies like that are dealing with the welder shortage right now and how they're automating some of these traditionally hard-to-automate parts."

### Triple Confirmation (live)

> "Hey Lane, I just sent you an invite for 2pm tomorrow. Would you mind doing me a quick favor? I want to make sure it didn't go to spam. Could you accept that calendar invite real quick? Awesome. Again, the agenda here is X, Y, Z. If we're more than a day out, I'll text/email to confirm."

## Voicemails — yes, leave them

[49:19] **Should you leave voicemails? Yes.**
- **3.24× email reply rate increase** when you leave a voicemail
- **25.8% increase in future pickup rate**

### Framework — Relevance + Problem/Social Proof + CTA

**Critical:** don't ask for a callback. Point them to your email. Lower friction.

> "Hey, I saw the focus on patient diversity. I imagine patient recruiting is a focus right now. I was just looking at your company, had a few ideas on how XYZ Company is recruiting a more diverse patient group. I sent you an email with a few ideas — subject line: 'patient diversity.' Again, subject 'patient diversity.' It's Jason."

### 5-word framework for AI gatekeepers (iOS screener, Google Voice)

When the gatekeeper says "who are you and why are you calling?" you have ~5 words:
> "**Jason, patient diversity.**"

When voicemail goes through, then full pitch.

### Mini voicemail (purposely vague)

> "Hey, I was calling about the FAPI study. I just sent you an email — subject line 'FAPI study.' It's Jason. Take a look at that."

## Mindset — handling call reluctance

[55:36] Bay's separation technique:

**Reframe the goal:**
- Bad: "My goal is to book a meeting" (creates pressure)
- Good: **"My goal is to start a conversation"** + "see if I can learn something insightful about this account"
- Even better: attach self-esteem to **inputs you control** (calls made, going to the gym, eating right) — not outcomes (booked meetings)

**Jon Jones radical acceptance technique** (UFC):
> "Jon Jones, moving up to heavyweight to fight guys 50 lbs heavier — when asked if he was scared, he said: 'Honestly, I've accepted that I could get knocked out or my jaw broken.' Radical acceptance of worst case. What's the worst case in a cold call? Someone hangs up and you feel awkward. That's it."

### Invisible scripts v CZ (majitelé malých firem)

Co si CZ majitel malé firmy myslí o cold-callerech / DM-erech:
- "Zase nějaký youtuber, co prodává kurz"
- "Zase někdo s ofertou na nějakou aplikaci"
- "Nemám čas, posílám to do koše"
- "Posrali mi to s reklamou minule"

→ Tohle baggage MUSÍŠ znát. Začátek hlasovky musí předjímat: **"Nejsem žádná youtuberka a nebudu ti prodávat kurz."**

### Connect rate workflow (DM hlasovky)

Bay's tactics aplikované na IG DM:
- **Mondays + Fridays** = majitelé provozoven méně přetížení, IG check více
- **8-9am v jejich timezone** = před prvním zákazníkem dne
- **Lunch break** (12-13h) = majitelé si dělají pauzu, scrollují IG
- **Večer 20-22h** = po work day, mohou si poslechnout hlasovku

→ A/B testuj tyto časy a měř reply rate (Bay's pattern: most reps avoid Monday+Friday).

### 37-second monologue insight (tvoje verze)

Bay's bod: monolog OK, pokud je o **insight**, ne o tobě.

**ŠPATNĚ (about you) — ukázka: firemní fyzioterapie:**
> "Děláme firemní fyzioterapii. Máme 4 programy. Pracujeme s certifikovanými fyzioterapeuty. Máme vlastní aplikaci na cvičení..." [37s o tobě]

**SPRÁVNĚ (about insight):**
> "Hele Jano, mám pro tebe zajímavou věc. U firem, se kterými pracujeme, jsme zjistili, že {kolik lidí v kanceláři má problém se zády — číslo z tvých dat}. A když se na to mrkneme detailněji, je to pravidelný vzorec: po {kolika měsících — z tvých dat} sezení bez pauz to začne bolet a člověk začne vypadávat na nemocenskou. Pokud se to v té době začne řešit, je {o kolik — z tvých dat} větší šance, že se to nerozjede. Pokud ne, máte z toho dlouhodobou absenci. Děje se to i u vás? Můžu ti to ukázat na vašem týmu?" [37s, ale s INSIGHT]

### Jon Jones acceptance pro tebe

**Co je worst case v tvém outreachi?**
- Nepřijde ti odpověď (bypass)
- Pošlou tě do koše ("nemáme zájem")
- Někdo se na tebe naštve a napíše negativně

→ Pokud děláš outreach pravidelně, zažila jsi tohle už mnohokrát. Bay's bod: radical acceptance = už víš, že to může nastat → není to nic dramatického → můžeš se uvolnit a soustředit na obsah, ne na strach.

### 5-word AI gatekeeper (CZ adaptace)

CZ ekvivalent screener problém: pokud voláš a zvedne recepční / zaměstnanec u dveří:
- Máš max 3-5 slov, než tě recepční přepojí nebo zavře
- **"{tvoje jméno}, kvůli úklidu kanceláří."** (5 slov)
- Nebo: **"{tvoje jméno}, kvůli fakturaci."** (3 slova)

→ Recepční slyší "neprodejní", spíš tě přepojí na majitele.

### Voicemail framework (IG DM adaptace)

Bay's voicemail = relevance + problem + CTA points to email.

**IG DM hlasovka template (60-90s):**

1. **Personal hook (5s):** "Hele Petře, viděla jsem vaši novou pobočku v Brně — gratuluju!"
2. **Relevance + insight (30s):** "Volám ti specificky proto, že většina firem, které otevírají druhou pobočku, prvních 6 měsíců bojuje s tím, aby to tam fungovalo stejně jako v té první. A mám pro tebe konkrétní data, jak na to."
3. **Social proof (15s):** "Pracujeme s {klient z tvých case studies} — {tvoje case study s čísly}."
4. **CTA → text follow-up (10s):** "Pošlu ti za chvíli krátký text s 2 minutami videa z té case study. Mrkni večer, je tam super věc, co bys mohl použít. Měj se."

Pak 5 min po hlasovce textová zpráva s linkem na case study.
