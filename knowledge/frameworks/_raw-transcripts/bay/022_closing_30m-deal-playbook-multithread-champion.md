# How two reps closed a $30M+ deal

## The premise

[00:02] Largest deal in ZoomInfo history. Rona = Senior Strategic AM. Ryan = Director Solution Architect. **4+ year customer relationship** spanning multiple reps.

Even if you sell mid-market 20K deals, the principles apply.

## Step 1 — First year = EDUCATION

[07:22] *"This might not be the standard mode where you get in and start selling-selling-selling. The first year was education. I wanted to completely immerse myself."*

Activities:
- Understand current state of all subsidiaries
- Learn how all segments work
- Learn their internal language
- Meet as many leaders as possible

> "Each deal had a purpose. Not just selling. Each deal was a proof point, a rapport and trust-building opportunity, leveraging connections + success from one segment to validate why we were the best choice."

## Step 2 — Stakeholder VALIDATION (not just mapping)

[10:15]
> "I call it stakeholder validation, not mapping. You're validating all the existing contacts."

Common situation: previous rep had relationships → now those people moved, reorganized, or just don't want a relationship with the new rep.

Process:
- Verify who's still in seat
- Verify team still exists
- Account map "with question marks" — what do we need to confirm
- Cleanup + course-correct from previous rep's positioning

> "It was a lot of validating on current state — the standard account plan, but a little bit more chaotic."

## Step 3 — Find the Named Initiative

[22:52] Most reps look for "the one business problem." Rona's twist for enterprise:

> "In an organization of this size, there is no SINGLE problem. If you're only solving one, there are seven others equally invested in by senior execs."

**The unlock = the NAMED INITIATIVE.**

Ryan: *"When you can find the named initiative across stakeholder groups, you've hit it. It's a project with a name, it's got budget. Especially when 2-3-4 VPs across different areas refer to the same project by the same name — you're golden."*

### What if you're not senior enough to know the OKR?

- Talk to ICs from the ground up (their pain points, their KPIs)
- Talk across leadership levels
- Use champions to introduce you to people you wouldn't know yourself
- Learn their lingo + internal acronyms for projects

## Step 4 — Champion vs Coach (the difference)

[26:22] **Champion:**
- In step with you, pulling you into different conversations
- Genuinely agrees with strategy + solution
- Matches your energy
- "Will go to war with you"
- Proven track record of senior leaders turning to them ("OK what's going on, how do we execute this?")
- Highly respected within their org
- Hard worker, **driving force**
- Even if no signing authority, they push as hard as if they did

**Coach:**
- Helps you with internal politics
- Plants seeds
- Gives lay of the land
- "Equally important" — but not the same role

> "You need an entire army from their end. All medic layers. Not just one person."

### Cheerleaders vs Champions
> "You'll have a lot of conversations with cheerleaders, a lot with coaches — and then the true champions are pushing just as hard."

## Step 5 — Handling "don't step on toes" gatekeeping

[30:32] When a contact says "before you reach out to anyone else, run it by me":

**Define your gatekeeper:**
- Executive assistant safeguarding for calendar sanity
- Or someone making a name for themselves in the company

Strategy: befriend, understand what's driving them. Then use **existing relationship validation** ("I'm working with Barbara at subsidiary X") + **social proof from senior leaders** they couldn't ignore.

> "Suddenly they're like 'oh shoot, this person is talking to senior X and Y. They name-dropped someone too niche for them not to actually know my company.'"

## Step 6 — Leverage internal resources EARLY

[40:00]
> "Bring leadership in from the JUMP. You should be bringing in all levels — VP, SVP, CEO — at different points of time. Not waiting for the 'connect-from-the-top' moment."

When momentum builds, you don't have to brief leaders cold — they've been engaged since the account started growing.

## Step 7 — Account 101 doc (living, breathing)

[42:12] Rona's confession: not naturally organized. Solution:
- **One singular doc** — labeled in 2023 as "Road to $10 Mil" (intention setting)
- Added everyone to it from the start
- New tabs as needed
- AI + call recording for raw data
- Single source of truth

Ryan = "master note-taker" — types every word during calls.

## Step 8 — Briefing new senior stakeholders

[44:03] When new senior person joins on customer side:
- Pre-meeting briefs aggregating context
- Send content prior ("state of the state")
- Take meetings WITHOUT the champion (to validate independent buy-in)
- **Always debrief WITH the champion** afterwards — keep them in loop

## Step 9 — In-person time matters

[47:30] Rona's hospitality background:
> "If you weren't in person, how are you even building a relationship?"

Rules:
- Every meeting > 1 hour → recommend **in-person workshop**
- Block time + go to lunch afterwards
- "You'd be surprised how quickly they say yes" — it breaks their back-to-back day

In-person uncovers new use cases through tangents. **You become more than another vendor on Zoom.**

> "The steak dinner doesn't close the deal. But prior to it was a workshop, a conference where you brought another exec to speak, a public forum of uniting both companies. That's what closes the deal."

## Step 10 — Micro-failures + dead-end recovery

[52:03] John Laney's question: contact passes you to next person, they don't respond.

> "Micro failures. They uncover a lot of value — they tell you how to pivot, why you failed, and lead you down a different path."

**Mindset:** can't take the no as end-all-be-all.

**Tactics:**
- Champion gets promoted to a new company → back to ground-floor prospecting
- Leverage prior work as proof: "we've already done all these things, they were positive, but XYZ didn't materialize here. Now I want to talk about it."
- "Failing fast" → pivot in different direction

## When to give up

[54:54]
> "If you truly believe you bring value, don't stop. But you must see PROGRESS. Not necessarily a deal — but a new contact, new division, new segment. If you're stalling for significant time → pivot."

## Pro tebe — aplikace (HIGH CZ adaptation needed)

**Pokud prodáváš single-location SMB majitelům**, většina obsahu této masterclass je o $30M enterprise dealu — irrelevant.

**Co ZLE z toho vytáhnout pro CZ context:**

### 1. "First year was education" → tvůj onboarding period

Pro klienty s více pobočkami (sítě, franšízy): **první 2-3 měsíce = education**, ne hned upsell. Musíš se ponořit do:
- Kolik mají poboček, jak různě fungují
- Jaké mají interní acronyms / názvy
- Kdo je rozhodovatel ve které pobočce
- Jak komunikují majitelé spolu

### 2. "Named initiative" pro klienty s více pobočkami

CZ ekvivalent: kdy majitel sítě poboček pojmenuje něco, co řeší napříč pobočkami:
- "Standard 2026" (= project name)
- "Brno launch" (= named expansion)
- "Léto attack" (= named campaign)

→ Pokud zjistíš, že 2-3 majitelé sítí mluví o "letní akci" jako koncepci → **THAT je named initiative** = tvoje nabídka tam musí být součástí (ne side feature).

### 3. Stakeholder validation (více poboček)

Pro firmu s více pobočkami (např. síť salonů) typical stakeholders:
- Majitel/CEO
- Provozní manažer (pokud existuje)
- Recepční / asistentka
- Hlavní specialista / brand face
- Účetní

MUSÍŠ ověřit, kdo má **influence**, ne jen kdo má title. Často **recepční** je real gatekeeper.

### 4. Champion vs Coach (CZ aplikace pro tvoje klienty)

Mezi tvými klienty najdeš tyhle 2 typy:

**Champion (např. majitel, který tě sám doporučuje):**
- Posílá ti data sám iniciativně
- Doporučuje tě dalším firmám
- Bere telefon, reaguje
- "Tahá za druhý konec šňůry"

**Coach (např. recepční / asistentka):**
- Pomáhá s detaily (jaké jsou dnes volné termíny)
- Říká insider info ("šéf zítra letí na dovolenou")
- Není rozhodovatel, ale zná dynamiku

→ Potřebuješ **OBOJÍ** ve velkém klientovi.

### 5. In-person pro CZ SMB (KRITICKÉ)

Pokud děláš většinu sales online (Google Meet / Zoom), Bay-Rona's lekce: **steak dinner closes deals.**

Aplikace pro CZ kontext:
- Vyšší-tier deal (více poboček, nejvyšší balíček z tvého ceníku) → **osobní návštěva provozovny** + káva místo Zoom callu
- Oborová konference tvých klientů → musíš tam být
- Klient s nejvyšší roční hodnotou → setkání s majitelem na něčí akci nebo v restauraci

Pokud tohle zatím neděláš, je to **growth opportunity** pro premium klienty.

### 6. Micro-failures jako positive (mindset)

Když máš v tabulce víc negative resolved než positive, Bay-Rona's framing:
- Každý "ne" je signal, kdy odpadl, proč, co to říká o ICP
- Pattern detection: jsou starší majitelé (50+) častěji negative? = signal pro narrowing
- Pivot na základě dat, ne emocí

### 7. Living "Road to X klientů" doc

Máš tabulku leadů. Bay's bod: vyhrne se to o **named intention setting** — pojmenovat ji "Road to {tvůj cíl} klientů" místo "Leady". Banal? Ano. Funkční? Také ano.

### 8. Jiná linie (jednotlivci vs firmy s týmem)

Pro jednotlivce (např. samostatné lektorky, fyzioterapeutky) = single-decision-maker SMB → většina enterprise lekcí z #022 NEPLATÍ. Pro **firmu s týmem (5+ lidí, např. síť studií)** ale možná — oslovovala bys ji s "named initiative" frame ("sjednocení všech poboček pod jeden standard").
