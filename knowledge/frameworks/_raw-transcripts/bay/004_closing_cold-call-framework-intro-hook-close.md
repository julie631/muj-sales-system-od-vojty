# Cold Calling — Give me 23 minutes (Mini Course)

## The data — top reps vs average (Gong 300M calls)

[00:00] **Cold calling doesn't work if you suck at it.** But the data:

| Metric | Average rep | Top 25% reps |
|---|---|---|
| Connect rate | 5.4% | 13.3% |
| Set rate (live conv → meeting) | 4.6% | 16.7% |
| Show rate | ~50%+ | 80%+ |

The average call = 20 calls per pickup × 20 live conversations per booked meeting × 50% show rate.

## Most common problem — first 30-60 seconds

[01:36] **The average cold call lasts ~80 seconds (Chorus data).** Not long enough to book a meeting.

Two root causes:
1. Reps showing up **not confident** → prospect doesn't want to talk
2. Reps don't feel the activity is worth it (skill gap + missed pipeline)

**The fix:** sell yourself, not your product. Sell yourself by showing you understand their world, talked to their peers, worked with their peers, understand their problems.

## The framework — Intro → Hook → Close

### Intro
- Opt-in (they didn't ask you to call)
- Reverse pitch instead of product pitch (peer problems → "does this resonate?")

### Hook
- Money questions to engage on a common problem (the reason they'd want a meeting)

### Close
- Summarize what you heard
- Secure time
- **Triple Confirmation** (80%+ show rate)

## Key Point 1 — First 60 Seconds

### Call reluctance management

**Physical:**
- **Box breathing** (Navy SEALs): 4 sec in, hold 4 sec, 4 sec out
- Take a deep breath while the phone is ringing (Bay's been short of breath starting many cold calls)
- **Stand up and pace** — energy is much better standing than hunched over
- Take a quick lap outside before sitting down

**Mindset — manage self-talk:**
- Instead of "they already have a solution" / "they look unfriendly" → **be curious**
- "I wonder what solution they're using"
- "I wonder if they're having trouble managing 3 HR solutions like my customer Susie"

**Reframe the goal:**
- Bad: "My goal is to book a meeting" → makes you desperate
- Good: "My goal is to see if they want to have a conversation"
- "They may not want to talk to me, and that's okay. Has nothing to do with me."

### Tonality

- **Remove up-talk, customer service voice, corporate persona.** "Hey, how's it going?" — that's not you.
- If your friends/family were listening, they'd ask "who is this person?"
- **Smile when you talk** — it comes through

### Pacing

- **Dial it down 25%. Talk at 0.75×.**
- Example: *"Hey, it's Jason with Outbound Squad. I know I probably caught you in the middle of something. Do you have a minute for me to share the reason for my call, and you can let me know if you want to hang up or not?"*
- When they pick up, they're doing something else → if you talk fast they can't understand → repeat yourself → more objections

## Permission-Based Openers (PBO) — 5 variants

### Avoid

- **"Did I catch you at a bad time?"** Popularized by Aaron Ross a decade ago. Now overused → most say "yes, bad time."
- **"How's it going?"** — Don't waste real estate. You don't know them. Asking how their day is going is insincere.

### 1. Classic PBO (new business, generic)

> "Hey Dave, it's Jason. I know you weren't expecting my call. Do you mind if I take a quick moment to share why I called you specifically?"

### 2. Front-Load Relevance (executives)

Put the reason for your call at the front. Optionally before introducing yourself.

> "Hey Tom, I was calling about the recent launch of ABC product. It's Jason with Outbound Squad. Do you got a minute? I promise to be brief."

### 3. "Heard the name tossed around" (Armand Farrokh / 30MPC)

Best for SLED, local, contractor-style sales:

> "Hey Jason, we work with a few other house painters here in Seattle like ABC Painters. It's Dave with XYZ Construction Pros. Have you heard our name tossed around?"

- Weave in: location + social proof + other example companies

### 4. J-Base Opener (front-load relevance + social proof + problem statement)

Execs need to hear all 3 quickly, otherwise they hang up:

> "Hey Katie, I was reaching out about the AEs you're hiring and the self-sourcing they're being asked to do. It's Jason over at Outbound Squad. Sales teams at Shopify, Rippling, and Gong use us to help their AEs self-source pipeline so they're not so reliant on marketing and SDRs. Do you got a minute to hear why I'm calling you specifically?"

### 5. Existing customer (account manager / AE cross-sell)

> "Hey Claire, it's Jason over at XYZ Company. I'm actually working with Ryan right now over in the Seesaw team on reducing downtime. Do you got a quick minute to connect?"

→ Name-drop someone else on their team + department + problem you're working on.

### 6. Cross-sell into base

> "Hey Bindu, it's Jason with XYZ Company. I'm your conversation optimization specialist and I work with Ryan on the checkout experience. We haven't connected yet with the web team and I wanted to introduce myself. Do you got a quick minute?"

→ Work in roles, people on their teams, social proof, any relevance.

**Pick 2-3 openers that fit your personality and practice them.**

## Reverse Pitch (replaces elevator pitch)

After PBO, resist every urge to product-pitch. **Problem-based language converts 3× more meetings than buzzwords.**

This is your **problem hypothesis** — best educated guess on what they're struggling with.

### Generic version

> "Hey, I'm calling because when I speak with CFOs and finance leaders in retail, usually we see these challenges. One is around cost control and margin protection — they want to avoid tool sprawl and unnecessary headcount to maintain margins. Second is predictable revenue growth — they want to grow MRR but don't want to add complexity. I'm curious, how does that compare to your day-to-day?"

→ Take **priority statements from your messaging matrix** = your reverse pitch.

### Tailored version (with trigger)

> "Hey, I'm calling you because you just launched XYZ product, and Fortnet — who we worked with — was in a similar position running into these two challenges. One was cost control as they grew new product lines, like what you guys are doing with ABC product. Second was predictable revenue growth — they wanted to grow MRR without adding complexity, also reduce churn."

→ Weave in: relevance + trigger + slightly tailored problems.

## Key Point 2 — The Hook (Money Questions)

[17:51] **Avoid:** "What's your biggest challenge?" Requires prospect to admit weakness to a stranger.

**Do:** **Displace the problem** — frame as a peers' problem, then ask if it resonates.

> "Hey, one of the big challenges we hear around reducing cost to serve is you put all this work in getting customers to solve their own problems, but they end up picking up the phone anyways. How does that resonate with what you're seeing at ABC company?"

### 4 question frameworks

**1. Money Question** — "If I was [role on your team], how would I figure out [thing we know they can't do]?"
> "Hey, if I was your finance ops manager, how would I figure out which parts of your revenue stack cost the most to maintain?"

**2. Spotlight Question** — "How do you do [something I know that you can't do]?"
> "How do you balance speed and accuracy if your finance systems don't talk to each other?"

**3. Pervasive Problem** — share what's common across all their peers, ask if it resonates.
> "We're seeing it's really hard to find great welding talent right now. Second, automation is starting to take place in manufacturing plants, but there are a lot of hard customized welding parts that are hard to do at scale. How does that compare to your experience?"

**4. Tell Me a Story** — get them to open up with a story.
> "I'm really curious — the last time you needed to give leadership an updated revenue projection, how quickly was your team able to turn that around?"

**Come up with 2-3 money questions aligned to problems in your messaging matrix.**

## Key Point 3 — Close & Secure (Summarize → Story → Assumptive Close → Triple Confirmation)

[20:00] If they're still talking → meeting is yours to lose.

### Summarize + Customer Story + Assumptive Close

> "Hey Tom, just to recap — sounds like welding talent has been tough to find, and there are some parts you'd love to automate. Did I miss anything? Cool. This is exactly what we're helping other manufacturers like XYZ tackle. Instead of digging in mid-day when I'm cold calling you, how about we impact this when I'm not cold calling you out of the blue? **Do you have your calendar handy?** The best next step is a quick call with my AE Tom — he can show you how other teams are tackling this. How's tomorrow look at 2pm?"

### Triple Confirmation (critical for show-rate)

**Don't lose them at the finish line.**

1. **Send the invite LIVE on the call.** "Hey, while I have you, I just sent the calendar invite. Can you go ahead and accept that in your email? Sometimes that doesn't go through or gets sent to spam, and I want to make sure it hit your calendar."

2. **Restate the agenda.** "Like I said, there's some hard-to-automate welding parts you wanted to dig into. Tom's going to talk to you about how we're helping clients with that and also how they're approaching the welder shortage right now."

3. **Day-before group text.** "Since you guys are not meeting until next Tuesday, I'll reach out the day before to confirm. In the meantime, I'm going to open up a group text between you and Tom so you can reach out to Tom directly if anything comes up."

### CZ PBO variants (4 verze, ukázka: jazyková škola → firmy)

**1. Classic PBO (CZ):**
> "Hele Petře, tady {tvoje jméno}. Vím, žes mě nečekal. Můžu ti vzít 30 vteřin a říct, proč ti volám specificky?"

**2. Front-Load Relevance (CZ — pokud máš trigger):**
> "Petře, volám ti kvůli tomu, že jste teď otevřeli pobočku v Německu — viděla jsem to na LinkedInu. Tady {tvoje jméno} z {tvoje firma}. Máš na mě minutu?"

**3. "Heard the name" (CZ — pokud máš local social proof):**
> "Petře, pracujeme s dalšími firmami v Brně — třeba s {klient z tvých case studies}. Tady {tvoje jméno} z {tvoje firma}. Slyšels o nás?"

**4. J-Base (CZ — exec ready):**
> "Petře, volám ti kvůli obchodníkům, kteří mají angličtinu na papíře, ale na callu se zahraničním klientem ztuhnou. Tady {tvoje jméno} z {tvoje firma}. Pracujeme s firmami jako {klient z tvých case studies} — {tvoje case study s čísly}. Máš minutu, abych ti řekla, proč zrovna tobě?"

### CZ Reverse Pitch

**Generic (ukázka):**
> "Hele, volám ti, protože když mluvím s majiteli firem, jako jsi ty, slyším pořád dvě stejné věci: jedna — platí lidem jazykové kurzy roky, a když přijde zahraniční klient, stejně volají toho jednoho kolegu, co umí anglicky. Dvě — mají rozpočet na vzdělávání, ale nemají jak změřit, jestli se lidi reálně zlepšují. Jak to máš ty?"

### CZ Money Questions

**1. Money Question (CZ):**
> "Hele, kdybych byla tvoje asistentka, jak bych zjistila, kdo z obchodníků zvládne zítra call se zahraničním klientem sám?"

**2. Spotlight Question (CZ):**
> "Jak víš, kdo z lidí se na kurzech reálně posouvá, vs kdo tam jen sedí?"

**3. Pervasive Problem (CZ):**
> "Vidím, že hodně firem má angličtinu v benefitech, ale na hodiny chodí pár nadšenců. A pak, když přijde poptávka ze zahraničí, se nedá najít nikdo, kdo by ji vzal. Děje se to i vám?"

**4. Tell Me a Story (CZ):**
> "Kdy jste naposled přišli o zakázku nebo klienta, protože se nikdo pořádně nedomluvil? Jak to dopadlo?"

### CZ Triple Confirmation (eliminate no-show)

Když děláš sales calls přes Google Meet / Zoom — adaptace:

**Step 1 (na callu):**
> "Hele, pošlu ti teď pozvánku do mailu. Můžeš ji potvrdit, ať vidím, že ti to padlo? Někdy to padá do spamu."

**Step 2 (restate agenda):**
> "Jak jsme se domluvili — pošlu ti tři varianty spolupráce s cenami, a v úterý ve 14h spolu projdeme {tvoje case study s čísly}, abys viděl, jak to vypadá naživo. Souhlas?"

**Step 3 (day-before WhatsApp):**
> "Hele Petře, jen krátká zpráva — zítra ve 14h spolu meeting. Mám ti něco připravit konkrétně? Pošli mi prosím seznam lidí, kterých se to týká, ať ho otevřeme rovnou."

### CZ Call reluctance

- Box breathing před hlasovkou nebo callem (4-4-4)
- **Stůj, choď po pokoji** během callů
- **Re-frame goal:** "Cílem mého callu není zavřít — cílem je dozvědět se, co Petr reálně potřebuje. Pokud moje služba není fit, OK."

### Jiný obor — same framework

**J-Base (ukázka: úklidová firma → kanceláře):**
> "Jano, volám ti kvůli úklidu vašich nových kanceláří — viděla jsem, že jste se stěhovali. Tady {tvoje jméno}. Starám se o úklid kanceláří menších firem v Praze. Pomáhám jim, aby kancelář každé ráno vypadala jako u velké firmy, ne jako po víkendu. Máš minutu?"
